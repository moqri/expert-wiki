Key work

Genetic and proteomic determinants of Alzheimer's disease risk, progression and fluid biomarkers.

  • Endophenotype genetics. Used cerebrospinal fluid tau levels as quantitative traits to identify Alzheimer's risk variants (Neuron 2013) and identified rare PLD3 coding variants that confer disease risk (Nature 2014).
  • TREM2 and microglia. Contributed to the identification of TREM2 risk variants (NEJM 2013) and showed that soluble TREM2 in cerebrospinal fluid is elevated in Alzheimer's disease (Acta Neuropathologica 2016).
  • Proteogenomic atlases. Mapped genetic regulation of proteins in brain, cerebrospinal fluid and plasma (Nature Neuroscience 2021) and prioritized putative causal, partly druggable proteins for Alzheimer's disease from a CSF protein QTL atlas (Nature Genetics 2024).
  • Disease course. Characterized cerebrospinal fluid proteomic changes across the natural history of autosomal dominant Alzheimer's disease (Nature Medicine 2023), and reported that Ms4a4a deficiency reduces plaque pathology in mice (Alzheimer's & Dementia 2025).

  1. 1
    38proteins in spinal fluid nominated as likely causes of Alzheimer's, 15 with existing drugs

    Measuring 6,361 proteins in spinal fluid from 3,506 people and linking them to genetics found 3,885 genetic controls of protein levels, most of them new. Combining this map with Alzheimer's genetics pointed to 38 proteins that are likely causal, 15 of which already have drugs, and a protein-based model predicted Alzheimer's better than genetics alone.

    Western et al. 2024 · Nat GenetSpinal fluid protein genetics study, 3,506 samples, with Mendelian randomization
  2. 2
    2xAlzheimer's risk for carriers of a rare PLD3 variant

    Sequencing families with many cases of late-onset Alzheimer's found a rare variant in the PLD3 gene (Val232Met) that tracked with disease in two families and doubled Alzheimer's risk across seven case-control groups totalling more than 11,000 people. PLD3 is lower in neurons from Alzheimer's brains and affects how amyloid precursor protein is handled.

    Cruchaga et al. 2014 · NatureWhole-exome sequencing of 14 families plus case-control follow-up in over 11,000 people
  3. 3
    ProtectiveMS4A variants that raise soluble TREM2 lower Alzheimer's risk

    A genome-wide study of soluble TREM2 in spinal fluid found that common variants in the MS4A gene region set its level. The variants that raise soluble TREM2 were linked to lower Alzheimer's risk and later onset, and lab work showed that the MS4A4A protein controls how much soluble TREM2 is made.

    Deming et al. 2019 · Sci Transl MedGenome-wide association study of spinal fluid sTREM2 with replication and cell experiments
  4. 4
    Fewer plaquesin mice lacking Ms4a4a

    Removing Ms4a4a in a mouse model of amyloid build-up reduced overall plaque burden and made plaques more compact, with microglia that were more inflammatory and made more MMP-9. People carrying the MS4A4A resilience variant also had higher MMP-9 in spinal fluid, supporting lower MS4A4A as a possible treatment approach.

    Danhash et al. 2025 · Alzheimers DementKnockout mouse study (5xFAD) plus human spinal fluid measures
  5. 5
    8spinal fluid proteins that change before standard Alzheimer's markers in inherited Alzheimer's

    In 286 carriers of inherited Alzheimer's mutations and 177 non-carrier relatives, 137 spinal fluid proteins followed different paths over the disease course, and eight changed before the usual amyloid and tau markers. Early changes involved stress response and mitochondrial damage, middle-stage changes neuronal death, and late presymptomatic changes microglia.

    Shen et al. 2024 · CellSpinal fluid proteomics in 463 members of families with inherited Alzheimer's
  6. 6
    AUC 0.945blood circular RNA test for biomarker-confirmed Alzheimer's

    A set of 34 circular RNAs measured in blood classified biomarker-confirmed Alzheimer's better than plasma p-tau217 (AUC 0.945 vs 0.877), and the two together reached 0.977. The test replicated in two independent groups, was specific to Alzheimer's over Parkinson's and frontotemporal dementia, and predicted progression to symptoms.

    Phillips et al. 2026 · Nat MedBlood RNA study in 1,221 people with replication in 551 and 1,767 people
  7. 7
    27%of the variation in cognitive impairment explained by a synapse protein ratio beyond amyloid and tau

    Across 3,397 people from six cohorts, synapse proteins in spinal fluid were the strongest correlates of cognitive impairment, independent of amyloid and tau. The YWHAG to NPTX2 ratio predicted who would move from normal cognition to mild impairment, and from mild impairment to dementia, over 15 years, and rose about 20 years before expected onset in inherited Alzheimer's.

    Oh et al. 2025 · Nat MedSpinal fluid proteomics in 3,397 people from six cohorts, up to 15 years of follow-up
  8. 8
    Mixeddirections of blood marker change on lecanemab

    In 197 patients receiving lecanemab in routine care, 34 of 130 plasma proteins changed. Amyloid and tau markers such as p-tau217 moved toward normal, while markers such as TREM2 and NPTXR moved further from normal and NfL did not change, suggesting different blood markers may serve different purposes in monitoring treatment.

    Mu et al. 2026 · Lancet NeurolLongitudinal cohort of 197 treated patients compared with 1,312 untreated and 458 amyloid-negative people
  9. 9
    7 proteinsblood panel that identifies biomarker-defined Alzheimer's (AUC above 0.88)

    Measuring about 6,100 plasma proteins in more than 3,300 people found 416 linked to Alzheimer's, 294 of them new, and validated them in over 7,000 more samples. A seven-protein machine-learning panel identified biomarker-defined Alzheimer's with AUC above 0.88 and replicated across cohorts and platforms.

    Heo et al. 2025 · Nat AgingPlasma proteomics in 3,300+ people with external validation in 7,000+ samples
  10. 10
    HR 11risk of progression in the highest-risk group when p-tau217 is combined with the protein panel

    In people with normal cognition, plasma p-tau217 alone and the seven-protein panel each predicted progression to symptomatic Alzheimer's similarly. Combining them identified a high-risk group with about 11 times the hazard of progression, and simple protein ratios such as ACHE to NPTXR also improved on p-tau217 alone.

    Liu et al. 2026 · Alzheimers DementLongitudinal Knight ADRC cohort with replication in ADNI
  11. 11
    <30%of genetic controls of protein levels are shared by plasma and spinal fluid

    Of almost 5,000 genetic controls of protein levels, fewer than 30% appeared in both plasma and spinal fluid. Of 427 links between proteins and 14 neurological traits, only 69 showed up in both fluids, and spinal fluid captured disease biology that plasma missed.

    Western et al. 2026 · Sci Transl MedComparison of plasma and spinal fluid protein genetics across 14 neurological traits
  12. 12
    2,029spinal fluid proteins altered across the Alzheimer's continuum, 57% newly reported

    A three-stage spinal fluid study found 2,029 proteins altered in Alzheimer's, grouped by stage, from neuronal death and tau phosphorylation early, to microglia and lysosome problems in the middle, to microglia-neuron crosstalk late. Models built from these proteins predicted biomarker status and clinical diagnosis with AUC above 0.90.

    Ali et al. 2025 · NeuronMulti-cohort spinal fluid proteomics with discovery, replication and meta-analysis
  13. 13
    10,527plasma samples compared across Alzheimer's, Parkinson's and frontotemporal dementia

    Plasma proteomics in 10,527 samples found thousands of proteins linked to each disease. Immune, glycolysis and extracellular matrix pathways were shared, while Alzheimer's showed disease-specific apoptosis changes, and Alzheimer's and Parkinson's overlapped least.

    Ali et al. 2025 · Nat MedGlobal Neurodegeneration Proteomics Consortium plasma study, 10,527 samples
  14. 14
    1,807spinal fluid proteins linked to amyloid in people with normal cognition

    Profiling 6,175 spinal fluid proteins in 994 cognitively normal people aged 43 to 91 found and replicated 2,172 age-linked, 711 sex-linked, 193 APOE4-linked and 1,807 amyloid-linked proteins, with heavy overlap between them. These signatures differed from those seen in plasma.

    Seo et al. 2025 · Nat AgingSpinal fluid proteomics in 994 cognitively normal adults
  15. 15
    Higher in womenAPOE4's link to tau in spinal fluid

    Across 4,592 people from 20 cohorts, APOE4 dose was tied to lower amyloid-beta 42 in spinal fluid, a sign of more amyloid in the brain. Women had higher p-tau and total tau, and APOE4's association with tau measures was stronger in women than in men in some ancestry groups.

    Xu et al. 2025 · JAMA Netw OpenCohort study, 4,592 people of African, Asian and European ancestry
  16. 16
    OR 10.7Alzheimer's risk for APOE4 carriers who also carry PSEN1 E318G

    People who carry APOE4 and the PSEN1 E318G variant had Alzheimer's risk (odds ratio 10.7) similar to APOE4 homozygotes (9.9), about double that of APOE4 carriers without the variant (3.9). The variant was also linked to higher spinal fluid tau.

    Benitez et al. 2013 · PLoS GenetDeep sequencing plus case-control series of 5,161 people
  17. 17
    33 and 11cell type-gene pairs in APOE4 non-carriers and carriers

    A genome-wide study of Alzheimer's split by APOE4 status (447,669 people) and linked to brain cell-type data identified 33 cell type-gene pairs in non-carriers and 11 in carriers, with oligodendrocytes most prominent in carriers. Several genes are druggable, pointing to treatments that could differ by APOE4 status.

    Zeng et al. 2026 · Alzheimers DementAPOE4-stratified genome-wide association study, 447,669 people
  18. 18
    Highersoluble TREM2 in spinal fluid in Alzheimer's

    People with Alzheimer's had higher soluble TREM2 in spinal fluid than controls, and levels tracked tau but not amyloid. Carriers of variants that cause Nasu-Hakola disease had lower levels, while R47H risk-variant carriers had higher levels, suggesting R47H raises risk by a different mechanism.

    Piccio et al. 2016 · Acta NeuropatholSpinal fluid study of 180 cases and controls plus 40 TREM2 variant carriers
  19. 19
    OR 1.27Alzheimer's risk for TREM2 L211P in African Americans

    In 906 African American people with Alzheimer's and 2,487 controls, TREM2 L211P raised risk, while the TREM2 variants that raise risk in people of European ancestry were extremely rare and not linked to risk. Risk variants in the same gene differed by ancestry.

    Jin et al. 2015 · Mol NeurodegenerExonic sequencing and case-control study, 3,393 African American participants
  20. 20
    4 locigenetic controls of soluble TREM2, including TGFBR2 and NECTIN2

    The largest genome-wide study of spinal fluid soluble TREM2 (3,350 people) found four regions, including two MS4A missense variants that interact to affect both TREM2 and Alzheimer's risk. Lab experiments showed that changing TGFBR2 or NECTIN2 changes soluble TREM2 levels.

    Wang et al. 2024 · Mol NeurodegenerGenome-wide association study of 3,350 people with fine mapping and cell assays
  21. 21
    8 newgenetic regions controlling spinal fluid amyloid and tau

    A genome-wide meta-analysis of spinal fluid amyloid-beta 42, total tau and p-tau181 in 18,948 people found 12 genetic regions, eight of them new, including BIN1 and MS4A6A. These variants were also linked to Alzheimer's risk, progression or brain amyloid, and implicate lipid handling and autophagy.

    Timsina et al. 2026 · Nat CommunGenome-wide association meta-analysis, 18,948 people
  22. 22
    13,409amyloid PET scans in the largest genetic study of brain amyloid

    A multi-ethnic genetic study of amyloid PET in 13,409 people confirmed a very strong APOE4 signal, weakest in Asian participants, and found ABCA7, CR1 and FERMT2 as additional regions that also affect Alzheimer's risk, plus two signals specific to women.

    Ali et al. 2023 · Acta Neuropathol CommunMulti-ethnic genome-wide association study of amyloid PET, 13,409 people
  23. 23
    OR 2.27per-score effect of late-onset risk genes in sporadic early-onset Alzheimer's

    A polygenic risk score built from 21 late-onset Alzheimer's risk regions was most strongly tied to sporadic early-onset Alzheimer's (odds ratio 2.27), then familial late-onset (1.75) and sporadic late-onset (1.40). In inherited autosomal dominant Alzheimer's the score did not change risk but may modify age at onset.

    Cruchaga et al. 2018 · Alzheimers DementPolygenic risk score analysis across sporadic, familial and autosomal dominant Alzheimer's
  24. 24
    3 locifor spinal fluid tau, one also raising Alzheimer's risk

    A genome-wide study of spinal fluid tau and p-tau in 1,269 people found three regions, including one near the TREM genes. The 3q28 variant also predicted Alzheimer's risk, tangle pathology and faster cognitive decline in independent groups, showing that biomarker levels can reveal new risk genes.

    Cruchaga et al. 2013 · NeuronGenome-wide association study of spinal fluid tau, 1,269 people
  25. 25
    Slower declinewith a CHI3L1 variant that lowers spinal fluid YKL-40

    A human CHI3L1 variant that lowers spinal fluid YKL-40 was linked to slower Alzheimer's progression. In mice, removing Chi3l1 reduced amyloid plaques and increased clearance of amyloid by glial cells, and the gene is regulated by the body clock.

    Lananna et al. 2020 · Sci Transl MedHuman genetic association plus mouse and cell experiments
