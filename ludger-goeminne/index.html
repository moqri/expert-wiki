Ask about Ludger J. E. Goeminne's research

Answers are drawn only from Ludger J. E. Goeminne's published papers listed on this page, with each point linked to its paper. They describe what the papers report, not personal views or advice from Ludger J. E. Goeminne. This is not medical advice.

Try

Plasma proteomic models of organ-specific aging, chemical reprogramming, and statistical methods for quantitative proteomics.

  • Organ-specific proteomic aging. Led development of plasma protein models of organ-specific aging in over 50,000 people, showing that accelerated organ aging precedes disease in the corresponding organ and is modified by diet, lifestyle and medication.
  • Chemical reprogramming. Showed that partial chemical reprogramming lowered molecular age markers and upregulated oxidative phosphorylation in aged mouse fibroblasts, whereas in vivo administration at higher doses caused toxic lipid accumulation.
  • Systemic regulation of ovarian aging. Using heterochronic transplantation and parabiosis in mice, showed that ovarian molecular age shifts with host age in both directions (bioRxiv preprint).
  • Metabolism and proteomics methods. Showed that inhibiting ceramide synthesis restored mitochondrial and protein homeostasis in aging muscle (Science Translational Medicine 2023), and developed the MSqRob method for label-free quantitative proteomics.

  1. 1
    50,000+people whose blood proteins were used to build organ-specific aging clocks

    Blood protein levels can estimate how fast each organ system is aging. Faster aging of an organ predicts disease in that organ, men age faster than women in most organs, and diet, lifestyle, job and medicines change organ aging rates.

    Goeminne et al. 2025 · Cell MetabPlasma proteome models in over 50,000 UK Biobank and other cohort participants
  2. 2
    Diseasesof aging are accelerated aging of specific organs

    Age-related chronic diseases look like speeded-up aging of the affected organ or body system. This supports both whole-body and personalized organ-specific anti-aging treatments.

    Goeminne et al. 2025 · Cell MetabOrgan-specific aging and mortality models trained on plasma proteomics in over 50,000 people
  3. 3
    R = 0.12correlation between how well a clock predicts age and how well it predicts death

    A clock that predicts calendar age well is not necessarily good at predicting death. Across 39 aging biomarkers, the Horvath skin and blood clock was best at predicting age (R² = 0.88), while GrimAge2 best predicted death (hazard ratio 2.57) and healthspan (hazard ratio 2.00).

    Ying et al. 2025 · Nat AgingBiolearn open-source framework testing 39 biomarkers in over 20,000 people from diverse cohorts
  4. 4
    11-22%fewer loss-of-function mutations in centenarians

    Centenarians carry 11 to 22% fewer rare gene-breaking mutations than controls, and their children show a similar pattern. RGP1, PCNX2 and ANO9 emerged as longevity genes with consistent effects on aging-related traits.

    Ying et al. 2024 · Nat CommunWhole-exome sequencing of Ashkenazi Jewish centenarians, offspring and controls, with UK Biobank validation
  5. 5
    6 of 6protein clocks showed lower biological age with treatment

    In a 12-week phase 2a trial of the anti-fibrotic drug rentosertib for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, all six protein-based aging clocks predicted lower biological age in treated patients. Clocks alone could not separate anti-aging from anti-disease effects, so pathway analyses were used alongside them.

    Zhavoronkov et al. 2026 · Nat BiotechnolSix proteomic clocks applied to serum proteomes from a randomized phase 2a trial
  6. 6
    Youngerbiological age of old mouse cells after chemical reprogramming

    A chemical cocktail lowered the biological age of fibroblasts from young and old mice by both gene expression and DNA methylation clocks. The strongest change was a boost in mitochondrial energy production, along with fewer aging-related metabolites.

    Mitchell et al. 2024 · ElifeEpigenome, transcriptome, proteome, phosphoproteome and metabolome profiling of mouse fibroblasts
  7. 7
    Toxicfat build-up when the reprogramming cocktail was given to mice

    In living middle-aged mice, low doses of the chemical reprogramming cocktail were safe but did not lower the biological age of kidney or liver. Higher doses caused severe weight loss and lipid droplet build-up in liver and kidney, a warning for this approach.

    Mitchell et al. 2026 · Aging CellIn vivo dosing in genetically diverse UM-HET3 male mice plus cell studies
  8. 8
    Both waysovarian aging shifts with the age of the host

    Old mouse ovaries became molecularly younger in young hosts and young ovaries became older in old hosts. Ovaries from 13-month-old donors supported live births at 17 to 18 months in young hosts but not in old ones, and young ovaries lowered the molecular age of aged liver and brain.

    Iqbal et al. 2026 · bioRxivPreprint; heterochronic ovary transplantation and parabiosis in mice with new ovarian clock (OvaAge)
  9. 9
    Immune genestop the list of aging genes seen in both DNA methylation and gene activity

    Genes whose methylation and expression both change with age in blood are enriched for adaptive immune functions. They replicate better across populations and track aging outcomes more strongly than genes found by either data type alone.

    Moqri et al. 2026 · Nat CommunIntegrative multi-omic analysis of human blood across diverse cohorts
  10. 10
    6key barriers to using aging biomarkers in the clinic

    A survey of experts found six main barriers to bringing biomarkers of aging into clinical use. Recommendations include tying them to clinical decisions, making them affordable and widely available, and validating them in individuals.

    Biomarkers of Aging Consortium et al. 2024 · Nat AgingExpert survey and consensus recommendations
  11. 11
    Ceramidesbuild up in aging muscle in worms, mice and humans

    Ceramide fats accumulate in skeletal muscle with age. Blocking their production with the drug myriocin restored mitochondrial and protein health, extended lifespan in worms, and improved muscle health and fitness in old mice.

    Lima et al. 2023 · Sci Transl MedLipidomics and interventions in C. elegans, mice and human muscle cells, plus human muscle transcriptomes
  12. 12
    CERS1decline with age weakens muscle

    The enzyme CERS1 falls with age in human muscle. Blocking it in old mice worsened muscle mass and function, and human gene variants that lower CERS1 were linked to weaker grip strength and poorer health in the UK Biobank.

    Wohlwend et al. 2024 · ElifeHuman muscle data, aged mice, C. elegans, and Mendelian randomization in the UK Biobank
  13. 13
    GDF15protects against age-related inflammation

    Blood GDF15, a signal released by stressed mitochondria, rises in older people. Mice lacking GDF15 had more tissue inflammation, liver injury and liver fat at old age, and genetically lower GDF15 in humans was linked to higher body weight and inflammation.

    Moon et al. 2020 · Aging CellElderly human subjects, BXD mouse population, Gdf15 knockout mice and Mendelian randomization
  14. 14
    CBP/p300links mitochondrial stress to longer life

    The worm version of CBP/p300 is needed for the mitochondrial stress response, lower amyloid-beta aggregation and lifespan extension. In mice and people, higher CBP/p300 levels go with this stress response and with longevity.

    Li et al. 2021 · Nat AgingC. elegans genetics with mouse and human population data and mammalian cell experiments
  15. 15
    Metal clockcopper isotope makeup of the liver changes with age

    The metal content of mouse organs changes with age in a consistent pattern, and the copper isotope ratio in liver tracks age. Iron levels and copper isotopes also relate to body fat and running capacity.

    Morel et al. 2022 · Nat CommunMetallome and isotope profiling of five organs across ages in mice, with multi-omics
  16. 16
    Fitnessgene variant in COX7A2L raises respiration and fitness

    A common 10-letter DNA insertion in the COX7A2L gene raises its level in muscle, increasing mitochondrial supercomplexes and respiration. Higher COX7A2L goes with lower body fat and better cardiorespiratory fitness in people, and restoring it in mice raised maximal oxygen use.

    Benegiamo et al. 2022 · Nat MetabHuman eQTL data, human muscle cells and mouse genetic models
  17. 17
    Myriocinbeat glucocorticoids in a muscular dystrophy model

    The sphingolipid production pathway is switched up in muscle of people with Duchenne muscular dystrophy. Blocking it with myriocin in mdx mice improved muscle function and reduced inflammation and fibrosis more than glucocorticoids did.

    Laurila et al. 2022 · Sci AdvHuman patient muscle transcriptomes and mdx mouse treatment study
  18. 18
    1 of 7mouse strains progressed to fatty liver disease with fibrosis

    Of seven mouse strains fed a liver-disease diet, only PWK/PhJ developed NASH with extensive fibrosis, while CAST/EiJ was fully resistant. Low liver mitochondrial function marked the susceptible strain, whose gene profile best matched human NASH.

    Benegiamo et al. 2023 · J Exp MedComparison of seven genetically diverse mouse strains
  19. 19
    9 strainsresponded very differently to a high-fat diet

    Like people, genetically different mice and the two sexes respond to a high-fat diet in very different ways, including immune, translation and mitochondrial changes. This argues for diverse mouse populations in metabolic research.

    Bachmann et al. 2022 · iScienceNine genetically diverse mouse strains of both sexes
  20. 20
    Ces1ccontrols blood levels of the protective bile acid TUDCA

    Both genetics and diet strongly shape bile acids in mice. Mapping hundreds of genetic loci identified the gene Ces1c as a master regulator of blood TUDCA, a bile acid with disease-preventing actions.

    Li et al. 2022 · Cell MetabBile acid profiling in 360 BXD mice on chow or high-fat diet, confirmed in knockout mice
  21. 21
    MSqRobmore accurate protein comparisons from mass spectrometry

    Modeling at the peptide level with robust ridge regression and shared variance estimates gives more precise and accurate protein fold changes than standard summarization methods, improving both sensitivity and specificity.

    Goeminne et al. 2016 · Mol Cell ProteomicsBenchmarking on the CPTAC spike-in study and a Francisella proteome study
  22. 22
    Peptide modelsoutperform summarization pipelines

    Peptide-based statistical models beat pipelines that first summarize peptides to proteins on sensitivity, specificity, accuracy and precision. Imputing missing values helps low-abundance proteins but hurts moderate and high-abundance ones.

    Goeminne et al. 2015 · J Proteome ResComparison of label-free proteomics analysis methods on benchmark data
  23. 23
    Hurdle modelhandles missing values in proteomics

    Combining a peptide count model with an intensity model lets proteins with many missing values be quantified without harmful assumptions about why values are missing.

    Goeminne et al. 2020 · Anal ChemMethod development and benchmarking against state-of-the-art tools
  24. 24
    IRG1rises in immune cells lacking A20

    Macrophages lacking the anti-inflammatory protein A20 have higher levels of several inflammatory proteins even without a stimulus, including IRG1, which is regulated at the gene expression level.

    Van Quickelberghe et al. 2018 · J Proteome ResDifferential proteomics of bone marrow macrophages from myeloid A20 knockout mice
