Ask about Dan Belsky's research

Answers are drawn only from Dan Belsky's published papers listed on this page, with each point linked to its paper. They describe what the papers report, not personal views or advice from Dan Belsky. This is not medical advice.

Try

Measurement of the pace of biological aging in humans and its use in evaluating interventions and social exposures.

  • Pace of aging. Using repeated multi-organ measurements in the Dunedin Study birth cohort, showed that young adults of the same age differ in their pace of biological aging, and developed DunedinPACE, a DNA methylation blood test of this rate.
  • Intervention trials. In secondary analyses of randomized trials, two years of caloric restriction in CALERIE slowed DunedinPACE, and omega-3 supplementation in DO-HEALTH had small slowing effects on several DNA methylation clocks.
  • Comparison of aging measures. A side-by-side analysis of eleven telomere, epigenetic clock and biomarker-composite measures in the same cohort found low agreement between different quantifications of biological aging.
  • Social exposures and cognitive aging. Faster pace of aging was associated with socioeconomic disadvantage in childhood and, in older adults, with cognitive impairment and dementia.

  1. 1
    1 blood testestimates how fast a person is aging right now

    DunedinPACE condenses twenty years of decline in 19 measures of organ health into a single DNA methylation blood test. It gave consistent results on repeat testing, was linked to disease, disability and death, and added prediction beyond the GrimAge clock.

    Belsky et al. 2022 · ElifeDunedin Study birth cohort (born 1972-73), tested in five more datasets
  2. 2
    25%calorie restriction for 2 years slowed the pace of aging

    In the CALERIE trial, healthy adults without obesity assigned to cut calories by 25% for two years had a slower pace of aging on DunedinPACE than those on their usual diet. Biological age estimates from other clocks, including PhenoAge and GrimAge, did not change significantly, and the effects were small.

    Waziry et al. 2023 · Nat AgingRandomized controlled trial, 220 adults
  3. 3
    954young adults of the same age were already aging at different speeds

    In Dunedin Study members followed through their 20s and 30s, the decline of lungs, gums, heart, kidneys, liver and immune function ran faster in some people than others. By age 38, the faster agers were less physically able, showed early cognitive decline and brain aging, rated their health worse and looked older.

    Belsky et al. 2015 · Proc Natl Acad Sci U S ABirth cohort, 954 people, 3 time points over 12 years
  4. 4
    HR 1.27higher dementia risk with faster DunedinPACE

    In older adults, the older Horvath, Hannum, PhenoAge and GrimAge clocks were not consistently tied to cognitive impairment, but faster DunedinPACE was tied to Alzheimer's diagnosis and worse test scores. In a second cohort, faster DunedinPACE predicted who went on to develop dementia (hazard ratio 1.27, 95% CI 1.07-1.49).

    Sugden et al. 2022 · NeurologyADNI (649 people) and Framingham Offspring Cohort (2,264 people)
  5. 5
    2.9-3.8 monthsless biological aging over 3 years, mainly from omega-3

    In older adults in the DO-HEALTH trial, omega-3 (1 g a day) on its own slowed the PhenoAge, GrimAge2 and DunedinPACE clocks over three years. Omega-3, vitamin D (2,000 IU a day) and a home exercise program added up for PhenoAge. The benefit was small.

    Bischoff-Ferrari et al. 2025 · Nat AgingPost hoc analysis of a randomized trial, 777 adults
  6. 6
    11 measuresof biological aging largely disagreed with each other

    When telomere length, three epigenetic clocks and three biomarker composites were measured in the same middle-aged people, they agreed poorly. The 71-site epigenetic clock and the biomarker composites tracked physical function, cognitive decline and looking older best, but effects were modest.

    Belsky et al. 2018 · Am J EpidemiolDunedin Study, 964 people
  7. 7
    about halfof education's survival benefit was explained by slower aging

    People who got more education than their parents had a slower pace of aging on DunedinPACE (r = -0.18), including when siblings were compared. Upward educational mobility was linked to lower risk of death (hazard ratio 0.89), and slower aging accounted for about half of that link.

    Graf et al. 2024 · JAMA Netw OpenFramingham Heart Study, 3,101 people across two generations
  8. 8
    6 decadesafter famine in the womb, survivors were aging faster

    People exposed before birth to the 1944-45 Dutch Hunger Winter had a faster DunedinPACE at about age 58 than matched controls, most strongly in women. GrimAge showed a smaller difference and PhenoAge none, and the effect did not depend on when in pregnancy the exposure happened.

    Cheng et al. 2024 · Proc Natl Acad Sci U S ANatural experiment birth cohort, 951 people
  9. 9
    19,045older adults in the US and UK measured with an adapted Pace of Aging

    An adapted Pace of Aging method that combines blood tests, physical measurements and function tests over time showed large differences in aging speed between population groups. Faster Pace of Aging consistently predicted new disease, disability and death.

    Balachandran et al. 2025 · Nat AgingHealth and Retirement Study and English Longitudinal Study of Ageing
  10. 10
    174diseases tested against 14 epigenetic clocks

    Over ten years of follow-up, second and third generation clocks predicted disease much better than first generation clocks. For 27 diseases, including primary lung cancer and diabetes, a clock's link to the disease was stronger than its link to death, with most promise for lung and liver conditions.

    Mavrommatis et al. 2025 · Nat CommunPopulation cohort, 18,859 people
  11. 11
    r = 0.18link between family and neighborhood disadvantage and faster aging in children

    Children aged 8 to 18 living in more disadvantaged families and neighborhoods had a faster pace of aging measured from saliva DNA methylation. The link held after accounting for puberty, body mass index and tobacco exposure.

    Raffington et al. 2021 · PediatricsTexas Twin Project, 600 children
  12. 12
    140 studieslower income and status go with older clocks, mostly the newer ones

    Across 140 studies, links between socioeconomic status and biological age were weak for first generation clocks (r = -0.03) and stronger for second (r = -0.11) and third generation clocks (r = -0.13). Newer clocks are more responsive to social inequality.

    Willems et al. 2026 · Nat Hum BehavPre-registered systematic review and meta-analysis, 65,919 people
  13. 13
    13-95%of Black-White differences in healthspan accounted for by biological aging

    Among older Americans, GrimAge, DunedinPoAm and three blood chemistry measures showed more advanced or faster biological aging in Black than White participants (Cohen's d 0.4-0.5). These measures accounted for 13% to 95% of the Black-White differences in health and survival.

    Graf et al. 2022 · Am J EpidemiolHealth and Retirement Study, 9,005 people
  14. 14
    2 decadesof cognitive testing showed faster decline in faster agers

    Older adults with faster DunedinPACE started with poorer cognitive function and declined faster over about twenty years of testing. The association of DunedinPACE with cognitive decline explained about a quarter of dementia risk, and higher baseline cognition was protective.

    Savin et al. 2024 · Alzheimers Dement (Amst)Framingham Heart Study Offspring Cohort, 2,296 people
  15. 15
    3 cohortsfaster pace of aging, smaller brains

    From midlife to old age, faster DunedinPACE was linked to lower total brain and hippocampal volume, more white matter lesions and thinner cortex. DunedinPACE and GrimAge had the strongest links with brain structure among five clocks tested.

    Whitman et al. 2024 · Neurobiol AgingDunedin, Framingham and ADNI MRI data, 2,322 people
  16. 16
    Frameworkfor naming, classifying and validating biomarkers of aging

    An expert consensus sets out shared terms, types and possible clinical uses for biomarkers of aging, the validation steps they need, and open challenges before they can be used in trials and practice.

    Moqri et al. 2023 · CellExpert consensus review
  17. 17
    11.7%average calorie cut achieved, and aging still slowed

    In an early analysis of CALERIE biobank data, two blood-test measures of biological age (Klemera-Doubal biological age and homeostatic dysregulation) rose more slowly with calorie restriction than with usual diet. Weight loss did not account for the effect.

    Belsky et al. 2017 · J Gerontol A Biol Sci Med SciRandomized controlled trial, 220 adults, 3 time points
  18. 18
    10% fasteraging with lifelong socioeconomic disadvantage

    Adults who were disadvantaged from childhood through adulthood aged about 10% faster over 11 years than those who were always advantaged. Health behaviors explained part, but not all, of the gap.

    Schrempft et al. 2022 · J Gerontol A Biol Sci Med SciSwiss population cohort, 5,309 adults, 12 biomarkers at 3 visits
  19. 19
    6 childhood factorspredicted adult pace of aging

    Less longevity in the family, lower childhood social class, adverse childhood experiences, poorer childhood health, lower intelligence and lower self-control each predicted a faster Pace of Aging in young adults, and more of these risks meant faster aging.

    Belsky et al. 2017 · Aging CellDunedin Study, 954 people
  20. 20
    24 yearsfaster rise in epigenetic clocks predicted death

    In older Italian adults, people whose epigenetic clocks advanced faster over time had a higher risk of death, independent of their starting epigenetic age and other factors.

    Kuo et al. 2026 · Nat AgingInCHIANTI cohort, 699 adults with repeat measures
  21. 21
    42,625adults with healthier diet and activity had younger biological age

    People who followed a Mediterranean diet more closely or did more leisure-time exercise had younger PhenoAge biological ages, each on its own, regardless of age, sex or body mass index.

    Thomas et al. 2023 · J Gerontol A Biol Sci Med SciUS national surveys (NHANES 1999-2018)
  22. 22
    11.3%higher risk of depression or anxiety per step of faster aging

    Over about nine years, people whose PhenoAge biological age ran ahead of their real age were more likely to develop depression or anxiety, an 11.3% higher risk per standard deviation. This added to genetic risk rather than overlapping with it.

    Gao et al. 2023 · Nat CommunUK Biobank, 424,299 people
  23. 23
    57%higher risk of death with persistent loneliness

    Older Americans with persistent loneliness had a 57% higher risk of death than those never lonely, and social isolation a 28% higher risk. Both groups also showed signs of more advanced biological aging.

    Crowe et al. 2021 · J Gerontol A Biol Sci Med SciHealth and Retirement Study, 11,302 adults aged 50-95
  24. 24
    24 to 72years, the spread of brain age among 45-year-olds

    Among people all aged 45, brain age estimated from MRI ranged from 24 to 72 years. Older brain age went with faster pace of aging, an older-looking face and cognitive decline, but also with poorer cognition in childhood, so it partly reflects lifelong differences, not only aging.

    Elliott et al. 2021 · Mol PsychiatryDunedin Study, 869 people
  25. 25
    6.4-7.8 hoursof sleep went with the youngest biological age across organs

    Across 23 aging clocks from brain and body imaging, blood proteins and metabolites, both short and long sleep were linked to older biological age, in a U-shaped pattern. Sleeping under 6 or over 8 hours was linked to higher risk of disease and death.

    MULTI Consortium et al. 2026 · NatureUK Biobank, adults aged 37-84
  26. 26
    20,000+people in 5 studies show education genetics predicts social mobility

    People with higher education polygenic scores tended to climb above their parents' social position, including when siblings were compared. A mother's score also predicted her child's success beyond the child's own score, pointing to effects through the family environment.

    Belsky et al. 2018 · Proc Natl Acad Sci U S AFive longitudinal studies in the US, UK and New Zealand
  27. 27
    about halfof genetic obesity risk worked through fast early-childhood growth

    Children with higher genetic risk for obesity were not heavier at birth but gained weight faster in early childhood and reached adiposity rebound earlier. These growth patterns accounted for about half of the genetic effect on adult obesity.

    Belsky et al. 2012 · Arch Pediatr Adolesc MedDunedin Study, 1,037 people followed to age 38
