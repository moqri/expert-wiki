Ask about Vadim Gladyshev's research

Answers are drawn only from Vadim Gladyshev's published papers listed on this page, with each point linked to its paper. They describe what the papers report, not personal views or advice from Vadim Gladyshev. This is not medical advice.

Try

Molecular biomarkers of aging, comparative biology of longevity, and selenoprotein biology.

  • Dynamics of biological age. Showed that biological age increases with stress such as surgery, pregnancy and severe COVID-19 and is restored upon recovery (Cell Metabolism 2023), and identified a rejuvenation event during embryogenesis (Science Advances 2021).
  • Comparative longevity. Sequenced the genome of the naked mole-rat (Nature 2011) and showed that naked mole-rat Has2 expression increases hyaluronan and improves healthspan in mice (Nature 2023).
  • Longevity interventions. Identified gene expression signatures associated with lifespan extension (Cell Metabolism 2019) and showed that rapamycin treatment during development extends lifespan in male mice (Science Advances 2022).
  • Selenoproteins. Characterized the mammalian selenoproteomes, including the 25 human selenoprotein genes (Science 2003).

  1. 1
    Reversiblerise in biological age during stress

    Biological age goes up quickly under stress and comes back down after recovery. This was seen in young mice joined to old mice and then separated, and in people and mice around major surgery, pregnancy and severe COVID-19.

    Poganik et al. 2023 · Cell MetabEpigenetic, transcriptomic and metabolomic aging clocks in mouse parabiosis and in human and mouse stress cohorts
  2. 2
    Ground zerothe point in early embryo development where biological age is lowest

    Biological age drops during early embryo development, reaches a minimum, and then starts rising again. This natural rejuvenation was seen in both mice and humans, and suggests organismal aging begins from this "ground zero".

    Kerepesi et al. 2021 · Sci AdvNew multi-tissue epigenetic clock plus other aging clocks applied to mouse and human prenatal development
  3. 3
    Longer lifefor old mice after 3 months joined to young mice

    Old mice that shared blood circulation with young mice for 3 months, then were separated for 2 months, lived longer and were healthier than controls. Their blood and liver epigenetic age stayed lower even after separation.

    Zhang et al. 2023 · Nat AgingExtended heterochronic parabiosis in mice with lifespan follow-up and multi-omic clocks
  4. 4
    >11,000transcriptomes from 4 mammals used to find shared signs of aging

    Aging changes in gene activity are shared across mouse, rat, macaque and human, and some, such as higher CDKN1A and LGALS3, also predict death and multiple diseases in people. Chronic diseases mainly sped up aging of inflammation genes, while caloric restriction acted on mitochondrial and metabolic genes.

    Tyshkovskiy et al. 2026 · NatureIntegration of more than 11,000 transcriptomes from more than 25 tissues, with UK Biobank protein validation
  5. 5
    41mammalian species compared for lifespan signatures

    Long-lived species and lifespan-extending treatments share some features, such as lower Igf1 and higher mitochondrial protein synthesis, but differ in others. A drug picked by these signatures, KU0063794, extended lifespan and healthspan in mice.

    Tyshkovskiy et al. 2023 · CellMulti-tissue RNA-seq across 41 mammalian species and lifespan-extending interventions, with a mouse lifespan study
  6. 6
    17lifespan-extending interventions with shared liver gene changes

    Many different ways of extending mouse lifespan produce similar gene expression changes in the liver, including higher oxidative phosphorylation and drug metabolism. These shared signatures pointed to new candidates such as chronic hypoxia and ascorbyl palmitate.

    Tyshkovskiy et al. 2019 · Cell MetabRNA-seq of mice on 8 longevity interventions, extended to 17 with public data
  7. 7
    +10%median lifespan from rapamycin in the first 45 days of life

    Giving mice rapamycin only during their first 45 days slowed growth, extended median lifespan by 10% (most strongly in males) and kept them healthier in old age. Their liver was molecularly younger at the end of treatment, and rapamycin during development also extended lifespan in water fleas.

    Shindyapina et al. 2022 · Sci AdvLifespan and healthspan study in genetically diverse UM-HET3 mice, plus Daphnia magna
  8. 8
    Lower body temperaturedrives slower epigenetic aging in a torpor-like state

    Switching on a group of brain neurons put mice into a torpor-like state, which slowed epigenetic aging across tissues and improved healthspan. The slowing came from lower body temperature rather than lower metabolism or eating less.

    Jayne et al. 2025 · Nat AgingNeural induction of a torpor-like state in mice, with epigenetic clocks and healthspan measures
  9. 9
    18rodent species showing DNA break repair improves with lifespan

    Longer-lived rodent species repair DNA double-strand breaks better, largely because their SIRT6 protein is more active. Five amino acids explain the difference between the weak mouse and strong beaver SIRT6.

    Tian et al. 2019 · CellComparative DNA repair assays in cells from 18 rodent species
  10. 10
    >200 yearslifespan of the bowhead whale, which repairs DNA unusually well

    Bowhead whale cells repair DNA breaks more accurately and have lower mutation rates than other mammals' cells, helped by high levels of the protein CIRBP. Extra CIRBP extended lifespan in fruit flies.

    Firsanov et al. 2025 · NatureBowhead whale primary cells compared with other mammals, plus human cell and fly experiments
  11. 11
    Longer lifeand less cancer in mice given a naked mole-rat gene

    Mice carrying the naked mole-rat version of the hyaluronan-making gene Has2 had more hyaluronan, less cancer, longer lifespan and better health, mainly through lower inflammation. A longevity trait from one species can be moved to another.

    Zhang et al. 2023 · NatureTransgenic mice expressing naked mole-rat Has2
  12. 12
    11-22%fewer rare gene-breaking mutations in centenarians

    Centenarians carried 11 to 22% fewer rare loss-of-function mutations than controls, and so did their children. Genes such as RGP1, PCNX2 and ANO9 emerged as candidate longevity genes.

    Ying et al. 2024 · Nat CommunWhole-exome sequencing of Ashkenazi Jewish centenarians and controls, with UK Biobank validation
  13. 13
    1.3 yearsof lifespan variation linked to rare damaging variants

    People who inherit more of the rarest protein-truncating gene variants have shorter healthspan and lifespan, accounting for about 0.4 and 1.3 years of variation. Such mutations acquired during life explain only a small part of aging.

    Shindyapina et al. 2020 · ElifeAnalysis of two large human cohorts with genetic and health data
  14. 14
    Partly randomnature of epigenetic aging

    The DNA methylation changes that epigenetic clocks track include coordinated changes and a large random component, which behaves somewhat like radioactive decay. This helps explain what the clocks actually measure.

    Tarkhov et al. 2024 · Nat AgingTissue and single-cell DNA methylation and RNA data in mice across development and aging
  15. 15
    Single cellscan now be given an epigenetic age

    The scAge method estimates epigenetic age in individual cells, showing that cells of the same tissue age at different rates and that muscle stem cells age more slowly.

    Trapp et al. 2021 · Nat AgingNew statistical method validated on mouse single-cell DNA methylation data
  16. 16
    ~90%of age-related DNA methylation gain falls in PRC2 target regions

    Regions bound by the PRC2 complex gain DNA methylation with age in all dividing body cells examined, making up about 90% of all age-related methylation gain. Their average, the PRC2-AgeIndex, works as a universal marker of cellular aging.

    Moqri et al. 2024 · Nat CommunGenome-wide DNA methylation analysis across somatic tissues and cell types
  17. 17
    DamAge and AdaptAgeclocks separating harmful from protective changes

    Using genetic data to find methylation sites that likely cause aging traits, the group built two clocks. DamAge tracks harmful changes and predicts death, while AdaptAge tracks protective adaptations.

    Ying et al. 2024 · Nat AgingEpigenome-wide Mendelian randomization and clock construction in human data
  18. 18
    >50,000people whose blood proteins were used to estimate organ age

    Blood protein clocks for individual organs predict disease in those organs and show men aging faster than women in most organs. Diet, lifestyle, job and medications change organ aging rates, so chronic diseases look like accelerated aging of specific organs.

    Goeminne et al. 2025 · Cell MetabPlasma proteomics in more than 50,000 people from UK Biobank and other cohorts
  19. 19
    Age reversalafter regeneration in old planarian flatworms

    Sexual planarian flatworms decline with age within 18 months. Cutting them and letting lost tissue regrow reversed aging changes even in tissues far from the cut.

    Dai et al. 2025 · Nat AgingPhysiological and single-cell profiling of young, old and regenerated planarians
  20. 20
    17rodent species showing protein-making accuracy rises with lifespan

    Longer-lived rodent species make fewer errors when building proteins. The basic error rate, not its change with age, tracks lifespan.

    Ke et al. 2017 · Aging CellLuciferase reporter assays of translation errors in cells from 17 rodent species
  21. 21
    20%slower protein building from young adulthood to mid-life

    The speed at which ribosomes build proteins differs by more than 50% between mouse organs and falls by about 20% between young adulthood and mid-life.

    Gerashchenko et al. 2021 · Nucleic Acids ResNew in vivo ribosome profiling method in mice
  22. 22
    Methionine restrictionextends life in flies and yeast

    Cutting the amino acid methionine extended lifespan in fruit flies and yeast, but only when overall amino acid intake was low. The effect depends on TOR signalling.

    Lee et al. 2014 · Nat CommunDiet and genetic experiments in Drosophila and yeast
  23. 23
    Naked mole-rats ageat the epigenetic level despite flat death rates

    Naked mole-rats, whose risk of death does not rise with age, still show age-related DNA methylation changes. A clock built for them shows they age much more slowly than mice and faster than humans.

    Kerepesi et al. 2022 · Nat CommunBlood DNA methylation sequencing of more than 100 naked mole-rats
  24. 24
    435DNA sites in a clock covering the whole mouse lifespan

    A mouse epigenetic clock using 435 DNA methylation sites works across tissues and the entire lifespan, and detects the effects of lifespan-changing treatments and reprogramming to stem cells.

    Meer et al. 2018 · ElifeMulti-tissue mouse DNA methylation data
  25. 25
    Deleteriomethe sum of harmful changes that defines biological age

    Competing theories of aging can be unified. Aging is a progressive loss of fitness from the build-up of countless small harmful changes, the deleteriome, shaped by genes, environment and chance.

    Gladyshev 2016 · Aging CellConceptual review
  26. 26
    25selenium-containing proteins in humans

    Most selenoprotein genes had been misannotated because their selenium codon also reads as a stop signal. New genome search methods showed the human selenoproteome has 25 selenoproteins.

    Kryukov et al. 2003 · ScienceComputational and experimental analysis of mammalian genomes
