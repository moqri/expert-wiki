Ask about Morgan E. Levine's research

Answers are drawn only from Morgan E. Levine's published papers listed on this page, with each point linked to its paper. They describe what the papers report, not personal views or advice from Morgan E. Levine. This is not medical advice.

Try

Development of clinical and DNA methylation biomarkers of biological aging and their determinants.

  • Clinical biological age. Developed a phenotypic aging measure from routine blood chemistry in NHANES that captured morbidity and mortality risk beyond chronological age (PLoS Medicine 2018).
  • Epigenetic clocks. Developed DNAm PhenoAge, a methylation clock trained on phenotypic age, and principal-component clocks that substantially improved test-retest reliability.
  • System-specific aging. Developed Systems Age, which estimates aging of 11 physiological systems from a single blood methylation profile (Nature Aging 2025).
  • Determinants and mechanisms. Reported accelerated epigenetic aging with earlier menopause and in the prefrontal cortex in Alzheimer's disease, and linked epigenetic aging to replication-driven changes shared with cancer.

  1. 1
    PhenoAgea DNA methylation clock trained on health, not calendar age

    A methylation clock built in two steps, first from clinical measures of "phenotypic age" and then from blood DNA methylation, strongly outperformed earlier clocks in predicting death, cancers, healthspan, physical functioning and Alzheimer's disease. Faster PhenoAge went with more inflammatory and interferon activity and less DNA repair and mitochondrial activity.

    Levine et al. 2018 · Aging (Albany NY)Clock development in large population cohorts, with transcriptional analysis in sorted blood cells
  2. 2
    11body systems scored from one blood draw

    Systems Age uses blood DNA methylation to estimate aging separately for 11 systems, including heart, lung, kidney, liver, brain, immune and metabolic. The system scores beat existing whole-body clocks at predicting the related diseases, and grouping people by their scores revealed distinct aging subtypes with different patterns of decline.

    Sehgal et al. 2025 · Nat AgingClock development combining clinical biomarkers, function and mortality data across cohorts
  3. 3
    9 → 1.5 yearsgap between repeat tests of the same sample

    Technical noise made six well-known epigenetic clocks disagree by up to 9 years between repeat measurements of the same sample. Rebuilding the clocks from principal components brought most repeats within 1.5 years and gave clearer detection of intervention effects and more reliable tracking over time.

    Higgins-Chen et al. 2022 · Nat AgingTechnical replicate datasets plus longitudinal human and cell culture data
  4. 4
    11,432US adults in which Phenotypic Age predicted death

    Phenotypic Age, calculated from age and nine routine blood chemistry markers, predicted death from all causes and most specific causes after accounting for calendar age. It worked across age, race and ethnic, education and health-behavior groups, and even in people who reported no disease and had a normal BMI.

    Liu et al. 2018 · PLoS MedNationally representative cohort (NHANES IV), 1,012 deaths over 12.6 years
  5. 5
    9,389adults used to compare five biological age methods

    Of five ways to compute biological age from clinical measures, the Klemera and Doubal method predicted death best. When it was in the model, calendar age was no longer significantly linked to mortality.

    Levine 2013 · J Gerontol A Biol Sci Med SciNHANES III cohort aged 30 to 75, 1,843 deaths over 18 years
  6. 6
    954young adults whose pace of aging already differed

    People of the same calendar age in their 30s already differed in how fast their organ systems were declining. Those aging faster were less physically able, showed cognitive decline and brain aging, rated their health worse and looked older.

    Belsky et al. 2015 · Proc Natl Acad Sci U S ADunedin birth cohort followed with repeated biomarkers across three time points
  7. 7
    Low agreementamong 11 measures of biological aging

    Telomere length, three epigenetic clocks and three biomarker composites measured in the same people agreed poorly with each other. The biomarker composites and one epigenetic clock were the most consistently linked to physical function, cognitive decline and looking older, though effects were modest.

    Belsky et al. 2018 · Am J EpidemiolDunedin Study, 964 middle-aged adults with repeated measures
  8. 8
    +75%overall mortality with high protein intake at ages 50 to 65

    People aged 50 to 65 who ate a high-protein diet had 75% higher overall mortality and a 4-fold higher risk of cancer death over 18 years, a link that weakened or vanished when the protein came from plants. After age 65, higher protein intake went with lower cancer and overall mortality.

    Levine et al. 2014 · Cell MetabNHANES III cohort follow-up plus mouse tumor experiments
  9. 9
    Menopausespeeds epigenetic aging of blood

    Earlier menopause, removal of both ovaries and longer time since menopause were each linked to faster epigenetic aging of blood. Menopausal hormone therapy was linked to a younger epigenetic age of cheek cells, and genetic (Mendelian randomization) evidence suggested menopause itself speeds blood aging.

    Levine et al. 2016 · Proc Natl Acad Sci U S AFour cohorts (WHI, InCHIANTI, PEG and NSHD) with genetic analysis
  10. 10
    h² = 0.41heritability of brain epigenetic age acceleration

    Faster epigenetic aging of the prefrontal cortex went with more diffuse and neuritic plaques and amyloid load, and with faster decline in overall cognition, episodic memory and working memory in people with Alzheimer's disease.

    Levine et al. 2015 · Aging (Albany NY)700 post-mortem brain samples from the Religious Orders Study and Rush Memory and Aging Project
  11. 11
    PCBrainAgea clock trained only on brain tissue

    A brain-specific epigenetic clock built with principal components was more reliable on retest and was more strongly linked to clinical Alzheimer's dementia, Alzheimer's pathology and APOE4 carrier status than existing clocks, across several brain regions.

    Thrush et al. 2022 · Aging (Albany NY)Multi-region brain methylation data from ROSMAP
  12. 12
    OR 1.63COVID-19 death per 5 years of older biological age

    Biological age (PhenoAge) measured more than 10 years before the pandemic predicted testing positive in hospital and dying with COVID-19, beyond calendar age. The link stayed after adjusting for pre-existing diseases.

    Kuo et al. 2021 · J Gerontol A Biol Sci Med SciUK Biobank, 613 inpatient positive tests and 154 deaths
  13. 13
    4,173women whose diet and lifestyle were linked to epigenetic age

    Eating more fish, fruit and vegetables, moderate alcohol and more education went with slower epigenetic aging, while higher BMI and metabolic syndrome went with faster aging. In this observational data, metformin users did not show slower epigenetic aging.

    Quach et al. 2017 · Aging (Albany NY)Cross-sectional analysis of the Women's Health Initiative plus 402 InCHIANTI participants
  14. 14
    −2.5 yearsmedian biological age after three fasting-mimicking diet cycles

    Three cycles of a fasting-mimicking diet lowered insulin resistance and liver fat and lowered median biological age by 2.5 years, independent of weight loss. A second clinical study gave nearly identical results.

    Brandhorst et al. 2024 · Nat CommunSecondary analysis of two clinical trials (NCT02158897, NCT04150159)
  15. 15
    HR 1.50lung cancer risk per unit of faster epigenetic aging

    Women whose blood showed faster intrinsic epigenetic aging were more likely to develop lung cancer over nearly 20 years, and the link looked stronger in women over 70 and in current smokers.

    Levine et al. 2015 · Aging (Albany NY)Women's Health Initiative, 2,029 women, 43 lung cancers
  16. 16
    Men > womenepigenetic aging rates in blood, saliva and brain

    Men showed faster epigenetic aging than women in blood, saliva and brain. Hispanic and Tsimane people had slower intrinsic but faster immune-related epigenetic aging than white people, patterns that may help explain known differences in mortality between groups.

    Horvath et al. 2016 · Genome BiolSamples from seven racial and ethnic groups
  17. 17
    156studies pooled on what speeds epigenetic clocks

    Across more than 1,300 findings, higher BMI, HIV infection and male sex were linked to faster epigenetic clocks, and faster clocks were linked to death, heart disease, cancer and diabetes. Four major clocks agreed in direction but differed in effect size.

    Oblak et al. 2021 · Ageing Res RevSystematic review of 156 publications
  18. 18
    1988 → 2010Americans' biological age got younger

    Biological age relative to calendar age was lower in 2007-2010 than in 1988-1994, most of all in older adults and in men. Changes in smoking, obesity and medication use explained part of the improvement.

    Levine 2018 · DemographyComparison of NHANES III and NHANES IV
  19. 19
    p = 1.5×10⁻⁷²APOE4 link to faster Phenotypic Age

    Genome-wide scans of two clinical biological age measures found their strongest signals in APOE, with the APOE4 variant tied to faster PhenoAge and the APOE2 variant to the other measure in opposite directions. The two measures pointed to different biology, immune for one and lipid for the other.

    Kuo et al. 2021 · Aging CellGenome-wide association study in UK Biobank participants of European descent
  20. 20
    Vision restoredin old and glaucoma mice by partial reprogramming

    Turning on three reprogramming genes (Oct4, Sox2, Klf4) in mouse retinal nerve cells restored youthful DNA methylation and gene activity, regrew nerve fibers after injury and reversed vision loss in glaucoma and in old mice. The effect needed the TET1 and TET2 enzymes.

    Lu et al. 2020 · NatureMouse experiments in retinal ganglion cells
  21. 21
    CellDRIFTa replication signature shared by aging and cancer

    A methylation signature of repeated cell division rose with age across tissues, separated tumors from normal tissue, was higher in normal breast tissue of cancer patients, and was briefly reset by reprogramming. Tissues with more lifetime stem cell divisions had higher scores and higher cancer risk.

    Minteer et al. 2023 · Sci AdvLong-term passaged human cells, tested in clinical tissue samples
  22. 22
    Senescenceand mitochondrial dysfunction speed some clocks

    Eleven epigenetic clocks barely shared any CpG sites, but five shared links to metabolism, immunity and autophagy. In cell experiments, the Levine and Lin clocks sped up with cellular senescence and mitochondrial dysfunction, and a combined meta-clock predicted death better than single clocks.

    Liu et al. 2020 · Aging CellMulti-omics analysis across human tissues plus in vitro experiments
  23. 23
    About 2×energy use of cells with mitochondrial defects

    Disrupting mitochondrial energy production roughly doubled cells' energy use, and patients with mitochondrial disease had raised resting energy expenditure. These defects also sped telomere loss and epigenetic aging per cell division.

    Sturm et al. 2023 · Commun Biol17 patient cohorts (690 people) plus longitudinal patient-derived fibroblasts
  24. 24
    Up to 5 yearsolder on mortality clocks in schizophrenia

    People with schizophrenia showed up to 5 years of faster aging on three mortality-trained epigenetic clocks, driven by smoking and six age-linked proteins, while two cell-division clocks ran slower, which may relate to differences in cancer risk.

    Higgins-Chen et al. 2020 · Biol PsychiatryThree blood methylation datasets, 567 cases and 594 controls, 14 clocks
  25. 25
    r = 0.93rat clock accuracy in an independent sample

    An epigenetic clock built in rats tracked age, related to physical function, also worked in mice, and was lower with caloric restriction. Its signal sat in regions marked by tightly packed chromatin.

    Levine et al. 2020 · ElifeReduced representation bisulfite sequencing in rats and mice
  26. 26
    Naked mole-ratsstill age epigenetically

    Although naked mole-rats show no rise in death rate with age, their blood methylation changes with age. A clock built for them showed they age epigenetically much more slowly than mice and much faster than humans, in line with their lifespan.

    Kerepesi et al. 2022 · Nat CommunBisulfite sequencing of blood from more than 100 naked mole-rats
  27. 27
    24-67%of stochastic epigenetic mutations not repeated on retest

    Rare outlier methylation changes, proposed as an aging biomarker, were often not reproduced in replicate samples. A machine learning filter improved reliability, and the reliable changes were linked to cardiovascular aging in the Framingham Heart Study.

    Markov et al. 2024 · GeroscienceTechnical replicates in three datasets plus the Framingham Heart Study
