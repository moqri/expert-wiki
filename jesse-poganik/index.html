Try

Dynamics of biological age under stress and organ transplantation, and validation of biomarkers of aging.

  • Reversible biological age. First author of a study showing that biological age increases with stress, including heterochronic parabiosis, major surgery, pregnancy and severe COVID-19, and is restored after recovery.
  • Organ transplantation. In mice and patients, transplanted hearts assimilated the recipient's biological age; in mice, old white adipose tissue was rejuvenated after transplantation into young hosts.
  • Biomarkers of aging. Co-authored the Biomarkers of Aging Consortium's definitions and evaluation criteria for aging biomarkers and contributed to a survey of researchers that found no consensus on foundational questions about aging.
  • Chemical biology. Earlier work developed Z-REX, a method for delivering reactive electrophiles to specific proteins in zebrafish, and identified mediators of immune-cell apoptosis induced by dimethyl fumarate.

  1. 1
    Up and back downbiological age rises under stress and recovers afterwards

    Biological age is not a one-way trajectory. It rose quickly in young mice joined to old mice and returned to normal after they were separated, and it rose temporarily in people and mice during major surgery, pregnancy and severe COVID-19, then fell after recovery.

    Poganik et al. 2023 · Cell MetabMouse parabiosis experiments plus human and mouse cohorts (surgery, pregnancy, COVID-19), measured with epigenetic, transcriptomic and metabolomic clocks
  2. 2
    Recipient, not donorsets the biological age of a transplanted heart

    Hearts transplanted between young and old mice quickly took on the biological age of the recipient, without changing the recipient's own overall biological age. In patients, the biological age of the transplanted heart was strongly linked to the recipient's age rather than the donor's.

    Poganik et al. 2026 · bioRxivHeterochronic heart transplants in mice plus heart transplant patients, DNA methylation and gene expression clocks (preprint)
  3. 3
    Frameworkfor defining and validating biomarkers of aging

    The Biomarkers of Aging Consortium set out shared terms, categories and possible clinical uses for biomarkers of aging, and the validation steps needed before they can be used in trials and practice.

    Moqri et al. 2023 · CellConsensus review by the Biomarkers of Aging Consortium (28 authors)
  4. 4
    R = 0.12link between a clock's age accuracy and its mortality prediction

    How well an aging clock predicts chronological age says little about how well it predicts death. Across 39 biomarkers in more than 20,000 people, the Horvath skin and blood clock best tracked age (R² 0.88), while GrimAge2 best predicted mortality (hazard ratio 2.57) and loss of healthspan (hazard ratio 2.00).

    Ying et al. 2025 · Nat AgingStandardized comparison of 39 aging biomarkers across more than 20,000 people in several cohorts (Biolearn)
  5. 5
    No majorityon any core question about what aging is

    A survey of aging researchers found broad disagreement on what aging is, what causes it, when it begins and what counts as rejuvenation. None of the questions drew a majority answer, not even whether the field needs a consensus.

    Gladyshev et al. 2024 · PNAS NexusSurvey of experts in aging research
  6. 6
    10%longer median lifespan from rapamycin in the first 45 days of life

    Giving mice rapamycin only during their first 45 days slowed growth, delayed reproduction without reducing offspring, extended median lifespan by 10% (most in males) and preserved health measures such as frailty, gait speed and glucose tolerance. Treated livers looked younger at the molecular level, and the same treatment extended lifespan in water fleas.

    Shindyapina et al. 2022 · Sci AdvLifespan and healthspan study in genetically diverse UM-HET3 mice, plus Daphnia magna
  7. 7
    Both directionsan ovary's molecular age follows its host

    Old mouse ovaries became molecularly younger in young hosts and young ovaries became older in old hosts. Ovaries from 13-month-old mice supported live births at 17 to 18 months of age when placed in young hosts but not old ones, and young ovaries lowered the molecular age of the uterus, liver and brain of old mice.

    Iqbal et al. 2026 · bioRxivReciprocal ovary transplantation and parabiosis in mice, with transcriptomic and DNA methylation clocks (preprint)
  8. 8
    Partly reversibleaging of fat tissue placed in a young body

    Old white fat tissue transplanted into young mice became younger by epigenetic and gene expression clocks, with smaller fat droplets and activation of heat-producing pathways. Transplantation itself aged tissue, and graft age shifted toward the host's age.

    Moldakozhayev et al. 2025 · bioRxivLongitudinal heterochronic and isochronic fat-tissue transplantation in mice (preprint)
  9. 9
    6 barriersstand between aging biomarkers and the clinic

    A survey of experts identified six key barriers to using biomarkers of aging in clinical settings. Core recommendations were to tie biomarkers to clinically actionable insights, make them affordable and widely available, and validate them as robust and responsive in individual people.

    Biomarkers of Aging Consortium et al. 2024 · Nat AgingExpert survey and recommendations
  10. 10
    Interpregnancy intervalproposed as a missing link to maternal aging

    Pregnancy temporarily raises biological age and the months after birth are a recovery window. Poganik and a colleague propose that starting another pregnancy before recovery is complete may let aging compound, so spacing between pregnancies could be a modifiable factor in a mother's lifelong aging.

    Albright 2026 · Trends Endocrinol MetabHypothesis and review with testable predictions
  11. 11
    Burden of prooffor claims that biological age was reversed

    Lowering a biological age estimate is not the same as reversing aging itself. The authors set out what age reversal should mean, what evidence such claims need, and how the language used in the debate shapes the conclusions.

    Poganik et al. 2026 · Cell MetabPerspective by aging and geriatrics researchers
  12. 12
    Validationof omic aging biomarkers in population studies

    No agreed standard yet exists for validating omic biomarkers of aging before clinical use. The review assesses how well they predict aging outcomes in population studies, the problems comparing them, and steps toward using them as endpoints in trials of treatments for healthy aging.

    Moqri et al. 2024 · Nat MedReview in Nature Medicine (29 authors)
  13. 13
    500+interventions found to lower biological age in public data

    AI agents reanalyzed 43,602 intervention-control comparisons from public methylation and RNA data with more than 40 aging clocks and found over 500 interventions that significantly lowered biological age. More interventions sped aging than slowed it, and ouabain, a top candidate, reduced frailty progression and neuroinflammation and improved heart function in old mice.

    Ying et al. 2025 · bioRxivReanalysis of millions of public human and mouse samples, with mouse validation (preprint)
  14. 14
    100xcheaper epigenetic clock measurement

    TIME-seq measures DNA methylation for epigenetic clocks at up to 100-fold lower cost than conventional methods. It was applied to more than 1,800 mouse samples from eight tissues and used to build a human blood clock (median error 3.4 years) in 1,056 people.

    Griffin et al. 2024 · Nat AgingMethods study, more than 1,800 mouse samples and 1,056 human blood samples
  15. 15
    17 tissuesshare conserved DNA methylation aging signatures

    Across more than 15,000 human methylation profiles from 17 tissues, aging produced both shared and tissue-specific changes, including rising variability and molecular disorder. One gene cluster linked to NAD+ metabolism was modifiable by beneficial interventions, pointing to NAD+ as a possible target.

    Jacques et al. 2026 · Nat AgingMeta-analysis of more than 15,000 human methylation profiles
  16. 16
    Pathway agesinstead of a single biological age

    Ageome estimates the epigenetic age of thousands of molecular pathways at once. Pathways aged at different rates, cell reprogramming rejuvenated some while accelerating the aging of others, and some pathway ages predicted age-related disease, especially cancer, better than existing clocks.

    Ying et al. 2024 · bioRxivComputational framework applied to mouse and human cohorts (preprint)
  17. 17
    r > 0.96accuracy of clocks that work across 185 mammal species

    Universal clocks built from 11,754 methylation samples covering 59 tissue types in 185 mammal species estimated age with high accuracy. The age-linked sites sit near developmental genes, suggesting aging is conserved across mammals and tied to development.

    Lu et al. 2023 · Nat AgingMammalian Methylation Consortium, 11,754 samples from 185 species
  18. 18
    Immune agingneeds agreed biomarkers for clinical trials

    There is no consensus on how to measure immune aging in trials. Applying five criteria, the authors found that multidimensional immune function scores, inflammaging scores and functional assays performed best as candidate biomarkers.

    Cipriano et al. 2026 · Nat MedReview and evaluation framework linked to XPRIZE Healthspan
  19. 19
    Dietand biological age

    Caloric restriction, Mediterranean diet, intermittent fasting and some micronutrient supplements are associated with lower biological age, while obesity, inflammatory diets and malnutrition are linked to faster aging. Responses vary between people and organs, which complicates translation.

    Albright et al. 2026 · Annu Rev NutrReview of animal and human studies, including clinical trials using omics aging biomarkers
  20. 20
    Replacementas a strategy against aging

    Replacing worn-out parts, through tissue engineering, cell therapy, transplants, xenotransplantation or devices, is considered as a route to counter aging, alongside evidence from parabiosis and age-mismatched transplants that a young environment can benefit old tissue.

    Lore et al. 2025 · Nat AgingPerspective review
  21. 21
    Multi-omic genesreplicate better than single-omic aging genes in blood

    Genes that change with age in both DNA methylation and gene expression in blood were enriched for adaptive immune functions, replicated more robustly across populations and were more strongly tied to aging outcomes than genes found with either data type alone.

    Moqri et al. 2026 · Nat CommunIntegrative epigenetic and transcriptomic analysis across diverse populations
  22. 22
    Data standardsfor longevity company trials

    Longevity biotechnology companies are urged to collect trial data and biomarkers of aging in standard ways, so results can be pooled to validate and benchmark aging biomarkers with little extra effort.

    Herzog et al. 2025 · NPJ AgingRecommendations paper
  23. 23
    Cell-type clocksfrom long-read DNA sequencing without cell sorting

    LongReadAge maps long DNA reads to their cell type of origin and estimates epigenetic age for each cell type, tracking myeloid cells and lymphocytes from bulk blood and from circulating cell-free DNA.

    Eames et al. 2025 · Aging CellMethods study using long-read sequencing
  24. 24
    Tecfiderakills specific immune cells through Keap1, Wdr1 and cofilin

    Targeted modification of the protein Keap1 by the multiple sclerosis drug dimethyl fumarate triggered death of neutrophils and macrophages, largely independent of the usual Nrf2 pathway, through Wdr1 and cofilin. Removing these players blocked the cell death in whole animals.

    Poganik et al. 2021 · Nat CommunPrecision electrophile targeting in zebrafish and cells
  25. 25
    HuR and AUF1control the antioxidant regulator Nrf2 at the RNA level

    Two RNA-binding proteins regulate Nrf2 by binding its messenger RNA. HuR helps it mature and leave the nucleus, AUF1 stabilizes it, and this control is conserved in whole zebrafish.

    Poganik et al. 2019 · FASEB JCell and zebrafish experiments with RNA sequencing
