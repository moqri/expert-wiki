Aging

Steve Horvath

Altos LabsPreviously Professor of Human Genetics and Biostatistics, University of California, Los Angeles

Builds DNA methylation "epigenetic clocks" that measure how old a tissue is biologically, and uses them to study what speeds up or slows down aging in humans and other mammals.

Steve Horvath's published work is best known for epigenetic clocks, which estimate age from chemical marks (DNA methylation) at a set of sites in the genome. Horvath's 2013 pan-tissue clock, built from about 8,000 samples covering 51 tissues and cell types, showed that one formula could estimate the age of almost any human tissue, reads near zero for embryonic and induced pluripotent stem cells, and gives a heritable measure of "age acceleration".

Later clocks, built with colleagues, were trained to predict health rather than calendar age. DNAm PhenoAge (2018) tracks mortality, cancer, healthspan and Alzheimer's disease; DNAm GrimAge (2019) and GrimAge2 (2022) combine methylation-based estimates of plasma proteins and smoking history and are among the strongest methylation predictors of time to death and heart disease. Independent cohorts have found GrimAge more closely tied to frailty, walking speed and mortality than first-generation clocks.

Applying these clocks, his papers report faster epigenetic aging with obesity (in liver), HIV infection, Down syndrome, Huntington's disease and earlier menopause, and slower aging in the cerebellum and in the offspring of people who lived past 105. Studies of older women link faster epigenetic aging with rising plasma Alzheimer's biomarkers and, for GrimAge2, with a higher risk of mild cognitive impairment or dementia.

Through the Mammalian Methylation Consortium, Horvath extended clocks to 185 and later 348 mammalian species, showing that age-related methylation changes are shared across mammals, concentrate at Polycomb (PRC2) target regions near developmental genes, and that a species' maximum lifespan is written into its methylation profile. Earlier, Horvath co-developed WGCNA, an R software package for finding networks of genes whose activity rises and falls together. Recent work uses clocks to test interventions such as young plasma fractions, partial reprogramming, metformin, plasmapheresis and torpor, with mixed results.

  1. 1
    51tissues and cell types read by one age formula

    A single DNA methylation formula based on 353 sites estimates the age of most human tissues and cell types. It reads close to zero for embryonic and induced pluripotent stem cells, rises with the number of times cells are passaged in culture, and its "age acceleration" is highly heritable.

    Horvath 2013 · Genome BiolPan-tissue clock built from 8,000 samples in 82 datasets; tested in 6,000 cancer samples
  2. 2
    P = 2×10⁻⁷⁵for predicting time to death

    GrimAge combines methylation-based estimates of seven plasma proteins (including PAI-1 and GDF15) and smoking pack-years. It outperformed earlier epigenetic clocks in predicting time to death, coronary heart disease and cancer, and was linked to fatty liver, visceral fat and age at menopause.

    Lu et al. 2019 · Aging (Albany NY)Clock development with validation in thousands of people from large cohorts
  3. 3
    PhenoAgea clock trained on health, not calendar age

    Training a methylation clock on a clinical measure of "phenotypic age" produced DNAm PhenoAge, which strongly outperformed earlier clocks in predicting death, cancers, healthspan, physical functioning and Alzheimer's disease. Faster PhenoAge went with more inflammatory and interferon activity and less DNA repair and mitochondrial activity.

    Levine et al. 2018 · Aging (Albany NY)Two-step clock development in blood, tested across tissues and sorted cells
  4. 4
    r > 0.96accuracy of one clock across 185 mammal species

    Universal pan-mammalian clocks estimate tissue age across 185 species and 59 tissue types. The age-related sites are concentrated at Polycomb (PRC2) binding locations near developmental genes, suggesting that aging is conserved across mammals and intertwined with development.

    Lu et al. 2023 · Nat Aging11,754 methylation arrays from the Mammalian Methylation Consortium
  5. 5
    8 of 9health outcomes linked to GrimAge, versus none for first-generation clocks

    In older Irish adults, faster GrimAge was associated with 8 of 9 clinical measures, including walking speed, frailty and cognition, and still predicted walking speed, polypharmacy, frailty and death after full adjustment. The Horvath and Hannum clocks did not predict these outcomes.

    McCrory et al. 2021 · J Gerontol A Biol Sci Med SciCohort of 490 adults (TILDA), up to 10 years of follow-up
  6. 6
    P = 4×10⁻¹⁶⁷GrimAge2 prediction of death across 9 cohorts

    GrimAge version 2 adds methylation estimates of C-reactive protein and hemoglobin A1c. It predicted mortality better than the original GrimAge across racial and ethnic groups, and also applies to younger people and saliva samples.

    Lu et al. 2022 · Aging (Albany NY)13,399 blood samples from nine cohorts
  7. 7
    HR 1.11higher risk of MCI or dementia per 1 SD faster GrimAge2

    Among 6,069 cognitively healthy older women followed for a median of 9.3 years, faster GrimAge2 aging was linked to a higher risk of mild cognitive impairment or probable dementia, and was the only clock that stayed significant after correction for multiple testing. First-generation clocks were not linked.

    Nguyen et al. 2026 · Aging CellWomen's Health Initiative Memory Study, 1,307 incident cases
  8. 8
    15 yearsof plasma Alzheimer's biomarker tracking

    In cognitively healthy older women, a faster pace of biological aging (DunedinPACE) at baseline was linked to faster rises over 15 years in plasma p-tau181, p-tau217, neurofilament light and GFAP.

    Zhang et al. 2025 · Alzheimers Dement2,366 women (873 with repeat samples), Women's Health Initiative Memory Study
  9. 9
    h² = 0.41heritability of brain epigenetic age acceleration

    Older epigenetic age of the prefrontal cortex was associated with more neuritic plaques and amyloid load, and with faster decline in global cognition, episodic memory and working memory in people with Alzheimer's disease. Brain epigenetic age acceleration was itself heritable.

    Levine et al. 2015 · Aging (Albany NY)700 post-mortem prefrontal cortex samples (ROS/MAP)
  10. 10
    3.3 yearsolder liver per 10 BMI units

    Higher body mass index was linked to older epigenetic age in the liver, but not in blood, muscle or fat tissue. The liver age acceleration did not reverse in the short term after rapid weight loss from bariatric surgery.

    Horvath et al. 2014 · Proc Natl Acad Sci U S AHuman blood, liver, muscle and adipose methylation datasets
  11. 11
    6.6 yearsolder blood and brain in Down syndrome

    Trisomy 21 increases the epigenetic age of blood and brain tissue by an average of 6.6 years, giving molecular evidence that Down syndrome accelerates aging.

    Horvath et al. 2015 · Aging CellEpigenetic clock analysis of blood and brain samples
  12. 12
    7.4 yearsolder brain tissue with HIV infection

    HIV infection was associated with older epigenetic age in brain tissue (7.4 years) and blood (5.2 years). The blood effect may partly reflect shifts in blood cell types such as exhausted cytotoxic T cells.

    Horvath 2015 · J Infect DisSix new DNA methylation datasets from brain and blood
  13. 13
    Earlier menopausegoes with faster blood epigenetic aging

    Earlier menopause, removal of both ovaries, and longer time since menopause were each associated with faster epigenetic aging of blood. Women on menopausal hormone therapy had a lower epigenetic age in cheek cells.

    Levine et al. 2016 · Proc Natl Acad Sci U S AAbout 3,100 women from four studies, including the Women's Health Initiative, with Mendelian randomization
  14. 14
    3.2 yearsolder brain regions in Huntington's disease

    Huntington's disease was associated with older epigenetic age in specific brain regions (frontal lobe, parietal lobe and cingulate gyrus), with an estimated increase in biological age of 3.2 years.

    Horvath et al. 2016 · Aging (Albany NY)475 brain samples from 26 cases and 39 controls
  15. 15
    5.1 yearsyounger blood in children of people who reached 105+

    Offspring of Italian semi-supercentenarians (age 105 to 109) had a lower epigenetic age than age-matched controls, and the semi-supercentenarians themselves were 8.6 years younger than expected for their age.

    Horvath et al. 2015 · Aging (Albany NY)82 semi-supercentenarians, 63 offspring and 47 controls
  16. 16
    Cerebellumages more slowly than other body sites

    Across up to 30 anatomic sites from supercentenarians and younger people, the cerebellum had a younger epigenetic age than other parts of the body, a pattern linked to RNA helicase genes.

    Horvath et al. 2015 · Aging (Albany NY)Three new and three published human methylation datasets
  17. 17
    Menage faster epigenetically than women

    Men had higher epigenetic aging rates than women in blood, saliva and brain. Hispanics and Tsimane Amerindians had lower intrinsic but higher immune-related aging rates in blood than Caucasians, and epigenetic aging rates were not associated with later coronary heart disease events.

    Horvath et al. 2016 · Genome BiolBlood, saliva and brain samples from seven racial and ethnic groups
  18. 18
    391sites in the skin and blood clock

    The skin and blood clock accurately estimates the age of fibroblasts, skin cells, cheek swabs, saliva and blood. It detected faster aging in cells from children with progeria and showed that epigenetic age rises steadily as cells divide in culture.

    Horvath et al. 2018 · Aging (Albany NY)Clock development in multiple human cell types and tissues
  19. 19
    r ≈ −0.75methylation telomere estimate versus age (measured telomeres ≈ −0.35)

    A methylation-based estimate of telomere length (DNAmTL) tracks age more closely than measured telomere length and better predicts time to death, coronary heart disease and heart failure.

    Lu et al. 2019 · Aging (Albany NY)Validation in about 10,000 samples from cohorts including Framingham and the Women's Health Initiative
  20. 20
    ~90%of age-related methylation gain falls in PRC2 regions

    Low-methylated regions bound by Polycomb Repressive Complex 2 gain methylation with age in every somatic dividing cell type examined, accounting for about 90% of genome-wide age-related gain. Their average methylation (the PRC2-AgeIndex) is proposed as a universal marker of cellular aging and rejuvenation.

    Moqri et al. 2024 · Nat CommunWhole-genome methylation analysis across somatic tissues and cells
  21. 21
    R = 0.89prediction of a species' maximum lifespan

    DNA methylation predicts a species' maximum lifespan, gestation time and age at sexual maturity. This lifespan signature is an intrinsic property of the species, is unaffected by caloric restriction or partial reprogramming, and differs from the signals that track an individual's mortality risk.

    Li et al. 2024 · Sci Adv15,000 samples from 348 mammalian species
  22. 22
    348mammal species in a methylation "family tree"

    Methylation profiles of 348 mammals form trees that closely mirror the species' evolutionary family tree. Of 55 co-methylation modules, 30 relate to traits such as maximum lifespan, body weight, age and sex, and some respond to caloric restriction, growth hormone receptor loss, high-fat diet and Yamanaka factors.

    Haghani et al. 2023 · Science15,456 methylation profiles from the Mammalian Methylation Consortium
  23. 23
    Queensof naked mole rats age more slowly than non-breeders

    Naked mole rats, long thought to show negligible aging, do age epigenetically. Breeding queens age more slowly than non-breeding animals.

    Horvath et al. 2022 · Nat Aging385 samples from 11 tissue types on a mammalian methylation array
  24. 24
    Over halfdrop in rat blood, heart and liver epigenetic age

    In old rats, a fraction of young pig plasma more than halved the epigenetic age of blood, heart and liver by rat-specific clocks, with smaller effects in the hypothalamus, alongside improvements in organ function and cognition tests.

    Horvath et al. 2024 · GeroscienceRat experiment using six new rat clocks built from 613 tissue samples
  25. 25
    No rejuvenationfrom repeated plasmapheresis in healthy adults

    Removing plasma 4 or 8 times over 18 weeks in healthy blood donors did not rejuvenate epigenetic clocks. GrimAge, the Hannum clock and DunedinPACE rose instead, although blood lipids fell.

    Borsky et al. 2025 · Sci RepRandomized cross-over clinical trial in healthy adults
  26. 26
    ~6 yearsyounger brain aging in metformin-treated monkeys

    Over 40 months, metformin slowed multiple aging clocks in adult male cynomolgus monkeys, with about a 6-year reduction in brain aging and preserved brain structure and cognition, partly through the antioxidant factor Nrf2.

    Yang et al. 2024 · Cell40-month primate study with transcriptomic, methylation, proteomic and metabolomic clocks
  27. 27
    Lower body temperatureslows blood epigenetic aging in mice

    Inducing a torpor-like state in mice slowed epigenetic aging across tissues and improved healthspan, and the slowing of blood aging was driven by lower core body temperature rather than lower metabolism or calorie intake.

    Jayne et al. 2025 · Nat AgingMouse experiments with neuronal induction of torpor
  28. 28
    39 daysof Yamanaka-factor gene therapy in old rat brains

    Delivering the Yamanaka factors (OSKM) to the hippocampus of old rats improved performance in a maze memory test, partly reversed age-related methylation in the hippocampus, and caused no pathology within 39 days; rat clocks showed only marginal rejuvenation.

    Horvath et al. 2025 · GeroscienceRat gene-therapy study in 25-month-old females
  29. 29
    WGCNAsoftware for gene co-expression networks

    The WGCNA R package finds modules of genes whose activity is highly correlated, summarizes them by eigengenes or hub genes, and relates them to sample traits, helping to screen for candidate biomarkers and drug targets.

    Langfelder 2008 · BMC BioinformaticsMethods and software paper with tutorials
  30. 30
    Clock theorylinking development and maintenance to aging

    Epigenetic clocks give accurate age estimates for any tissue across the whole life course, and link developmental and maintenance processes to biological aging in a unified "epigenetic clock theory" of aging.

    Horvath 2018 · Nat Rev GenetReview in Nature Reviews Genetics
