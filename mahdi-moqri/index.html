Ask about Mahdi Moqri's research

Answers are drawn only from Mahdi Moqri's published papers listed on this page, with each point linked to its paper. They describe what the papers report, not personal views or advice from Mahdi Moqri. This is not medical advice.

Try

Development and validation of biomarkers of aging for evaluating longevity interventions in humans.

  • Biomarker frameworks. First author of the Biomarkers of Aging Consortium's definitions and evaluation criteria for aging biomarkers (Cell 2023) and of its framework for their validation (Nature Medicine 2024).
  • Benchmarking. Co-developed Biolearn, an open framework for evaluating aging biomarkers across cohorts, and a comparison of 14 epigenetic clocks against 174 incident diseases.
  • Mechanisms of epigenetic aging. Age-associated DNA methylation gain is concentrated at PRC2 (Polycomb) targets, and single-cell analyses indicate a substantial stochastic component.
  • Multi-omic and computational approaches. Plasma proteomic models estimate organ-specific aging, and AI agents screen public molecular profiles for interventions associated with lower biological age.

  1. 1
    No standardfor what counts as a reliable biomarker of aging

    The field lacked shared terms and agreed properties for aging biomarkers, which held back their use in trials. This consensus paper sets out terminology, types of biomarkers, clinical use cases and the validation steps needed.

    Moqri et al. 2023 · CellConsensus review, Biomarkers of Aging Consortium
  2. 2
    R = 0.12link between a clock's age accuracy and its death prediction

    Across clocks, how well a clock predicts calendar age says almost nothing about how well it predicts death (P = 0.67). The Horvath skin and blood clock was the most accurate for age (R² = 0.88), while GrimAge2 had the strongest link to death (hazard ratio 2.57).

    Ying et al. 2025 · Nat AgingStandardized evaluation of 39 biomarkers in more than 20,000 people (Biolearn)
  3. 3
    27 diseaseswhere a clock predicts the disease more strongly than death

    Second- and third-generation epigenetic clocks clearly beat first-generation clocks at predicting new disease over 10 years. For 27 diseases, including lung cancer and diabetes, the clock's link to the disease was stronger than its link to death, with the most promise for lung and liver conditions.

    Mavrommatis et al. 2025 · Nat CommunCohort study of 14 clocks and 174 diseases in 18,859 people, 10-year follow-up
  4. 4
    ~90%of the DNA methylation gained with age falls in PRC2 regions

    Low-methylated regions bound by the Polycomb complex PRC2 gain methylation with age in every dividing body cell type examined, accounting for about 90% of age-related methylation gain genome-wide. Their average, the PRC2-AgeIndex, is proposed as a universal marker of cellular aging that can separate the effects of anti-aging interventions.

    Moqri et al. 2024 · Nat CommunGenome-wide methylation analysis across human and mouse tissues and cells
  5. 5
    No consensuson how to validate aging biomarkers before clinical use

    Omic biomarkers of aging could serve as surrogate endpoints in trials, but there is no agreement on how to validate them. This review sets out the challenges of comparing them across studies and populations and recommends steps for systematic validation.

    Moqri et al. 2024 · Nat MedReview of validation studies in population cohorts
  6. 6
    50,000+people whose blood proteins were used to time each organ's aging

    Organ-specific aging models built from the plasma proteome predict disease in the matching organ, and suggest men age faster than women in most organs. Faster organ aging leads to disease in that organ, and diet, lifestyle, occupation and medications shift organ aging rates.

    Goeminne et al. 2025 · Cell MetabPlasma proteomics in the UK Biobank and other cohorts
  7. 7
    Adaptive immunityis where blood's multi-omic aging genes cluster

    Genes that change with age in both DNA methylation and gene expression in blood are enriched for adaptive immune functions. They replicate better across diverse populations and track aging outcomes more strongly than genes found with either data type alone, making them candidates for epigenetic editing.

    Moqri et al. 2026 · Nat CommunIntegrative analysis of high-resolution methylation and transcriptome data in blood
  8. 8
    Random + coordinatedtwo parts of epigenetic aging

    Epigenetic aging has a major random component, which behaves like radiocarbon decay, alongside co-regulated changes. Single-cell methylation and RNA data in mice support both, which helps explain what epigenetic clocks capture.

    Tarkhov et al. 2024 · Nat AgingTissue and single-cell methylation and RNA sequencing in mice
  9. 9
    DamAge and AdaptAgeclocks for harmful versus protective change

    Using Mendelian randomization, the study found DNA methylation sites likely to cause aging-related traits, which existing clocks do not concentrate on. Clocks built from them separate damage (DamAge, linked to death and poor outcomes) from adaptation (AdaptAge, linked to beneficial change), and respond to short-term interventions.

    Ying et al. 2024 · Nat AgingEpigenome-wide Mendelian randomization and clock development
  10. 10
    54 proteinsin a blood signature of brain amyloid that replicated

    Of 454 plasma proteins linked to amyloid load on PET, 54 replicated across cohorts. A score built from them tracked amyloid burden, other Alzheimer's biomarkers and clinical severity, complementing p-tau217 and amyloid PET.

    Chen et al. 2026 · Alzheimers DementPlasma proteomics (about 7,000 proteins) and amyloid PET in 1,429 people, two cohorts
  11. 11
    64%of Alzheimer's-linked blood proteins missed without APOE stratification

    Analyzing blood proteins and metabolites separately by APOE genotype found many Alzheimer's-linked proteins that unstratified studies miss, and 17% behaved differently by genotype. Mitochondrial dysfunction was tied to Alzheimer's regardless of APOE, and lipids were raised in cases even compared with controls of the same genotype.

    Li et al. 2026 · Adv Sci (Weinh)APOE-stratified multi-omic analysis in several large datasets
  12. 12
    About halfof the variation in cognitive decline explained by current biomarkers

    The 2025 Brain Aging Symposium at Harvard highlighted plasma p-tau217 as making large-scale Alzheimer's screening newly feasible, and combining omics, imaging, cognition and lifestyle for better prediction. Current biomarkers still explain only about half of the variation in cognitive decline.

    de Magalhães et al. 2026 · Aging CellMeeting report
  13. 13
    Minutes, not monthsfor expert-checked biomedical analyses by an AI co-scientist

    OpenScientist, an open-source AI co-scientist, completed analyses in minutes that would otherwise take months. In five case studies checked by domain experts, it measured Alzheimer's blood biomarker performance, built a survival model that held up in a hidden cohort, and matched an independent human analysis in a blinded comparison.

    Roberts et al. 2026 · Nat MedFive expert-evaluated case studies
  14. 14
    500+interventions found to lower biological age in public data

    AI agents reanalyzed 43,602 intervention-versus-control comparisons from public methylation and RNA data with more than 40 aging clocks. Over 500 interventions lowered biological age, but more interventions sped aging than slowed it, and disease states mostly sped it.

    Ying et al. 2025 · bioRxivReanalysis of millions of public human and mouse samples (preprint)
  15. 15
    Both directionsan ovary's molecular age moves with the age of its host

    Old mouse ovaries became molecularly younger in young hosts and young ovaries older in old hosts. Ovaries from 13-month-old donors supported live births at 17 to 18 months in young hosts but not old ones, and young ovaries lowered the transcriptomic age of aged liver and brain.

    Iqbal et al. 2026 · bioRxivHeterochronic ovary transplantation and parabiosis in mice (preprint)
  16. 16
    Partial reprogramminga leading route to cell rejuvenation

    Brief expression of nuclear reprogramming factors has countered age-related decline in mouse and human models at the level of cells, tissues and whole animals. The review covers each factor's role, chemical alternatives, chromatin changes and open problems for treating aging.

    Cipriano et al. 2024 · Nat AgingReview
  17. 17
    11–22% fewerdamaging gene variants in centenarians

    Centenarians carried a lower burden of rare loss-of-function mutations than controls, and so did their offspring. Of 35 genes depleted of such mutations, 14 were confirmed in the UK Biobank, and RGP1, PCNX2 and ANO9 showed consistent causal effects on aging traits.

    Ying et al. 2024 · Nat CommunWhole-exome sequencing in Ashkenazi Jewish longevity cohorts, with UK Biobank validation
  18. 18
    31,000patients' routine lab results behind OMICmAge

    EMRAge, a mortality-risk measure from routine clinical labs, was rebuilt from DNA methylation (DNAmEMRAge) and multi-omics (OMICmAge). Both were strongly linked to chronic disease and death, performing as well as or better than current biomarkers, and OMICmAge can be measured from DNA methylation alone.

    Chen et al. 2026 · Nat AgingMass General Brigham Biobank, validated in cohorts of 14,213 and 18,672 people
  19. 19
    6 of 6proteomic clocks showed lower biological age with treatment

    In a 12-week phase 2a trial of rentosertib for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, all six proteomic aging clocks showed lower biological age in treated arms. Clocks alone could not separate aging from disease effects, so pathway analysis was added, supporting trials that measure aging and disease together.

    Zhavoronkov et al. 2026 · Nat BiotechnolSecondary analysis of serum proteomes from a randomized phase 2a trial
  20. 20
    6 barriersto bringing aging biomarkers into clinical use

    A survey of experts identified six key barriers to clinical translation. Core recommendations are to link biomarkers to actionable clinical insight, make them affordable and widely available, and validate them as robust and responsive in individuals.

    Biomarkers of Aging Consortium et al. 2024 · Nat AgingExpert survey and recommendations, Biomarkers of Aging Consortium
  21. 21
    Noneof the core questions about aging got a majority answer

    A survey of aging researchers found no consensus on what aging is, what causes it, when it begins or what counts as rejuvenation. No question received a majority opinion, not even whether consensus is needed.

    Gladyshev et al. 2024 · PNAS NexusSurvey of researchers in the field
  22. 22
    15,000+methylation profiles across 17 human tissues

    Aging produces both shared and tissue-specific methylation changes, with rising variability and disorder across tissues. A modifiable gene cluster tied to NAD+ metabolism stood out as a possible treatment target, and PCDHGA1 emerged as a hub across tissues.

    Jacques et al. 2026 · Nat AgingMeta-analysis of human methylation data
  23. 23
    11,000+transcriptomes from four mammals reveal shared aging signatures

    Gene expression changes with aging and mortality were conserved across mouse, rat, macaque and human, including CDKN1A and LGALS3, whose protein levels also tracked death and multiple diseases in the UK Biobank. The resulting clocks predicted lifespan-changing interventions, time to death and rejuvenation.

    Tyshkovskiy et al. 2026 · NatureMulti-species transcriptome analysis across more than 25 tissues
  24. 24
    Some modules olderafter cell reprogramming, despite overall rejuvenation

    Ageome measures the epigenetic age of thousands of pathways at once rather than a single number. Reprogramming rejuvenated cells overall but sped the aging of some modules, and some modules predicted age-related disease, especially cancer, better than existing clocks.

    Ying et al. 2024 · bioRxivComputational framework applied to mouse and human data (preprint)
  25. 25
    ~4 yearserror of a skin clock read from face tape strips

    Skin biological age can be measured from the face with non-invasive tape stripping. Two new clocks predicted age within about 4 years, varied less than 2 years within a person, and captured rejuvenation from Yamanaka factor treatment.

    Menendez Vazquez et al. 2025 · NPJ AgingEnzymatic methyl-sequencing of epidermis from 462 people, with external validation
  26. 26
    Cell by cellepigenetic age from long-read sequencing without sorting

    Long-read sequencing can assign DNA fragments to their cell type of origin, allowing epigenetic age to be estimated separately for myeloid cells and lymphocytes from bulk blood, and from cell-free DNA.

    Eames et al. 2025 · Aging CellMethod development (LongReadAge)
  27. 27
    R = 0.929accuracy of a DNA methylation foundation model

    MethylGPT, a transformer trained on more than 150,000 methylation profiles, predicted methylation values well, tolerated up to 70% missing data, beat existing methods at age prediction and predicted 60 major conditions in Generation Scotland.

    Ying et al. 2024 · bioRxivFoundation model development (preprint)
  28. 28
    78%correct on diseases the health model never trained on

    HealthFlux, a model of health trained on data from 502,166 UK Biobank participants, predicted 1,256 diseases and death over 20 years, generally beating published models. It correctly ranked a future case above an unaffected person 78% of the time for diseases left out of training.

    Wang et al. 2026 · medRxivUK Biobank with four independent US cohorts (preprint)
  29. 29
    5 criteriafor judging immune aging biomarkers in trials

    There is no consensus on measuring immune aging in trials. Against five evaluation criteria, measures of multidimensional immune function, inflammaging scores and functional assays performed best.

    Cipriano et al. 2026 · Nat MedReview and evaluation framework
