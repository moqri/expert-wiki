Aging clocks

Models that estimate biological age, or the pace of aging, from molecular or clinical measurements.

Overview

An aging clock is a predictive model trained on biological data, most often DNA methylation, to estimate age or age-related outcomes. The difference between predicted and chronological age ("age acceleration") is studied as a marker of biological aging and of response to interventions.

Generations of clocks

TypeTrained to predictExamples
First generationChronological ageHorvath multi-tissue clock (2013), Hannum blood clock (2013)
Second generationPhenotypic age, mortality, morbidityPhenoAge (2018), GrimAge (2019)
Pace of agingRate of physiological declineDunedinPACE (2022)

Data types

Clocks have been built from DNA methylation, transcriptomics, proteomics, metabolomics, clinical blood chemistry, imaging, and more, at both whole-organism and organ- or cell-type-specific levels.
