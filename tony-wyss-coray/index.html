Ask about Tony Wyss-Coray's research

Answers are drawn only from Tony Wyss-Coray's published papers listed on this page, with each point linked to its paper. They describe what the papers report, not personal views or advice from Tony Wyss-Coray. This is not medical advice.

Try

Systemic regulation of brain aging and plasma proteomic measures of organ aging and Alzheimer's disease.

  • Systemic factors in brain aging. Showed that aged blood impairs neurogenesis and cognition in mice (Nature 2011) and that young blood reverses age-related impairments in synaptic plasticity and cognition (Nature Medicine 2014).
  • Rejuvenating factors. Identified plasma and cerebrospinal fluid factors that improve brain function in aged mice, including TIMP2 in human umbilical cord plasma (Nature 2017) and Fgf17 in young cerebrospinal fluid (Nature 2022).
  • Plasma proteome and organ aging. Described nonlinear changes in the plasma proteome across the lifespan (Nature Medicine 2019) and developed organ-specific aging estimates from plasma proteins associated with disease risk (Nature 2023).
  • Microglia in aging. Identified lipid-droplet-accumulating microglia as a dysfunctional state in the aging brain (Nature Neuroscience 2020) and showed that CD22 blockade restores microglial phagocytosis in aged mice (Nature 2019).

  1. 1
    3.1× Alzheimer's riskfor people whose blood proteins show an especially old brain, similar to carrying one APOE4

    Using 2,916 blood proteins from 44,498 UK Biobank participants to estimate the age of 11 organs, an especially aged brain raised the risk of Alzheimer's disease about as much as one copy of APOE4, while a youthful brain protected about as much as two copies of APOE2, regardless of APOE genotype. Organ ages also responded to lifestyle and medicines, and each extra aged organ raised the risk of death, up to 8.3 times for eight or more.

    Oh et al. 2025 · Nat MedPlasma proteomics in 44,498 UK Biobank participants with up to 17 years of follow-up
  2. 2
    ~1 in 5 adultshave one organ aging much faster than the rest of the body

    Blood proteins that come from specific organs were used to estimate the age of 11 organs in 5,676 adults across five cohorts. Nearly 20% showed strongly accelerated aging in one organ and 1.7% in several. Accelerated organ aging carried 20–50% higher risk of death, faster heart aging carried 250% higher heart failure risk, and faster brain and vascular aging predicted Alzheimer's progression as well as the blood test pTau-181.

    Oh et al. 2023 · NaturePlasma proteomics and machine learning in 5 cohorts, 5,676 adults
  3. 3
    Young bloodreversed memory loss and synapse decline in old mice

    Connecting the circulation of old mice to young mice, or simply injecting young mouse plasma into old mice, increased connections between neurons in the hippocampus, improved synaptic plasticity and improved learning and memory. The effect worked in part through the memory-related switch Creb.

    Villeda et al. 2014 · Nat MedMouse parabiosis and plasma infusion studies
  4. 4
    Old bloodreduced new neuron growth and memory in young mice, partly through CCL11

    Exposing young mice to the circulation or plasma of old mice reduced the birth of new neurons and impaired learning and memory. Levels of the immune signal CCL11 (eotaxin) rose in the blood and spinal fluid of healthy aging people, and raising CCL11 in young mice reduced new neurons and impaired memory.

    Villeda et al. 2011 · NatureMouse parabiosis, plasma transfer and injection studies, plus human blood and spinal fluid levels
  5. 5
    18 patientswith Alzheimer's disease received young plasma infusions safely

    In the PLASMA trial at Stanford, 18 people with mild to moderate Alzheimer's dementia received four weekly infusions of plasma from donors aged 18 to 30. There were no treatment-related serious side effects and attendance was high. The trial was small and designed to test safety, not benefit; larger placebo-controlled trials are needed.

    Sha et al. 2019 · JAMA NeurolRandomized crossover plus open-label safety trial, 18 patients
  6. 6
    3× Alzheimer's riskwith extremely aged astrocytes in people carrying two copies of APOE4

    Models built from more than 7,000 blood proteins in 60,542 people estimated the biological age of over 40 cell types. About 20–25% of people had one cell type aging fast. APOE4 carriers had older astrocytes, and in people with two APOE4 copies, extreme astrocyte aging tripled the risk of Alzheimer's disease while youthful astrocytes lowered it.

    Ding et al. 2026 · Nat MedPlasma proteomics in 60,542 individuals with 15 years of follow-up
  7. 7
    TIMP2a protein rich in umbilical cord blood, improved memory in old mice

    Treating old mice with human umbilical cord plasma revitalized the hippocampus and improved memory. The protein TIMP2, enriched in cord plasma and young blood, entered the brain and improved synaptic plasticity and memory in old mice.

    Castellano et al. 2017 · NatureMouse studies with human cord plasma and single-protein treatment
  8. 8
    Clusterincarried in exercising mice's blood, calmed brain inflammation

    Plasma from mice that ran voluntarily, given to sedentary mice, lowered inflammation genes in the brain. The exercise plasma was rich in clusterin, which bound brain blood vessel cells and reduced brain inflammation in mouse models, including an Alzheimer's model. People with cognitive impairment who exercised for 6 months had higher clusterin in their blood.

    De Miguel et al. 2021 · NatureMouse plasma transfer studies plus a 6-month human exercise study
  9. 9
    Young spinal fluidimproved memory in old mice, through the growth factor Fgf17

    Infusing young cerebrospinal fluid directly into old mouse brains improved memory, mainly by boosting the cells that make myelin, the insulation around nerve fibres. The growth factor Fgf17 was enough to restore this and improve long-term memory in old mice, while blocking it impaired memory in young mice.

    Iram et al. 2022 · NatureMouse spinal fluid infusion, gene expression and cell studies
  10. 10
    APOE4/4brains with Alzheimer's have the most fat-laden, damaging microglia

    In Alzheimer's brain tissue, a microglia state marked by the fat-storage enzyme ACSL1 was most common in people with two copies of APOE4. In human stem cell microglia, amyloid triggered fat droplet buildup depending on APOE, and fluid from these microglia caused tau changes and harmed neurons.

    Haney et al. 2024 · NatureSingle-nucleus RNA sequencing of human brains plus human stem cell microglia
  11. 11
    3 wavesof change in blood proteins across life, in the 30s, 60s and 70s

    Measuring 2,925 blood proteins in 4,263 people aged 18 to 95 showed that the blood proteome does not change steadily with age. Instead it shifts in waves in the fourth, seventh and eighth decades of life, each wave reflecting different biological pathways and links to age-related diseases.

    Lehallier et al. 2019 · Nat MedPlasma proteomics in 4,263 people aged 18–95
  12. 12
    27% more explainedof thinking problems by a spinal fluid synapse protein ratio, beyond amyloid and tau

    Spinal fluid proteomics in 3,397 people from six Alzheimer's cohorts found that synapse proteins tracked cognitive impairment independent of amyloid and tau. The YWHAG:NPTX2 ratio explained 27% more of the variation beyond the standard pTau181:Aβ42 test, rose 20 years before expected symptoms in inherited Alzheimer's, and predicted progression to impairment and dementia over 15 years.

    Oh et al. 2025 · Nat MedCSF proteomics in 3,397 individuals from six cohorts, with plasma follow-up in 13,401 samples
  13. 13
    CD22blocking it restored clean-up by old microglia and improved memory in mice

    Genetic screens found that CD22 is raised on aged microglia and stops them engulfing debris. Blocking CD22 helped clear myelin debris, amyloid-β and α-synuclein in mice, and long-term antibody treatment returned microglia to a healthier state and improved memory in old mice.

    Pluvinage et al. 2019 · NatureCRISPR screens and mouse antibody treatment
  14. 14
    Fat dropletsbuild up in microglia with age and make them inflamed and ineffective

    Microglia in aging mouse and human brains accumulated lipid droplets. These cells were poor at clearing debris, made damaging reactive oxygen and released inflammatory signals. Genes controlling droplet formation included progranulin (GRN), a cause of inherited neurodegeneration.

    Marschallinger et al. 2020 · Nat NeurosciMouse and human brain tissue, RNA sequencing and a CRISPR screen
  15. 15
    ~2× longerneuronal proteins last in old mouse brains, and many end up in microglia

    Tagging newly made neuronal proteins showed their average half-life about doubled between 4 and 24 months of age in mice. Of 1,726 proteins in the aged neuronal "aggregome", nearly half were degraded more slowly with age. Neuronal proteins, especially synapse proteins, piled up in aged microglia, which may contribute to synapse loss.

    Guldner et al. 2026 · NatureBioorthogonal protein labelling in young and old mice
  16. 16
    Epstein-Barr virusis targeted by T cells expanded in the spinal fluid in Alzheimer's

    People with Alzheimer's disease had more of a killer T cell type (CD8+ TEMRA) in the blood, and more of these cells went with worse cognition. Clonally expanded CD8 T cells were found in their spinal fluid, and their receptors recognized two Epstein-Barr virus proteins.

    Gate et al. 2020 · NatureMass cytometry and single-cell sequencing across three human cohorts
  17. 17
    ~90%accuracy for 18 blood signalling proteins in telling Alzheimer's from controls

    A panel of 18 signalling proteins in blood plasma classified blinded samples from people with Alzheimer's disease and controls with close to 90% accuracy, and identified people with mild cognitive impairment who went on to develop Alzheimer's 2 to 6 years later.

    Ray et al. 2007 · Nat MedBlood plasma protein arrays in Alzheimer's, MCI and control samples
  18. 18
    β2-microglobulinrises in aging blood and impairs memory in mice

    β2-microglobulin (B2M) is higher in the blood of older people and mice. Injecting B2M into young mice impaired memory and new neuron growth, while old mice lacking B2M were protected from age-related memory decline.

    Smith et al. 2015 · Nat MedMouse injection and knockout studies plus human blood levels
  19. 19
    VCAM1on brain blood vessels links aged blood to brain inflammation

    Blood vessel cells in the aged mouse hippocampus raised the adhesion protein VCAM1, and its shed form was higher in the blood of older people and mice. Blocking VCAM1 with an antibody, or removing it from brain vessels, protected young brains from old plasma and reduced microglial activation and memory deficits in old mice.

    Yousef et al. 2019 · Nat MedMouse plasma transfer, antibody and genetic studies plus human blood levels
  20. 20
    Less protein entryinto the old brain as the blood-brain barrier changes transport route

    Labelling hundreds of mouse plasma proteins showed that they normally cross into the healthy brain. With age this uptake fell as the barrier shifted from specific receptor transport to non-specific transport, alongside loss of pericytes. Blocking the enzyme ALPL increased brain uptake of transferrin, antibodies and plasma.

    Yang et al. 2020 · NatureLabelled plasma proteome tracing in young and old mice
  21. 21
    Sugar coatingof brain blood vessels breaks down with age and weakens the barrier

    The glycocalyx, a sugar-rich coat lining brain blood vessels, was disrupted in aging and neurodegenerative disease, especially its mucin-type proteins. These changes weakened the blood-brain barrier and in severe cases caused bleeding in mice. Restoring the coating with gene therapy improved barrier function, reduced inflammation and improved cognition in old mice.

    Shi et al. 2025 · NatureMouse vascular profiling and gene therapy studies
  22. 22
    Interferonresponse at the choroid plexus harms the aging brain

    The choroid plexus, which makes spinal fluid, showed a type I interferon response in aged mice and humans. Blocking this signal in old mouse brains partly restored cognitive function and new neuron growth.

    Baruch et al. 2014 · ScienceMulti-organ gene expression in aged mice and human brains, plus blocking studies
  23. 23
    848 proteinsrise in spinal fluid relative to blood with healthy aging

    Paired spinal fluid and blood samples from 2,171 older people showed that the spinal fluid to blood ratio of 848 proteins rose with age, including complement, clotting and inflammatory proteins. Higher ratios of some blood or vessel proteins, such as VEGFA, went with preserved cognition.

    Farinas et al. 2025 · Nat MedPaired CSF and plasma proteomics in 2,171 people across cohorts
  24. 24
    11,925 womenmenopause blood protein scores tracked cognitive aging and dementia risk

    Spontaneous menopause was marked by blood protein changes in inflammation, synapses, metabolism and Alzheimer's processes that tracked hormones more than age, plus faster organ aging including the brain. In four cohorts of older women, higher menopause protein scores went with cognitive aging and dementia risk.

    Wood Alexander et al. 2026 · Nat MedSerum and plasma proteomics in 80 staged women, 2,814 validation women and 11,925 older women
  25. 25
    CXCL16rises in spinal fluid in cognitive impairment, a possible T cell entry signal

    Single-cell sequencing of spinal fluid from 45 healthy people aged 54 to 82 and 14 with cognitive impairment showed immune changes with age. In impairment, clonal CD8 T cells raised the receptor CXCR6 and its partner CXCL16 was higher in spinal fluid, suggesting a route for T cells into the brain.

    Piehl et al. 2022 · CellSingle-cell RNA sequencing of CSF from 59 people
  26. 26
    3× fewer plaqueswhen astrocytes made a little more TGF-β1 in Alzheimer's mice

    A modest increase of TGF-β1 from astrocytes in Alzheimer's model mice cut amyloid plaques three-fold and hippocampal and cortical amyloid-β by half, with strongly activated microglia, though more amyloid collected in blood vessels. TGF-β1 also stimulated amyloid clearance by microglia in culture.

    Wyss-Coray et al. 2001 · Nat MedTransgenic mouse studies, human brain tissue and cell culture
  27. 27
    Beclin 1an autophagy protein, is low early in Alzheimer's and its loss raises amyloid in mice

    Beclin 1, a key protein for cellular recycling (autophagy), was reduced in affected brain regions early in Alzheimer's disease. Lowering it in Alzheimer's model mice increased amyloid-β and neurodegeneration, while adding beclin 1 back reduced amyloid pathology.

    Pickford et al. 2008 · J Clin InvestHuman brain tissue plus mouse genetic and gene therapy studies
