Key work

Development and evaluation of epigenetic and multi-omic biomarkers of aging, and computational discovery of aging interventions.

  • Causal epigenetic clocks. Used epigenome-wide Mendelian randomization to identify CpG sites potentially causal for aging traits and built DamAge and AdaptAge, clocks tracking detrimental and adaptive methylation changes.
  • Mechanisms of epigenetic aging. Contributed to studies showing that age-associated methylation gain concentrates at PRC2 target regions and that epigenetic aging has a substantial stochastic component.
  • Biomarker benchmarking. Led Biolearn, which evaluated 39 aging biomarkers across more than 20,000 individuals and found that chronological age accuracy does not predict mortality association, and co-authored a comparison of 14 epigenetic clocks.
  • AI and longevity genetics. Developed ClockBase Agent to reanalyze public intervention datasets with aging clocks, and found a lower burden of loss-of-function mutations in centenarians.

  1. 1
    DamAge vs AdaptAgetwo clocks that separate harmful from protective DNA methylation changes

    Using genetic data to find DNA methylation sites that plausibly cause changes in aging-related traits, this study built two clocks. DamAge tracks harmful changes and is linked to adverse outcomes including death, while AdaptAge tracks protective adaptations. Existing epigenetic clocks are not enriched for these causal sites.

    Ying et al. 2024 · Nat AgingEpigenome-wide Mendelian randomization and clock development in large human cohorts
  2. 2
    R = 0.12correlation between how well a clock predicts age and how well it predicts death

    Across 39 aging biomarkers tested the same way, a clock's accuracy at predicting chronological age did not track its ability to predict mortality. The Horvath skin and blood clock predicted age best (R² = 0.88), while GrimAge2 had the strongest link with death (hazard ratio 2.57) and healthspan.

    Ying et al. 2025 · Nat AgingStandardized evaluation of 39 biomarkers in over 20,000 people from multiple cohorts (Biolearn)
  3. 3
    11-22%fewer rare loss-of-function gene variants in centenarians than in controls

    Centenarians carried a significantly lower burden of rare gene-disrupting variants than controls, and their children showed a similar pattern. Gene-level analysis found 35 genes with depleted variants in centenarians, 14 confirmed in UK Biobank, pointing to RGP1, PCNX2 and ANO9 as longevity genes.

    Ying et al. 2024 · Nat CommunWhole-exome sequencing of Ashkenazi Jewish centenarians, offspring and controls, with UK Biobank replication
  4. 4
    500+interventions found to lower biological age when AI agents reanalyzed public data

    AI agents reanalyzed 43,602 intervention-versus-control comparisons with more than 40 aging clocks and found over 500 interventions that significantly lower biological age, many missed by the original studies. Far more interventions sped up aging than slowed it. The top candidate, ouabain, reduced frailty progression and neuroinflammation and improved heart function in aged mice.

    Ying et al. 2025 · bioRxivReanalysis of millions of public methylation and RNA-seq samples (ClockBase Agent), with mouse validation; preprint
  5. 5
    50,000+people whose blood proteins were used to estimate the age of individual organs

    Blood protein models estimated the biological age of separate organs and systems. Faster aging of an organ predicted disease in that organ, men aged faster than women in most organs, and diet, lifestyle, occupation and medications were linked to organ aging rates.

    Goeminne et al. 2025 · Cell MetabPlasma proteomics in over 50,000 UK Biobank participants and other cohorts
  6. 6
    27 diseaseswhere an epigenetic clock predicted the disease more strongly than it predicted death

    Among 14 widely used epigenetic clocks followed for 10 years, second- and third-generation clocks clearly outperformed first-generation ones in predicting 174 diseases. For 27 diseases, including lung cancer and diabetes, the clock's link was stronger than its link with overall mortality, especially for respiratory and liver conditions.

    Mavrommatis et al. 2025 · Nat CommunCohort study of 18,859 people (Generation Scotland), 10-year follow-up
  7. 7
    No consensuson how biomarkers of aging should be validated before clinical use

    Biomarkers of aging could predict aging-related outcomes and serve as surrogate endpoints in trials, but there is no agreed way to validate them. The review sets out recommendations for testing their predictive validity in population studies so they can be used in gerotherapeutic trials.

    Moqri et al. 2024 · Nat MedConsensus review by the Biomarkers of Aging Consortium
  8. 8
    ~90%of age-related DNA methylation gain falls in Polycomb (PRC2) target regions

    Regions bound by Polycomb Repressive Complex 2 gain methylation with age in every somatic dividing cell type examined, accounting for about 90% of age-related methylation gain genome-wide. Their average methylation, the PRC2-AgeIndex, is proposed as a universal aging biomarker that can distinguish anti-aging interventions.

    Moqri et al. 2024 · Nat CommunGenome-wide methylation analysis across somatic tissues and cell types
  9. 9
    Stochastica large part of epigenetic aging is random drift

    Comparing tissue and single-cell DNA methylation in mice across development and adult aging showed that epigenetic aging combines coordinated changes with a major random component, which can be modeled like radiocarbon decay. This helps explain what epigenetic clocks measure.

    Tarkhov et al. 2024 · Nat AgingTissue and single-cell methylation and RNA sequencing in mice, with modeling
  10. 10
    11,000+transcriptomes from 4 mammals reveal shared signatures of aging and mortality

    Gene activity changes with aging and mortality risk were conserved across mouse, rat, macaque and human and across cell types, including CDKN1A and LGALS3, whose protein levels were linked to death and multimorbidity in UK Biobank. Chronic disease mainly aged inflammatory modules, while calorie restriction acted on mitochondrial and metabolic modules.

    Tyshkovskiy et al. 2026 · NatureIntegration of over 11,000 transcriptomes from more than 25 tissues in 4 species
  11. 11
    OR 0.31lower odds of COVID-19 infection per 10 extra years of genetically predicted life

    Genetic variation that supports a longer life was linked to lower risk of COVID-19 infection (odds ratio 0.31) and hospitalization (0.46) per additional 10 years of life. Faster biological aging was associated with later COVID-19 incidence and severity, with Notch signaling and B cells implicated in both.

    Ying et al. 2021 · Commun Med (Lond)Mendelian randomization and aging clock analysis in UK Biobank
  12. 12
    226,555methylation profiles used to train MethylGPT, a foundation model of the methylome

    MethylGPT, a transformer model trained on 226,555 human methylation profiles, predicted methylation values accurately (R = 0.929), kept working with up to 70% missing data, and predicted age better than existing methods. Fine-tuned on 18,859 people, it predicted mortality and 60 major diseases.

    Ying et al. 2024 · bioRxivFoundation model trained on 5,281 public datasets; preprint
  13. 13
    No majorityon any core question about aging among surveyed aging researchers

    A survey of aging researchers found broad disagreement on basic questions such as what aging is, what causes it, when it begins and what counts as rejuvenation. No question received a majority answer, which affects which experiments the field prioritizes.

    Gladyshev et al. 2024 · PNAS NexusSurvey of researchers in the aging field
  14. 14
    Both waysovarian molecular age shifts toward the age of the host

    When ovaries were transplanted between young and old mice, old ovaries became molecularly younger in young hosts and young ovaries became older in old hosts. Ovaries from 13-month-old donors supported live births at 17-18 months in young hosts but not in old hosts, and young ovaries also lowered the molecular age of the uterus, liver and brain.

    Iqbal et al. 2026 · bioRxivHeterochronic ovary transplantation and parabiosis in mice with aging clocks; preprint
  15. 15
    Ageomebiological age measured separately for thousands of molecular pathways

    Instead of one biological age, Ageome estimates the epigenetic age of thousands of pathways in mice and humans. It showed that cell reprogramming rejuvenates cells overall but accelerates aging of some modules, and some modules predicted age-related disease, especially cancer, better than existing clocks.

    Ying et al. 2024 · bioRxivComputational framework applied to mouse interventions and human cohorts; preprint
  16. 16
    848protein ratios between spinal fluid and blood that rise with age

    In paired spinal fluid and blood samples, the spinal fluid to plasma ratio of 848 proteins rose with age in healthy people, pointing to changes in the brain barrier system. Higher ratios of some blood-derived proteins, including DCUN1D1, MFGE8 and VEGFA, were linked to preserved cognition.

    Farinas et al. 2025 · Nat MedSomaScan proteomics of paired CSF and plasma from 2,171 older people, with genome-wide association
  17. 17
    Up to 100xcheaper DNA methylation measurement for epigenetic clocks with TIME-seq

    TIME-seq, a highly multiplexed sequencing method, built accurate mouse epigenetic clocks across eight tissue and cell types at up to 100-fold lower cost than conventional methods. A human blood clock built the same way reached R above 0.96 with a median error of 3.39 years.

    Griffin et al. 2024 · Nat AgingOver 1,800 mouse DNA samples and 1,056 human blood samples
  18. 18
    6 of 6proteomic aging clocks showed lower biological age with treatment in a drug trial

    In a 12-week phase 2a trial of the anti-fibrotic drug rentosertib for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, all six proteomic clocks predicted lower biological age in treated arms. Clocks alone could not separate aging from disease effects, but pathway analysis suggested shifts in senescence and metabolism, supporting trials that measure aging alongside disease outcomes.

    Zhavoronkov et al. 2026 · Nat BiotechnolReanalysis of serum proteomes from a phase 2a randomized trial
  19. 19
    Reversibleold fat tissue becomes molecularly younger in a young body

    When subcutaneous white fat was transplanted between young and old mice, the molecular age of grafts shifted toward the host. Old fat in a young body showed lower epigenetic and transcriptomic age, smaller lipid droplets and activation of heat-producing pathways.

    Moldakozhayev et al. 2025 · bioRxivLongitudinal heterochronic fat transplantation in mice; preprint
  20. 20
    Splicingerrors in RNA splicing rise with age and fall with calorie restriction or rapamycin

    Across mouse and human tissues, aging brings a genome-wide loss of fidelity in RNA splicing that alters the proteins made. This splicing degeneration rose with age and was reduced by calorie restriction or rapamycin, suggesting a new molecular hallmark of aging.

    Zhang et al. 2026 · bioRxivIntegrative analysis of mouse and human transcriptome data; preprint
  21. 21
    Multi-omicaging genes in blood replicate better when methylation and gene activity agree

    Genes that change with age in both DNA methylation and gene activity in blood were enriched for adaptive immune functions, replicated more robustly across diverse populations and were more strongly tied to aging-related outcomes than genes found with either data type alone.

    Moqri et al. 2026 · Nat CommunIntegrative epigenetic and transcriptomic analysis of human blood across populations
  22. 22
    OR 1.63higher odds of severe COVID-19 with genetically higher blood ACE2

    The largest genetic study of blood ACE2, the coronavirus receptor, found 10 genetic loci explaining 30% of its heritability. Genetically higher ACE2 levels were linked to more severe COVID-19 (odds ratio 1.63), hospitalization and infection.

    Yang et al. 2022 · CirculationGenome-wide association meta-analysis in over 28,000 people (SCALLOP Consortium)
  23. 23
    Minutesfor analyses an open AI co-scientist completed that would take months by hand

    OpenScientist, an open-source agentic AI system, completed data analyses in minutes across five biomedical case studies reviewed by experts, including quantifying Alzheimer's blood biomarker performance and building a plasma protein survival model that held up in a hidden cohort. It can run fully offline for sensitive clinical data.

    Roberts et al. 2026 · Nat MedExpert evaluation across five biomedical case studies
  24. 24
    Clonal B cellsan age-associated B cell population drives lymphoma in old mice

    Mouse B cell lymphoma arose from an age-associated clonal B cell population driven by c-Myc activation, DNA hypermethylation and mutations, which looked biologically older and caused malignancy when moved into young mice. Blocking mTOR or c-Myc in old mice reduced the pre-cancerous changes.

    Castro et al. 2024 · Nat AgingExperimental study in naturally aged mice with epigenetic comparison to human lymphoma
  25. 25
    Acarbosea longevity drug also eases a severe mitochondrial disease in mice

    Acarbose, a drug that extends lifespan in normal mice, suppressed symptoms and improved survival in a mouse model of Leigh syndrome, a severe mitochondrial disease. It acted independently of mTOR, added to the benefit of rapamycin, and remodeled the gut microbiome.

    Bitto et al. 2023 · Nat MetabExperimental study in Ndufs4 knockout mice
  26. 26
    90% of peakthreshold that defines Peakspan, the years of near-peak function

    Peakspan is defined as the age range in which a person keeps at least 90% of peak performance in a given body system or cognitive domain. Most systems peak in the 20s or 30s, so people spend most of adult life healthy but functionally declined.

    Zhavoronkov et al. 2026 · Aging DisMulti-system analysis of published functional data and conceptual framework
