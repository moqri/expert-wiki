How simple blood and spinal fluid tests can detect Alzheimer's disease and related brain diseases early and accurately, years before dementia.

Oskar Hansson's work centers on measuring the brain changes of Alzheimer's disease in living people. Through the Swedish BioFINDER studies, Hansson's group showed that spinal fluid tests for amyloid and tau could predict which people with mild memory problems would go on to Alzheimer's dementia, and that amyloid in spinal fluid is already fully abnormal 5 to 10 years before dementia, earlier than it shows on PET scans.

The best-known thread is the blood test for p-tau217, a form of the tau protein. Hansson's studies found that plasma p-tau217 told Alzheimer's apart from other brain diseases about as well as spinal fluid or PET, rose about 20 years before symptoms in people with an inherited form of the disease, and kept rising as the disease advanced. In about 1,200 patients seen in Swedish primary and specialist care, a p-tau217 blood test was right about 90% of the time, compared with 61% for primary care doctors and 73% for dementia specialists using standard workups.

A second thread maps how the disease spreads. Amyloid first builds up in the brain's default mode network, tau then follows connected brain pathways, and tau PET revealed four distinct spreading patterns rather than one typical course. People without symptoms who already have both amyloid and tau on PET face a much higher risk of developing memory problems.

Hansson's work also covers diseases that mimic or coexist with Alzheimer's. A spinal fluid test for misfolded α-synuclein showed that Lewy body disease is common in memory clinic patients, often alongside Alzheimer's changes, and speeds decline. Blood neurofilament light helps separate Parkinson's disease from atypical parkinsonian disorders. These biomarkers are now part of revised diagnostic criteria and of trials of amyloid-lowering drugs such as donanemab.

    AUC 0.96for a p-tau217 blood test separating Alzheimer's from other brain diseases

    Plasma p-tau217 distinguished Alzheimer's dementia from other neurodegenerative diseases with an area under the curve of 0.96, better than p-tau181, neurofilament light and MRI and on par with spinal fluid tests and tau PET. In 81 people with autopsy results it separated Alzheimer's from non-Alzheimer's with an AUC of 0.89.

    Palmqvist et al. 2020 · JAMACross-sectional study of 3 cohorts (autopsy, BioFINDER-2, Colombian PSEN1 kindred), 1,402 participants

    91% vs 61%accuracy of a blood test vs primary care doctors

    In 1,213 Swedish patients evaluated for memory symptoms, a blood test based on p-tau217 identified Alzheimer's with about 90% accuracy. Primary care doctors using exams, cognitive tests and CT scans were right 61% of the time, and dementia specialists 73%.

    Palmqvist et al. 2024 · JAMAProspective diagnostic study in primary and secondary care, 1,213 patients
  3. 3
    ~20 yearsbefore symptoms that blood p-tau217 rises in inherited Alzheimer's

    In a Colombian family carrying the PSEN1 E280A mutation, plasma p-tau217 was higher in mutation carriers than non-carriers from about age 25, roughly 20 years before the expected onset of mild cognitive impairment.

    Palmqvist et al. 2020 · JAMAColombian autosomal-dominant kindred, 365 carriers and 257 non-carriers
  4. 4
    Equal to CSFa blood test matched FDA-approved spinal fluid tests

    Plasma %p-tau217 measured by mass spectrometry detected amyloid plaques on PET as well as FDA-approved spinal fluid tests (AUC 0.95 to 0.97) and detected tau tangles better. In people with cognitive impairment it was 89 to 90% accurate for amyloid, rising to 95% when an intermediate zone was set aside for further testing.

    Barthélemy et al. 2024 · Nat MedDiagnostic study, BioFINDER-2 (1,422) and Knight ADRC (337) cohorts
  5. 5
    HR 17.7higher risk of Alzheimer's with abnormal spinal fluid tau and amyloid

    Among 137 people with mild cognitive impairment followed for 4 to 6 years, abnormal spinal fluid total tau and Aβ42 at the start identified those who developed Alzheimer's with 95% sensitivity and 83% specificity, a 17.7-fold higher risk, far stronger than age, sex, education or APOE.

    Hansson et al. 2006 · Lancet NeurolProspective clinical cohort, 137 MCI patients and 39 controls
  6. 6
    5 to 10 yearsbefore dementia that spinal fluid amyloid is fully abnormal

    Following people with mild cognitive impairment for a median of 9.2 years, spinal fluid Aβ42 was just as low in those who developed Alzheimer's 5 to 10 years later as in those who developed it within 5 years, while tau rose closer to onset. About 90% of people with abnormal spinal fluid markers developed Alzheimer's within 9 to 10 years.

    Buchhave et al. 2012 · Arch Gen PsychiatryClinical cohort, 137 MCI patients, median 9.2-year follow-up
  7. 7
    Fluid before PETspinal fluid detects amyloid build-up earlier than PET

    People with abnormal spinal fluid Aβ42 but a normal amyloid PET scan accumulated amyloid at 1.2% a year, the same rate as people abnormal on both and more than three times faster than people normal on both, showing that spinal fluid changes first.

    Palmqvist et al. 2016 · BrainLongitudinal ADNI cohort, 437 non-demented participants, repeated PET
  8. 8
    Default mode networkwhere amyloid first builds up

    The earliest amyloid build-up appeared in the precuneus, medial orbitofrontal and posterior cingulate cortex, core parts of the brain's default mode network, and was already linked to weaker connectivity there before any shrinkage or drop in brain metabolism.

    Palmqvist et al. 2017 · Nat CommunAmyloid PET and CSF in ADNI and BioFINDER cohorts
  9. 9
    4 patternsof tau spread across the brain

    Tau PET scans of 1,612 people revealed four distinct patterns of tau spread, each seen in 18 to 33% of people, with different ages, cognitive profiles and rates of decline, suggesting that a single "typical" Alzheimer's course does not fit everyone.

    Vogel et al. 2021 · Nat MedTau PET data-driven subtyping, 1,612 participants, replicated with a second tracer
  10. 10
    Up to 70%of the tau pattern explained by brain connections

    A model in which tau spreads between connected neurons explained up to 70% of the spatial pattern of tau on PET in 312 people. Regions with more amyloid had more tau than the model predicted, suggesting amyloid speeds up the spread.

    Vogel et al. 2020 · Nat CommunEpidemic spreading model compared with tau PET, 312 participants
  11. 11
    HR 19.2risk of memory problems with amyloid and tau but no symptoms

    Among 1,325 cognitively unimpaired people, those with amyloid plus tau in the temporal neocortex on PET had a 19.2-fold higher risk of progressing to mild cognitive impairment than people with neither, compared with 2.4-fold for amyloid alone.

    Ossenkoppele et al. 2022 · Nat MedMulticenter longitudinal amyloid and tau PET study, 1,325 participants
  12. 12
    Tau follows amyloidsoluble tau rises before tau tangles show on PET

    In people without symptoms who had amyloid, spinal fluid p-tau181, p-tau217 and total tau rose before amyloid PET turned positive, while tau PET began rising only afterward, suggesting amyloid triggers tau release years before tangles form.

    Mattsson-Carlgren et al. 2020 · Sci AdvLongitudinal CSF and PET in humans, plus MAPT mutation carriers and 5xFAD mice
  13. 13
    Rises over timeblood p-tau217 tracks disease progression

    Repeated blood tests over up to 6 years in 250 people showed plasma p-tau217 increasing in people with amyloid, both with and without symptoms, but not in amyloid-negative people. The rise matched worsening memory and brain shrinkage.

    Mattsson-Carlgren et al. 2020 · BrainLongitudinal BioFINDER cohort, 250 participants, median 3 samples each
  14. 14
    Only p-tau217changed over time among blood markers

    Over 4 to 6 years, only plasma p-tau217 showed clear amyloid-linked increases tied to cognitive decline and atrophy. P-tau231 and the Aβ42/40 ratio changed earliest but stayed flat afterward, making them more suited to finding early disease than to tracking it.

    Ashton et al. 2022 · Nat MedLongitudinal BioFINDER-1, replicated in the Wisconsin Registry for Alzheimer's Prevention
  15. 15
    AUC 0.91for predicting Alzheimer's dementia within 4 years

    Combining plasma p-tau217 with brief memory and executive tests and APOE genotype predicted who would develop Alzheimer's dementia within 4 years with an AUC of 0.91. Memory clinic doctors' own predictions reached only 0.71.

    Palmqvist et al. 2021 · Nat MedPrognostic study, BioFINDER (340) and ADNI (543)
  16. 16
    Up to 86% fewerspinal fluid tests needed with a two-step blood workflow

    Using a blood p-tau217 model first, and sending only uncertain cases on to spinal fluid testing, classified amyloid status with 88 to 92% accuracy while cutting the number of spinal fluid tests by 61 to 86%.

    Brum et al. 2023 · Nat AgingTwo memory clinic cohorts (348 MCI patients), validated in TRIAD
  17. 17
    85–94%accuracy of an automated blood p-tau217 test across 5 cohorts

    A fully automated, commercially available p-tau217 blood test detected Alzheimer's pathology with 89 to 91% accuracy in specialist care and 85% in primary care, reaching 92 to 94% when intermediate results were set aside. Accuracy was not affected by kidney disease, diabetes, sex or APOE.

    Palmqvist et al. 2025 · Nat MedDiagnostic study, 1,767 patients in Sweden, Spain and Italy
  18. 18
    Early markerblood GFAP rises with amyloid, not tau

    Plasma GFAP, a marker of reactive astrocytes, was raised in all amyloid-positive groups and tracked amyloid PET even in cognitively normal people with normal amyloid levels, independent of tau, pointing to astrocyte activation early in the disease.

    Pereira et al. 2021 · BrainBioFINDER-2, 504 participants with amyloid and tau PET
  19. 19
    90–94%agreement of automated spinal fluid tests with amyloid PET

    Fully automated spinal fluid tau/Aβ42 ratios agreed with amyloid PET in about 90% of cases, with cutoffs that held up in an independent cohort, and predicted greater 2-year decline in mild cognitive impairment.

    Hansson et al. 2018 · Alzheimers DementDiagnostic study, BioFINDER (277) and ADNI (646)
  20. 20
    ~90%sensitivity and specificity of tau PET for Alzheimer's

    Tau PET with flortaucipir distinguished Alzheimer's dementia from other neurodegenerative diseases with about 90% sensitivity and 91% specificity, far better than MRI measures of brain volume (AUC 0.92 to 0.95 vs 0.63 to 0.75).

    Ossenkoppele et al. 2018 · JAMACross-sectional study at 3 dementia centers, 719 participants
  21. 21
    23%of memory clinic patients have Lewy body pathology

    A spinal fluid α-synuclein seed test found Lewy body pathology in 23% of 883 memory clinic patients, most of whom did not meet clinical criteria for Parkinson's or dementia with Lewy bodies. Nearly half also had Alzheimer's pathology, and Lewy pathology predicted faster decline and hallucinations.

    Quadalti et al. 2023 · Nat MedBioFINDER memory clinic cohort, 883 patients
  22. 22
    8%of people without symptoms carry Lewy body pathology

    Among 1,182 cognitively unimpaired people, 8% had Lewy body pathology on a spinal fluid seed test. It was linked to faster memory and cognitive decline and loss of smell, and only these people progressed to clinical Lewy body disease over 10 years.

    Palmqvist et al. 2023 · Nat MedBioFINDER cohort, 1,182 cognitively unimpaired participants
  23. 23
    AUC 0.91for a blood test separating Parkinson's from atypical parkinsonism

    Blood neurofilament light was higher in multiple system atrophy, progressive supranuclear palsy and corticobasal syndrome than in Parkinson's disease, separating them with an AUC of 0.91, and 0.81 even within 3 years of onset.

    Hansson et al. 2017 · NeurologyThree prospective cohorts (Lund, London, early disease), 504 participants
  24. 24
    Slower declinewith the amyloid-clearing drug donanemab

    In 1,736 people with early symptomatic Alzheimer's, the amyloid-clearing antibody donanemab slowed decline on the iADRS scale at 76 weeks (3.25 points less worsening in the low/medium tau group). Brain swelling or effusion (ARIA-E) occurred in 24% of treated participants.

    Sims et al. 2023 · JAMAPhase 3 randomized placebo-controlled trial (TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2), 1,736 participants
  25. 25
    No linkbetween midlife artery thickening and Alzheimer's dementia

    Over 20 years in 6,103 middle-aged Swedes, thicker carotid artery walls raised the risk of vascular dementia (HR 1.32) but not Alzheimer's dementia, and were linked to small vessel disease rather than Alzheimer's markers in spinal fluid.

    Gustavsson et al. 2020 · Ann NeurolPopulation cohort (Malmö Diet and Cancer Study), 6,103 participants, 20-year follow-up
