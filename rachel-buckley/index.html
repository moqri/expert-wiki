Sex differences, hormonal factors and early markers of preclinical Alzheimer's disease.

  • Sex differences in tau. Showed greater regional tau in clinically normal women than men, and a meta-analysis of longitudinal tau PET found faster tau accumulation in women with elevated amyloid.
  • Menopause and hormone therapy. Associated earlier menopause and late initiation of hormone therapy with higher tau PET signal in women (JAMA Neurology 2023).
  • Early cognitive markers. Contributed to characterizing subjective cognitive decline, and showed that amyloid and tau jointly predict cognitive decline and that daily digital testing detects amyloid-related memory changes.
  • Modifiable risk factors. Associated higher physical activity with slower tau accumulation and cognitive decline, and vascular risk with faster amyloid-related decline in clinically normal adults.

  1. 1
    Higher tauin women than men with no memory problems

    In clinically normal older adults from two cohorts (296 people), women had more tau in the entorhinal cortex, one of the first brain regions affected by Alzheimer's, than men, and the difference was tied to higher amyloid.

    Buckley et al. 2019 · JAMA NeurolCross-sectional PET study, 2 cohorts (HABS and ADNI), 296 participants
  2. 2
    Fastertau build-up in women once amyloid is high

    Pooling 6 longitudinal studies of 1,376 people, women with high amyloid accumulated tau faster than men in the inferior temporal, fusiform and lateral occipital regions. Among APOE4 carriers, women also built up inferior temporal tau faster.

    Coughlan et al. 2025 · JAMA NeurolMeta-analysis of 6 longitudinal tau-PET cohorts, 1,376 participants
  3. 3
    Aβ → tau → memorythe order of change over 7 years

    Following clinically normal older adults for up to 7 years, a rise in amyloid came first, was followed by a rise in tau, and tau rise was followed by memory decline. People with high amyloid whose tau rose fastest went on to mild cognitive impairment.

    Hanseeuw et al. 2019 · JAMA NeurolProspective cohort with repeated amyloid and tau PET, 60 participants, Harvard Aging Brain Study
  4. 4
    Both neededamyloid plus tau for rapid memory decline

    In 137 older adults, higher amyloid and higher tau were each linked to memory decline, but rapid decline was seen mainly in people who had high levels of both, suggesting both proteins are needed for memory loss in the earliest stage.

    Sperling et al. 2019 · Ann NeurolProspective PET cohort, 137 clinically normal participants
  5. 5
    Earlier menopauselinked to more tau in women with high amyloid

    Among 292 cognitively unimpaired people, women, women with earlier menopause, and hormone therapy users had more tau when amyloid was elevated. Starting hormone therapy more than 5 years after menopause was linked to more tau than starting early.

    Coughlan et al. 2023 · JAMA NeurolCross-sectional PET study, 292 participants, Wisconsin Registry for Alzheimer Prevention
  6. 6
    ~20 yearsearlier than thought, sex differences in tau appear by midlife

    In 328 cognitively normal Framingham participants with a mean age of 57, postmenopausal women had more tau than age-matched men, while premenopausal women did not differ from men, placing the divergence about 20 years earlier than previously reported.

    Buckley et al. 2022 · Ann NeurolCross-sectional PET study, 328 participants, Framingham Heart Study
  7. 7
    Over 70hormone therapy users showed faster tau build-up

    In 146 clinically normal women scanned over several years, about 14 years after starting hormone therapy, users older than 70 accumulated tau faster than non-users in Alzheimer's-vulnerable regions, and this was linked to cognitive decline. In women younger than 70 the association was negligible.

    Coughlan et al. 2025 · Sci AdvLongitudinal PET cohort, 146 women
  8. 8
    38%5-year risk of impairment with very high blood p-tau217

    In 2,684 cognitively unimpaired older adults from 6 cohorts, each step up in plasma p-tau217 raised the risk of progressing to cognitive impairment (hazard ratio 1.38 per SD). People with high or very high levels had a 24% or 38% absolute risk of progressing within 5 years.

    Buckley et al. 2026 · JAMAPooled longitudinal cohort study, 6 cohorts, 2,684 participants, median 5.4 years follow-up
  9. 9
    Faster declinein women with high amyloid

    Across three cohorts of 755 cognitively normal older adults, amyloid and APOE4 did not differ by sex, but women with higher amyloid declined faster than men, especially women who were both amyloid positive and APOE4 carriers.

    Buckley et al. 2018 · Alzheimers DementLongitudinal analysis of 3 cohorts (ADNI, AIBL, HABS), 755 participants, median 4 years
  10. 10
    Wider in womentau across the cortex, tied to faster decline

    Across 41 brain regions in 398 older adults, women showed higher tau signal than men in many cortical areas, not just the medial temporal lobe, and women with more tau in these regions declined faster than men.

    Buckley et al. 2020 · Ann NeurolCross-sectional PET with longitudinal cognition, 398 participants, HABS and ADNI
  11. 11
    Entorhinal tautracks with worry about one's memory

    Among 133 healthy older adults, people who reported more subjective cognitive decline had more tau in the entorhinal cortex, regardless of their amyloid level, and to a lesser extent more amyloid.

    Buckley et al. 2017 · JAMA NeurolCross-sectional PET study, 133 participants, Harvard Aging Brain Study
  12. 12
    HR 5.1faster progression with high self-reported memory decline

    In 58 cognitively normal older adults with high amyloid, those reporting more memory decline were about 5 times more likely to progress to mild cognitive impairment or Alzheimer's dementia, even though their test scores did not drop faster.

    Buckley et al. 2016 · Alzheimers DementLongitudinal cohort, 58 amyloid-positive participants, Australian AIBL study
  13. 13
    17.7dementia cases per 1,000 person-years with memory complaints

    Across 11 cohorts with 2,978 people reporting memory complaints and 1,391 controls, dementia incidence was 17.7 per 1,000 person-years with complaints versus 14.2 without. Risk was much higher when complaints brought people to a memory clinic than in the community.

    Slot et al. 2019 · Alzheimers DementMulticenter cohort study, 11 cohorts, 4,369 participants
  14. 14
    Most won't declinepeople with memory complaints

    A Lancet Neurology review of subjective cognitive decline found that it raises the risk of mild cognitive impairment and dementia, but most people with it will not show progressive decline, so evaluation should be tailored to the individual.

    Jessen et al. 2020 · Lancet NeurolReview
  15. 15
    ≥5×higher risk of symptoms with both amyloid and tau

    Across 4 cohorts of 580 cognitively unimpaired older adults, 33% to 83% of people positive for both amyloid and tau progressed to mild cognitive impairment within about 2 to 3 years, compared with under 20% in other groups.

    Strikwerda-Brown et al. 2022 · JAMA NeurolMulticohort longitudinal study, 4 cohorts, 580 participants
  16. 16
    Centiloid 15-18.5lowest amyloid level that predicts decline

    In 3 cohorts of clinically normal people, the lowest amyloid PET level that predicted future amyloid build-up and cognitive decline fell in a narrow range, 15 to 18.5 Centiloids.

    Farrell et al. 2021 · NeurologyLongitudinal study, 3 cohorts, 855 participants
  17. 17
    Day 4when smartphone learning tests show amyloid-related decline

    Older adults who took a 12-minute daily memory test on their own devices for a week showed no amyloid-related differences on standard clinic tests or on day 1, but amyloid-positive people learned less from day 4 on, and weaker learning predicted faster decline over a year.

    Papp et al. 2024 · Ann NeurolDigital cognitive testing study with amyloid PET, cognitively unimpaired older adults
  18. 18
    Synergyvascular risk and amyloid speed decline together

    In 223 clinically normal older adults followed for about 4 years, higher Framingham cardiovascular risk and higher amyloid each predicted faster cognitive decline, and together they acted synergistically.

    Rabin et al. 2018 · JAMA NeurolLongitudinal cohort, 223 participants, Harvard Aging Brain Study
  19. 19
    More tauwhen vascular risk and amyloid are both high

    In 152 clinically normal older adults, the combination of high cardiovascular risk and elevated amyloid was linked to more tau measured about 3 years later in the inferior temporal cortex.

    Rabin et al. 2019 · Ann NeurolLongitudinal PET study, 152 participants
  20. 20
    5,001-7,500daily steps where the benefit plateaus

    In cognitively unimpaired older adults with elevated amyloid, more pedometer-measured steps were linked to slower tau build-up and slower cognitive and functional decline, without lower amyloid. The benefit levelled off at a moderate 5,001 to 7,500 steps a day.

    Yau et al. 2025 · Nat MedLongitudinal cohort with pedometer step counts and amyloid and tau PET
  21. 21
    Women onlya resilience gene locus on chromosome 10

    Sex-specific genome-wide analyses of 5,024 people found that resilience to Alzheimer's pathology is 20-25% heritable and identified a locus on chromosome 10 linked to higher resilience in women but not in men.

    Eissman et al. 2022 · BrainSex-stratified genome-wide association study, 5,024 participants
  22. 22
    1.14×higher age-adjusted Alzheimer's death rate in women

    Among about 1.1 million deaths in Australia from 2006 to 2014, women's older age at death explained most of their higher dementia death rate, but an excess of Alzheimer's deaths in women (rate ratio 1.14) and of vascular dementia deaths in men remained.

    Buckley et al. 2019 · Am J EpidemiolPopulation death-record study, 1,104,684 deaths
  23. 23
    Lower tauin men with higher midlife SHBG

    In men, higher midlife levels of sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG) were linked to less tau later in life, mostly in non-APOE4 carriers. Testosterone itself was not linked to amyloid or tau.

    Buckley et al. 2025 · Ann NeurolLongitudinal cohort with midlife blood hormones and later PET
  24. 24
    10%of brain gene links to Alzheimer's differ by sex

    In brain tissue from 767 people, 10% of gene expression links to amyloid, tau and cognitive decline were sex-specific, mostly in women, and four X-chromosome genes behaved differently by sex.

    Seto et al. 2025 · Nat CommunPost-mortem brain transcriptomics, 767 decedents, 3 brain regions
  25. 25
    Earlier menopause × vascular risktogether predict lower cognition

    In 8,360 postmenopausal women in the Canadian Longitudinal Study on Aging, higher vascular risk predicted lower cognition 3 years later only in women with earlier menopause (ages 35-48), and more strongly than in age-matched men.

    Wood Alexander et al. 2024 · NeurologyLongitudinal population cohort, 8,360 women and 8,360 men
  26. 26
    Amyloid × moodrising depression predicts decline when amyloid is high

    In 276 cognitively unimpaired older adults followed up to 7 years, worsening depression symptoms were linked to cognitive decline in people with higher brain amyloid.

    Gatchel et al. 2019 · JAMA Netw OpenLongitudinal cohort, 276 participants, Harvard Aging Brain Study
