APOE4

The ε4 allele of the apolipoprotein E gene, the strongest common genetic risk factor for late-onset Alzheimer's disease.

Overview

APOE has three common alleles: ε2, ε3 and ε4. ε3 is the most common; ε4 raises Alzheimer's risk in a dose-dependent way, and ε2 is associated with lower risk. Carrying one ε4 copy raises risk several-fold, and carrying two (ε4/ε4) raises it substantially more.

APOE4 homozygotes

People with two copies (ε4/ε4) make up a small share of the population. Recent work has proposed that APOE4 homozygosity be considered a distinct genetic form of Alzheimer's disease, given the near-universal presence of Alzheimer's pathology in older homozygotes and earlier symptom onset.

Clinical relevance

  • Higher risk of ARIA (amyloid-related imaging abnormalities) with anti-amyloid antibody therapies, especially in ε4/ε4 carriers.
  • Blood biomarkers such as plasma p-tau217 are being studied for early detection in high-risk groups.
