Key work

Measurement of biological ageing, sarcopenia and malnutrition in older adults, and evaluation of longevity interventions.

  • Markers of biological age. In the Leiden studies, showed that handgrip strength predicts mortality in the oldest old (CMAJ 2010) and that the number of p16INK4a-positive cells in skin reflects biological age (Aging Cell 2012).
  • Sarcopenia. Meta-analyses associated sarcopenia with falls and fractures (J Cachexia Sarcopenia Muscle 2019) and with mortality (Gerontology 2022) in adults.
  • Frailty and malnutrition in hospital. Showed that frailty, sarcopenia and malnutrition frequently co-occur in hospitalised older adults (J Am Med Dir Assoc 2020), and compared malnutrition criteria in the RESORT geriatric rehabilitation cohort (Clinical Nutrition 2020).
  • Longevity interventions. Co-authored a framework for precision geromedicine (Cell 2025) and a randomized, placebo-controlled trial of nicotinamide mononucleotide supplementation in healthy middle-aged adults (GeroScience 2023).

  1. 1
    37% / 66% / 84%of hospitalised older adults have sarcopenia, (risk of) malnutrition, and physical (pre-)frailty

    In hospitalised older adults, frailty, sarcopenia and malnutrition are each common and frequently occur together. About half of patients have two and perhaps all three conditions, which supports standardised screening at admission.

    Ligthart-Melis et al. 2020 · J Am Med Dir AssocSystematic review and meta-analysis of 47 studies, 18,039 patients
  2. 2
    0 to 45%range of sarcopenia prevalence in the same cohort depending on which definition is used

    Applying seven published definitions of sarcopenia to the same middle-aged and older adults gave prevalence anywhere from 0% to 45%, and only one person met every definition. Without a shared definition, studies cannot be compared or used in clinical care.

    Bijlsma et al. 2013 · Age (Dordr)Cross-sectional study, 654 participants of the Leiden Longevity Study
  3. 3
    HR 1.35 to 2.04higher risk of death for people aged 85 to 89 with weaker grip

    Among people aged 85, lower handgrip strength, and a larger loss of grip strength over four years, predicted higher all-cause mortality over about 10 years. A simple grip test may help estimate mortality risk in the oldest old.

    Ling et al. 2010 · CMAJPopulation-based cohort (Leiden 85-plus Study), 555 participants, 9.5-year follow-up
  4. 4
    −0.09 SDlower GrimAge acceleration per 1 SD more physical activity

    People who are more physically active tend to have a lower biological age on DNA methylation clocks, with significant links for the Horvath and GrimAge clocks but not Hannum or PhenoAge. Most evidence is cross-sectional, so cause and effect is not yet shown.

    Shan et al. 2026 · Lancet Healthy LongevSystematic review of 44 studies (145,465 participants), meta-analysis of 7
  5. 5
    600 mg/dayNMN dose with the best NAD and walking results

    In healthy middle-aged adults, 60 days of NMN raised blood NAD at every dose, was safe up to 900 mg a day, and improved six-minute walking distance versus placebo. Blood biological age rose in the placebo group but stayed unchanged with NMN.

    Yi et al. 2023 · GeroscienceRandomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, 80 adults, 60 days
  6. 6
    +28.6% to −100%deviation of NMN and urolithin A supplement content from the label

    Off-the-shelf NMN and urolithin A supplements bought online or in pharmacies often did not contain the amount stated on the label, with some containing none. Quality control of supplements sold as geroprotectors is limited.

    Sandalova et al. 2024 · GeroscienceLaboratory testing of commercially available supplements
  7. 7
    HR 0.541-year mortality for metformin users after geriatric rehabilitation

    Among older rehabilitation inpatients, those taking metformin, ACE inhibitors or ARBs, aspirin or beta-blockers had lower one-year mortality after discharge, and those taking three or more of these drugs had a 41% lower risk. Physical function and readmission did not differ. These are observational associations.

    Wang et al. 2026 · Mech Ageing DevObservational cohort (RESORT), 1,890 inpatients, mean age 83
  8. 8
    14biomarkers of aging agreed by experts for intervention studies

    An international Delphi panel agreed on 14 biomarkers of aging suitable as outcomes in intervention studies, including IGF-1, GDF-15, hs-CRP, IL-6, muscle mass and strength, grip strength, gait speed, frailty index, blood pressure and epigenetic clocks.

    Perri et al. 2025 · J Gerontol A Biol Sci Med SciThree-round Delphi consensus, up to 116 experts
  9. 9
    r = 0.97accuracy of a step-count-only aging clock for age

    MoveIt! Age estimates biological age from wearable step counts alone. It predicted mortality better than the blood-based PhenoAge in US survey data and was more closely linked to frailty, grip strength and function in rehabilitation patients than other clocks.

    Lu et al. 2026 · GeroscienceModel development in NHANES, validated in two cohorts (55 and 145 people)
  10. 10
    +2.8 yearshigher Horvath epigenetic age in people with clonal haematopoiesis (CHIP)

    People with clonal haematopoiesis of indeterminate potential had older epigenetic ages on all four clocks studied, larger clones were linked to older epigenetic age, and TET2 mutations showed stronger effects than DNMT3A.

    Simonson et al. 2026 · Ageing Res RevSystematic review of 5 studies (7,483 people), meta-analysis of 3
  11. 11
    HR 0.74mortality for 85-year-olds with a strong, unopposed inflammatory response

    At age 85, people whose immune cells mounted a high pro-inflammatory and low anti-inflammatory response survived longer over 10 years, while those with a weak response overall had higher mortality (HR 1.79).

    Wijsman et al. 2011 · J Gerontol A Biol Sci Med SciPopulation-based cohort (Leiden 85-plus Study), 562 participants, 10-year follow-up
  12. 12
    Fewersenescent (p16INK4a-positive) skin cells in people who are biologically younger

    Middle-aged people from long-lived families, who are biologically younger, had fewer senescent cells in their skin as marked by p16INK4a, linking cellular senescence to biological age in humans.

    Waaijer et al. 2012 · Aging CellSkin biopsy study within the Leiden Longevity Study
  13. 13
    1.4 yearsyounger-looking faces in men from long-lived families

    Offspring of long-lived siblings had less skin wrinkling on a sun-protected site, and men looked 1.4 years younger than controls. Women with the lowest cardiovascular risk looked more than 2 years younger for their age.

    Gunn et al. 2013 · J Gerontol A Biol Sci Med SciCross-sectional study, 261 offspring of nonagenarians and 253 controls
  14. 14
    4.8 vs 5.2 mmol/Lnight-time glucose in people from long-lived families vs controls

    Continuous glucose monitoring showed that glucose levels rise and become more variable with age, and that middle-aged people from long-lived families had lower glucose levels, especially at night, than people of the same age from the general population.

    Wijsman et al. 2013 · Aging CellContinuous glucose monitoring, 84 non-diabetic adults
  15. 15
    AbsentCMV-driven immune ageing in members of long-lived families

    In the general population, cytomegalovirus infection was linked to typical signs of immune ageing such as fewer naive T cells. These changes were not seen in offspring of long-lived families, who also had lower CRP and stronger T-cell responses to the virus.

    Derhovanessian et al. 2010 · J ImmunolComparison of 97 Leiden Longevity Study offspring with 97 partners
  16. 16
    Higher BPlinked to slower physical and cognitive decline after age 85

    In 85-year-olds, higher systolic blood pressure and pulse pressure were linked to less decline in daily activities and cognition over the following years, especially in people who were already disabled.

    Sabayan et al. 2012 · J Am Geriatr SocPopulation-based cohort (Leiden 85-plus Study), 572 participants
  17. 17
    1,406rehabilitation patients in whom sarcopenia predicted death within a year

    In geriatric rehabilitation inpatients, sarcopenia, low muscle mass and low muscle strength at admission were linked to higher mortality at 3 months and 1 year after discharge, which supports measuring muscle in routine care.

    Xu et al. 2021 · Age AgeingProspective cohort (RESORT), 1,406 inpatients, median age 83
  18. 18
    OR 2.07odds of severe sarcopenia among malnourished rehabilitation patients

    Half of older rehabilitation inpatients were malnourished and 19% had severe sarcopenia. Malnutrition was linked to severe sarcopenia but not to probable sarcopenia, and the two coexisted in 13% of patients.

    Verstraeten et al. 2021 · Clin NutrCohort (RESORT), 506 inpatients
  19. 19
    κ = 0.08agreement between grip strength and chair stand test for probable sarcopenia

    Grip strength and the chair stand test identified very different groups of patients as having probable sarcopenia, and only grip strength predicted institutionalisation and death. Grip strength should be used rather than the chair stand test in rehabilitation inpatients.

    Verstraeten et al. 2022 · Age AgeingCohort (RESORT), 1,250 inpatients
  20. 20
    HR 1.4higher post-discharge mortality per point on the Clinical Frailty Scale

    A higher Clinical Frailty Scale score at admission or discharge from geriatric rehabilitation predicted death within 3 months and 1 year, and patients whose frailty worsened during the stay had twice the 3-month risk.

    Soh et al. 2023 · Ann Phys Rehabil MedCohort (RESORT), 1,766 inpatients
  21. 21
    26 to 31%of people with heart disease, dementia, diabetes or lung disease have sarcopenia

    Sarcopenia is common in people with cardiovascular disease (31%), diabetes (31%), respiratory disease (27%) and dementia (26%), roughly two to three times the rate in people without these diseases.

    Pacifico et al. 2020 · Exp GerontolMeta-analysis of 63 studies, 17,206 patients and 22,375 controls
  22. 22
    277,921people whose grip strength data set the first pan-Asian reference values

    Pooled data from 20 national cohorts in 12 Asian countries produced age- and sex-specific reference values for grip strength, gait speed, chair stand, muscle mass and calf circumference, with clear differences between Asian regions.

    Grgic et al. 2026 · J Cachexia Sarcopenia MusclePooled individual participant data, 20 cohorts, 12 countries
  23. 23
    OR 2.80odds of severe white matter lesions on brain MRI in malnourished older adults

    Older outpatients who were malnourished or at risk of malnutrition, and those with lower vitamin B1 and B12, were more likely to have severe white matter lesions on MRI, but not more brain atrophy.

    de van der Schueren et al. 2016 · J Am Geriatr SocCross-sectional study, 359 geriatric outpatients
  24. 24
    0 to 14%malnutrition prevalence across four groups using the ESPEN definition

    Under the ESPEN definition, malnutrition ranged from 0% in healthy young people to 6% in geriatric outpatients and 14% in acutely ill middle-aged patients, while low fat-free mass was common in all groups.

    Rojer et al. 2016 · Clin NutrCross-sectional study, 969 people in four populations
  25. 25
    Mixedbenefits of multivitamins across 19 meta-analyses

    Multivitamin and mineral supplements improved some measures of memory and lowered blood pressure in some groups, but showed no benefit for overall mortality. Effects varied by population, which argues for targeted rather than blanket supplementation.

    Wang et al. 2026 · Ageing Res RevRapid review of 19 meta-analyses
  26. 26
    5 studiesof urolithin A in 250 healthy people

    Urolithin A taken for up to four months had a dose-dependent anti-inflammatory effect and increased muscle strength and endurance, but did not change physical function, body measures or cardiovascular outcomes. Side effects were mild or moderate.

    Kuerec et al. 2024 · Ageing Res RevSystematic review of 5 human studies, 250 participants
  27. 27
    41%of Singapore adults could correctly define healthspan

    In a national survey, 82% of Singapore adults correctly defined lifespan but only 41% defined healthspan, and 56% were interested in healthy longevity clinics. Interest was higher among younger, more educated and more active people.

    Amalaraj et al. 2026 · GeroscienceNationwide cross-sectional survey (HELO), 3,034 participants
  28. 28
    49%of Singapore adults report experiencing ageism

    Almost half of Singapore adults reported experiencing ageism, most often among the youngest and oldest age groups and those with lower education. Feeling older than one's actual age was one of the strongest predictors.

    Bruschinski et al. 2026 · GeroscienceNationwide cross-sectional survey (HELO), 3,034 participants
  29. 29
    1,218older adults randomised in Southeast Asia's first large multidomain dementia prevention trial

    The SINGER trial randomised 1,218 older Singaporeans at increased dementia risk to a structured or self-guided lifestyle programme. At baseline, inactivity, low cognitive engagement and poor diet were common.

    Yap et al. 2026 · Alzheimers DementRandomised controlled trial, baseline report
  30. 30
    Gerogenesand gerosuppressors as the basis of precision geromedicine

    Ageing can be seen as driven by gerogenes and slowed by gerosuppressors, much as cancer involves oncogenes and tumour suppressors. Measuring these pathways as biomarkers could allow treatments for ageing to be tailored to each person, pending clinical trials.

    Kroemer et al. 2025 · CellReview
