Alzheimer'sAging

Rudolph E. Tanzi, PhD

Massachusetts General Hospital and Harvard Medical SchoolGenetics and Aging Research Unit, McCance Center for Brain Health, Department of Neurology

Finds the genes behind Alzheimer's disease, works out what they do in the brain, and asks why the brain's own defenses may drive the disease.

Tanzi's work began with gene hunting in families. In the 1980s Tanzi's group helped place the Huntington's disease gene on chromosome 4, cloned the gene for the amyloid precursor protein (APP), whose fragment amyloid-β builds Alzheimer's plaques, and mapped it to chromosome 21, explaining why people with Down syndrome develop Alzheimer's brain changes. Tanzi's group also helped identify the Wilson disease copper transporter gene and the presenilin gene behind aggressive early-onset Alzheimer's.

For the common late-onset form, Tanzi's group built the AlzGene database, which pooled hundreds of conflicting genetic studies into one running meta-analysis, and carried out family-based genome-wide and whole-genome sequencing studies that found new risk genes, many of them acting on the brain's immune cells (microglia), on how amyloid-β is cut from APP, or on synapses. Follow-up studies showed how genes such as CD33, TREM2, ADAM10 and ATXN1 change amyloid build-up in mice and human brain tissue.

To study these genes in human cells, Tanzi's group grew human neurons in a three-dimensional culture and for the first time reproduced both amyloid plaques and tau tangles in a dish. Later versions added microglia, immune T cells and a blood-brain barrier, letting the team watch how immune cells entering the brain worsen damage.

Tanzi's group also proposed the antimicrobial protection hypothesis: amyloid-β, and more recently tau, behave like natural antimicrobial proteins that trap bacteria, fungi and herpes viruses, so infection may seed the plaques and tangles of Alzheimer's. Other work, relevant to brain aging generally, found that exercise protects memory in Alzheimer's mice by making new neurons and raising BDNF and the exercise hormone irisin.

  1. 1
    Chromosome 21carries the gene for the amyloid precursor protein, near the inherited Alzheimer's locus

    A cloned DNA copy showed that amyloid-β, the main protein in Alzheimer's plaques, is cut from a larger protein made in many tissues. Its gene sits on chromosome 21, close to the inherited Alzheimer's defect, which explains why it is overexpressed in the brains of fetuses with Down syndrome (three copies of chromosome 21).

    Tanzi et al. 1987 · ScienceGene cloning, mRNA mapping and genetic linkage in families
  2. 2
    Chromosome 21holds the defect causing inherited Alzheimer's in several families

    Genetic linkage to DNA markers placed the dominant gene defect in several families with inherited Alzheimer's disease on chromosome 21, offering an explanation for the Alzheimer's-like brain changes seen in Down syndrome.

    St George-Hyslop et al. 1987 · ScienceGenetic linkage study in families with dominantly inherited Alzheimer's disease
  3. 3
    8 microbeskilled by amyloid-β as potently as a known human antimicrobial peptide

    Amyloid-β killed eight common, clinically relevant microorganisms with potency equal to, and sometimes greater than, the classic human antimicrobial peptide LL-37. Alzheimer's brain tissue had more antimicrobial activity than age-matched normal brain, and removing amyloid-β with antibodies abolished the difference.

    Soscia et al. 2010 · PLoS OneLaboratory antimicrobial assays and post-mortem human brain extracts
  4. 4
    Plaques and tanglesreproduced together in human neurons in a dish for the first time

    Human neural stem cells carrying inherited Alzheimer's mutations and grown in three dimensions formed amyloid-β plaques and then tangles of aggregated, phosphorylated tau. Blocking amyloid-β production with β- or γ-secretase inhibitors reduced both, showing that amyloid can drive tau pathology in human cells.

    Choi et al. 2014 · Nature3D human neural stem cell culture model
  5. 5
    13 geneslinked to Alzheimer's risk by pooled meta-analysis, besides APOE4

    The AlzGene database collected every published Alzheimer's genetic association study and ran meta-analyses for each variant. Beyond APOE ε4, it pinpointed over a dozen candidate genes, including ACE, CST3, IDE, PSEN1 and TNF, with modest effects (odds ratios 1.11–1.38 for risk alleles and 0.67–0.92 for protective ones).

    Bertram et al. 2007 · Nat GenetSystematic meta-analyses of all published genetic association studies
  6. 6
    Seeded by herpesamyloid-β deposits form rapidly around herpes viruses and trap them

    Amyloid-β clumps bound the surface proteins of herpes simplex virus 1 and human herpesvirus 6, rapidly seeding amyloid deposits that trapped the virus and protected Alzheimer's mice and 3D human neural cultures from infection. This suggests herpes infection could directly drive amyloid build-up.

    Eimer et al. 2018 · NeuronAlzheimer's mouse model and 3D human neural cell culture infection experiments
  7. 7
    CD33an Alzheimer's risk gene that blocks microglia from clearing amyloid-β

    CD33 was raised in microglia of Alzheimer's brains, and the protective version of the gene lowered both CD33 and insoluble Aβ42. CD33 blocked uptake of Aβ42 by microglia, and Alzheimer's mice lacking CD33 had markedly less amyloid plaque, making CD33 a possible drug target.

    Griciuc et al. 2013 · NeuronPost-mortem human brain, microglial cell cultures and knockout mice
  8. 8
    Tau fights virusphosphorylated tau binds herpes viruses and blocks infection of human neurons

    Herpes simplex virus 1 infection made tau become hyperphosphorylated in human neurons, and that tau bound viral capsids and neutralized infection. The authors propose that plaques, tangles and inflammation together may be the brain's innate defense against infection.

    Eimer et al. 2026 · Nat NeurosciHuman neuron infection experiments
  9. 9
    Exercise mimicnew neurons plus BDNF reproduced exercise's memory benefit in Alzheimer's mice

    Exercise improved memory in Alzheimer's mice only when it both created new hippocampal neurons and raised brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF). Boosting both with genes or drugs reproduced the benefit without exercise, while blocking new neurons later worsened memory.

    Choi et al. 2018 · Science5xFAD Alzheimer's mouse model
  10. 10
    Infection seeds plaquesbacteria in the brain triggered fast amyloid deposition that trapped them

    Amyloid-β protected mice, worms and cells from fungal and bacterial infection. Salmonella infection of the brain in Alzheimer's mice rapidly seeded amyloid plaques that wrapped around the bacteria, supporting a dual protective and damaging role for amyloid-β.

    Kumar et al. 2016 · Sci Transl MedMouse, nematode and cell culture infection models
  11. 11
    Presenilina new gene on chromosome 14 with five mutations causing aggressive early-onset Alzheimer's

    Narrowing the chromosome 14 region linked to aggressive early-onset Alzheimer's led to a new membrane protein gene (later named presenilin 1). Five different mutations in conserved parts of the gene travelled with the disease in families and were absent from healthy controls.

    Sherrington et al. 1995 · NaturePositional cloning in families with early-onset inherited Alzheimer's disease
  12. 12
    Irisinthe exercise hormone needed for exercise's cognitive benefits in mice

    Mice lacking the exercise hormone irisin had impaired memory with exercise, aging and Alzheimer's disease. Raising irisin levels in the blood was enough to improve both memory and brain pathology in Alzheimer's mice.

    Islam et al. 2021 · Nat MetabKnockout and gene-delivery studies in mice
  13. 13
    TREM2 neededfor the benefits of removing CD33 in Alzheimer's mice

    Knocking out CD33 reduced amyloid and improved memory in Alzheimer's mice, but not when TREM2 was also removed. Losing TREM2 worsened amyloid and nerve damage, so TREM2 acts downstream of CD33 in controlling microglia.

    Griciuc et al. 2019 · NeuronDouble-knockout 5xFAD mice with microglial RNA sequencing
  14. 14
    CD8+ T cellsentering Alzheimer's cultures worsened inflammation and neuron loss

    A 3D human model with neurons, astrocytes, microglia and blood immune cells showed T cells and monocytes moving selectively into Alzheimer's cultures. Infiltrating CD8+ T cells increased microglial activation, inflammation and neurodegeneration through the CXCL10–CXCR3 signal.

    Jorfi et al. 2023 · Nat Neurosci3D human neuroimmune cell culture model with single-cell RNA sequencing
  15. 15
    13 lociof rare variants newly linked to Alzheimer's by whole-genome sequencing

    Whole-genome sequencing of 2,247 people from 605 families with Alzheimer's found 13 new candidate loci of rare variants that replicated in 1,669 unrelated people. Unlike common risk variants, which point to immunity and amyloid, these rare variants pointed to synapses and brain development.

    Prokopenko et al. 2021 · Alzheimers DementFamily-based whole-genome sequencing, 2,247 people, with replication in 1,669
  16. 16
    OR 3.56higher Alzheimer's risk with a deletion in the A2M gene

    A deletion in the alpha-2-macroglobulin (A2M) gene, which helps clear amyloid-β, raised Alzheimer's risk about 3.6-fold, comparable to APOE ε4 in the same families, but unlike APOE ε4 it did not change age of onset.

    Blacker et al. 1998 · Nat GenetFamily-based genetic association study
  17. 17
    Chromosome 10linked to late-onset Alzheimer's near the amyloid-clearing IDE gene

    Linkage analysis in 435 families with several affected members showed significant linkage of Alzheimer's to markers on chromosome 10q near the insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE) gene, which breaks down amyloid-β. The signal was strongest in late-onset families.

    Bertram et al. 2000 · ScienceLinkage study in 435 multiplex Alzheimer's families
  18. 18
    ADAM10rare late-onset mutations shift APP cutting toward amyloid-β

    Two rare mutations in ADAM10, the enzyme that cuts APP in a way that prevents amyloid-β, travelled with late-onset Alzheimer's in families. In mice they weakened this protective cut, increased plaques and reduced new neuron formation.

    Suh et al. 2013 · NeuronFamily genetics and transgenic mice
  19. 19
    Ataxin-1 lossraises BACE1 and amyloid in Alzheimer's-vulnerable brain regions

    Losing ataxin-1, the protein behind spinocerebellar ataxia type 1, released a brake on the BACE1 gene, raising BACE1 levels and amyloid-β production in brain regions vulnerable to Alzheimer's, and worsening plaques in Alzheimer's mice.

    Suh et al. 2019 · CellKnockout and Alzheimer's mouse models
  20. 20
    Huntington's genelocated on chromosome 4 using a DNA marker

    Family studies showed that the Huntington's disease gene is linked to a DNA marker on chromosome 4, the first step toward finding the gene that causes the disease.

    Gusella et al. 1983 · NatureGenetic linkage study in Huntington's disease families
  21. 21
    Wilson disease genea copper-transporting pump, 76% similar to the Menkes disease gene

    A gene in the Wilson disease region of chromosome 13 encodes a copper-transporting ATPase with 76% amino acid similarity to the Menkes disease gene. Four disease-specific mutations confirmed it as the Wilson disease gene.

    Tanzi et al. 1993 · Nat GenetGene cloning and mutation screening in patients
  22. 22
    Above 300 nMzinc rapidly turns human amyloid-β into amyloid

    Zinc above 300 nanomolar quickly made human amyloid-β form amyloid, while rat amyloid-β, which binds zinc less tightly, did not, which may explain why rats rarely form brain amyloid.

    Bush et al. 1994 · ScienceLaboratory biochemistry
  23. 23
    AUC 0.93for improved executive function on the metal-targeting drug PBT2

    In a further analysis of a 12-week phase II trial in Alzheimer's disease, patients on 250 mg of the copper and zinc ionophore PBT2 were much more likely than those on placebo to improve on executive function tests (AUC 0.93). Changes in spinal fluid amyloid or tau did not track cognitive changes.

    Faux et al. 2010 · J Alzheimers DisPost-hoc analysis of a randomized, placebo-controlled phase IIa trial
  24. 24
    Earlier works bettergene therapy lowering CD33 cut plaques more when given early

    A virus-delivered microRNA that lowered CD33 in Alzheimer's mice reduced amyloid-β and brain inflammation. Treating at 2 months of age cut plaque burden more than treating at 8 months.

    Griciuc et al. 2020 · Hum Mol GenetAAV gene therapy in APP/PS1 mice
  25. 25
    GGA3loss stabilizes BACE and raises amyloid-β after stroke and in Alzheimer's

    During reduced blood flow to the brain, levels of the trafficking protein GGA3 fell while BACE, the enzyme that starts amyloid-β production, rose. In Alzheimer's brains, lower GGA3 tracked with higher BACE.

    Tesco et al. 2007 · NeuronCell, ischemia model and post-mortem human brain studies
  26. 26
    Leaky barriera 3D human model recreates blood-brain barrier failure in Alzheimer's

    A microfluidic 3D model of human neural cells and brain blood vessel cells reproduced Alzheimer's-like blood-brain barrier damage, with higher permeability, loss of tight-junction proteins, more oxidative stress and amyloid-β deposits on the vessel wall.

    Shin et al. 2019 · Adv Sci (Weinh)3D microfluidic human cell culture model
  27. 27
    +4 pointson cognitive tests after 12 weeks of moderate exercise in older adults

    In 24 inactive middle-aged and older adults, 12 weeks of moderate aerobic exercise raised total cognitive score (111.1 to 115.2) and fitness, but did not change sleep or a sleep EEG–based measure of brain age.

    Ouyang et al. 2024 · Life (Basel)Single-arm pilot clinical trial, 24 participants
