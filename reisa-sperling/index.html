Characterization of preclinical Alzheimer's disease and secondary prevention trials in cognitively unimpaired older adults.

  • Preclinical Alzheimer's disease. Led the NIA-AA workgroup that defined preclinical stages of Alzheimer's disease for research (Alzheimer's & Dementia 2011) and showed altered default network function in amyloid-positive older adults without dementia (Neuron 2009).
  • Amyloid, tau and decline. In the Harvard Aging Brain Study, showed that amyloid and tau together are associated with faster cognitive decline in clinically normal adults (JAMA Neurology 2019), with sex differences in regional tau deposition (JAMA Neurology 2019).
  • Prevention trials. Led the A4 study, in which solanezumab did not slow cognitive decline in preclinical Alzheimer's disease (N Engl J Med 2023), and designed the AHEAD 3-45 lecanemab prevention trial (Alzheimer's & Dementia 2023).
  • Blood and digital markers. Estimated absolute risk of progression to cognitive impairment from plasma p-tau217 across six cohorts (JAMA 2026) and developed a remote multi-day learning paradigm for detecting early deficits (NPJ Digital Medicine 2025).

  1. 1
    Preclinical ADdefined as a research stage before symptoms

    An international NIA-AA workgroup set out a framework and research criteria for a "preclinical" stage of Alzheimer's disease, when brain changes are present years before mild cognitive impairment or dementia, to make earlier treatment trials possible.

    Sperling et al. 2011 · Alzheimers DementConsensus recommendations of the NIA-AA diagnostic workgroup
  2. 2
    -0.30PACC points difference with solanezumab, not significant

    In the A4 trial of 1,169 cognitively normal people aged 65 to 85 with elevated brain amyloid, solanezumab did not slow cognitive decline over 240 weeks compared with placebo (P = 0.26), although amyloid rose less on the drug (11.6 vs 19.3 centiloids).

    Sperling et al. 2023 · N Engl J MedPhase 3 randomized placebo-controlled trial, 1,169 participants
  3. 3
    38%5-year risk of impairment with very high blood p-tau217

    Across 2,684 cognitively unimpaired older adults in six cohorts, higher plasma p-tau217 predicted progression to cognitive impairment. People with high or very high levels had a 24% or 38% absolute risk of progressing within 5 years.

    Buckley et al. 2026 · JAMAPooled longitudinal cohort study, 6 cohorts, 2,684 participants, median 5.4 years follow-up
  4. 4
    29.5%of cognitively normal 65-85 year olds had elevated amyloid

    Of 4,486 cognitively normal older adults screened for the A4 trial, 29.5% had elevated brain amyloid. It was linked to family history of dementia and APOE4 (58% vs 25% carriers), but not to education, lifestyle or other reported risk factors.

    Sperling et al. 2020 · JAMA NeurolCross-sectional screening study, 4,486 participants, 67 sites
  5. 5
    Aβ × tauboth needed for fast memory decline

    In cognitively normal older adults, higher amyloid and higher tau on PET each predicted memory decline, but rapid decline occurred mainly in people with high levels of both.

    Sperling et al. 2019 · Ann NeurolLongitudinal PET cohort (Harvard Aging Brain Study), 137 participants
  6. 6
    1.51points worse on PACC at 4 years with elevated amyloid

    Cognitively normal people with elevated brain amyloid scored worse after 4 years than those with normal amyloid on the PACC (1.51 points), MMSE (0.56 points) and CDR-Sum of Boxes (0.23 points); the clinical meaning of these small differences was uncertain.

    Donohue et al. 2017 · JAMALongitudinal cohort (ADNI), 445 cognitively normal participants, median 3.1 years
  7. 7
    Default networkdisrupted in symptom-free people with amyloid

    Older adults without dementia who had high amyloid on PET showed abnormal fMRI activity in the default network, the memory-related brain network that fails in Alzheimer's dementia.

    Sperling et al. 2009 · NeuronAmyloid PET and functional MRI study of asymptomatic and minimally impaired older adults
  8. 8
    A3 + A45two lecanemab prevention trials under one protocol

    The AHEAD 3-45 study is testing lecanemab in cognitively unimpaired people aged 55 to 80 for 4 years, with doses tailored to their amyloid level - intermediate amyloid (about 20-40 centiloids) in A3 and elevated amyloid (above 40) in A45.

    Rafii et al. 2023 · Alzheimers DementTrial design paper, phase 2 and phase 3 prevention trials
  9. 9
    24.4%ARIA-E with donanemab vs 1.9% on placebo

    In the donanemab trials, brain swelling (ARIA-E) occurred in 24.4% of treated people vs 1.9% on placebo, mostly mild or moderate and usually by the third infusion. Risk rose with APOE4 copies, existing microbleeds, higher amyloid and higher blood pressure.

    Zimmer et al. 2025 · JAMA NeurolSecondary analysis of 2 randomized trials plus open-label addendum, 3,030 participants
  10. 10
    34.5%ARIA-E in APOE4 homozygotes on lecanemab

    In the Clarity AD lecanemab trial and its extension, ARIA-E occurred in 13.6% of treated people overall, 16.8% of APOE4 carriers and 34.5% of APOE4 homozygotes, mostly within 3 to 6 months of starting treatment.

    Honig et al. 2024 · Alzheimers Res TherSafety analysis of a phase 3 randomized trial and open-label extension, 1,795 participants
  11. 11
    ARIAfirst recommendations for detection and monitoring

    An Alzheimer's Association workgroup defined amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA-E for swelling and ARIA-H for microbleeds) seen with amyloid-lowering drugs, linked them to amyloid in blood vessel walls, and recommended how to monitor for them in trials.

    Sperling et al. 2011 · Alzheimers DementExpert workgroup review of trial data and literature
  12. 12
    Synergyamyloid plus vascular risk speeds decline

    In 223 cognitively normal older adults, higher cardiovascular risk scores and higher amyloid each predicted faster cognitive decline, and together they acted synergistically. Vascular risk still predicted decline after accounting for brain imaging markers.

    Rabin et al. 2018 · JAMA NeurolLongitudinal cohort (Harvard Aging Brain Study), 223 participants, mean 3.7 years
  13. 13
    Aβ + NDonly group that declined over time

    Among 166 cognitively normal adults, only those with both amyloid and neurodegeneration (small hippocampus or low brain metabolism) declined, and their decline was greater than the two factors added together.

    Mormino et al. 2014 · JAMA NeurolLongitudinal cohort, 166 cognitively normal participants
  14. 14
    PACCcognitive test composite built for prevention trials

    The Preclinical Alzheimer Cognitive Composite, combining memory, timed executive function and global cognition tests, detected amyloid-related decline in cognitively normal people within 18 to 36 months, making secondary prevention trials feasible.

    Donohue et al. 2014 · JAMA NeurolAnalysis of 3 observational cohorts (ADNI, AIBL, ADCS-PI)
  15. 15
    ρ = -0.55entorhinal tau vs later cognitive change

    In the A4 and LEARN studies, baseline tau PET in the entorhinal cortex and plasma p-tau217 best predicted later cognitive decline in amyloid-positive people, while tau PET change tracked concurrent cognitive change, supporting tau measures as trial outcomes.

    Insel et al. 2025 · JAMA NeurolLongitudinal trial and observational data, 1,707 participants (443 with tau PET)
  16. 16
    Very low%p-tau217 means minimal near-term risk

    In cognitively unimpaired older adults, higher plasma %p-tau217 predicted faster build-up of amyloid and tau on PET, even in people still amyloid-negative, while very low levels meant minimal risk of pathology build-up and decline.

    Yang et al. 2026 · Nat CommunProspective cohort with mass spectrometry plasma tests and serial PET
  17. 17
    15,612people screened with a blood test for AHEAD 3-45

    In 15,612 people screened for the AHEAD 3-45 trial, plasma %p-tau217 was lower in Asian, Black and Hispanic participants, but its links to cognition and amyloid PET were consistent across racial groups, supporting blood-based screening before PET.

    Molina-Henry et al. 2026 · JAMA NeurolCross-sectional trial screening analysis, 106 sites in 4 world regions
  18. 18
    Fastestdecline with high p-tau217 and poor digital learning

    Among 183 cognitively unimpaired adults, a one-week remote learning test added to plasma p-tau217 in predicting decline. People with high p-tau217 but preserved learning stayed stable, while those with both risk factors declined fastest.

    Kim et al. 2026 · Alzheimers DementLongitudinal cohort, 183 participants, 2.5 years
  19. 19
    Womenshow more early entorhinal tau than men

    In two cohorts of clinically normal adults, women had higher tau in the entorhinal cortex than men, especially with higher amyloid, pointing to a biological basis for sex differences in Alzheimer's risk.

    Buckley et al. 2019 · JAMA NeurolTwo cross-sectional PET cohorts (HABS, ADNI), 296 participants
  20. 20
    Fasterdecline in women with higher tau

    Across 398 older adults, women showed higher tau PET signal than men in many cortical regions, and women with higher tau in these regions declined cognitively faster than men.

    Buckley et al. 2020 · Ann NeurolLongitudinal PET cohorts (HABS, ADNI), 398 participants
  21. 21
    7.5×higher odds of loneliness with high amyloid

    Cognitively normal older adults with high amyloid were 7.5 times more likely to be lonely than those without, after adjusting for depression, anxiety and social network, suggesting loneliness may be an early symptom of Alzheimer's.

    Donovan et al. 2016 · JAMA PsychiatryCross-sectional study (Harvard Aging Brain Study), 79 participants
  22. 22
    Hubsdisconnected before dementia

    Brain hub regions such as the posterior cingulate and precuneus showed lost connectivity and lower metabolism in amyloid-positive people with mild cognitive impairment, and subtler changes already in amyloid-positive people without symptoms.

    Drzezga et al. 2011 · BrainAmyloid PET, FDG-PET and resting-state fMRI study
  23. 23
    Processing speedfirst to slow as amyloid emerges

    In clinically normal adults followed 7 or more years, rising amyloid first slowed processing speed, while declines in memory retrieval came with emerging amyloid and tau together.

    Farrell et al. 2022 · NeurologyLongitudinal PET cohort (Harvard Aging Brain Study), 112 participants
  24. 24
    13% vs 39%passed the blood screen, Hispanic Black vs non-Hispanic White

    In AHEAD screening of 4,905 people, fewer Black, Hispanic and Asian participants passed the plasma amyloid screen than non-Hispanic White participants, but among those who passed, PET eligibility was the same across groups.

    Molina-Henry et al. 2024 · Alzheimers DementTrial screening analysis, 4,905 participants
