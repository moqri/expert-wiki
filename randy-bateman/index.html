Stable isotope labeling kinetics, blood biomarkers and prevention trials in autosomal dominant Alzheimer's disease.

  • Amyloid-β kinetics. Developed stable isotope labeling kinetics to measure amyloid-β synthesis and clearance in human cerebrospinal fluid (Nature Medicine 2006) and showed that late-onset Alzheimer's disease is associated with decreased amyloid-β clearance (Science 2010).
  • Autosomal dominant Alzheimer's disease. In the Dominantly Inherited Alzheimer Network, described the sequence of biomarker, imaging and clinical changes preceding expected symptom onset by up to 25 years (N Engl J Med 2012).
  • Plasma biomarkers. Showed that plasma %p-tau217 detects brain amyloid with accuracy comparable to clinical cerebrospinal fluid tests (Nature Medicine 2024) and that plasma MTBR-tau243 reflects tau tangle pathology (Nature Medicine 2025).
  • Prevention trials. In the DIAN-TU platform trial, gantenerumab but not solanezumab reduced amyloid plaques (Nature Medicine 2021); an open-label extension evaluated long-term high-dose gantenerumab for delaying symptom onset (Lancet Neurology 2025).

  1. 1
    25 yearsbefore expected symptoms, spinal fluid amyloid-β starts to fall in inherited Alzheimer's

    In families with an Alzheimer's-causing gene, amyloid-β in spinal fluid began to decline about 25 years before expected symptom onset. Amyloid plaques on PET, rising tau and brain shrinkage appeared about 15 years before, memory problems about 10 years before, and dementia about 3 years after.

    Bateman et al. 2012 · N Engl J MedProspective DIAN cohort, 128 people from families with inherited Alzheimer's
  2. 2
    89–90%accuracy of a blood %p-tau217 test for brain amyloid in people with memory problems

    A mass spectrometry blood test for %p-tau217 classified amyloid PET status as well as FDA-approved spinal fluid tests (AUC 0.95–0.97 for both) and was generally better at detecting tau tangles. Using two cut-offs raised accuracy to 95%.

    Barthélemy et al. 2024 · Nat MedTwo cohorts, 1,422 people in Sweden and 337 in the US, with PET as the reference
  3. 3
    Slower clearancenot faster production, marks late-onset Alzheimer's

    Measuring amyloid-β turnover directly in the brain showed that people with common late-onset Alzheimer's clear both Aβ42 and Aβ40 more slowly than cognitively normal people, while production rates did not differ on average.

    Mawuenyega et al. 2010 · ScienceStable isotope labeling study in people with Alzheimer's and controls
  4. 4
    7.6% and 8.3% per hourof brain amyloid-β made and cleared, the first such measurement in living people

    A new method using a labeled amino acid and serial spinal fluid sampling measured, for the first time, how fast amyloid-β is produced (7.6% per hour) and cleared (8.3% per hour) in the human brain.

    Bateman et al. 2006 · Nat MedMethod development in healthy volunteers
  5. 5
    HR 0.53for clinical decline in people without symptoms who took gantenerumab longest

    In an open-label extension of the DIAN-TU trial, three years of high-dose gantenerumab strongly reduced amyloid plaques. Partial or short-term amyloid removal showed no clear clinical effect, but long-term full removal potentially delayed symptom onset (hazard ratio 0.53, 95% CI 0.27–1.03); this needs confirming in longer controlled trials. Amyloid-related imaging abnormalities occurred in 53% of participants.

    Bateman et al. 2025 · Lancet NeurolOpen-label extension of a phase 2/3 trial, 73 participants, external controls
  6. 6
    No cognitive benefitfrom gantenerumab or solanezumab in the first inherited Alzheimer's prevention trial

    In the DIAN-TU trial, neither antibody slowed cognitive decline after 4–7 years of treatment. Gantenerumab did clear amyloid plaques and lowered spinal fluid tau and p-tau181, while solanezumab showed no downstream biomarker benefit. Brain swelling (ARIA-E) occurred in 19.2% on gantenerumab.

    Salloway et al. 2021 · Nat MedRandomized placebo-controlled trial, 144 mutation carriers
  7. 7
    ±3–3.7 yearserror of a blood p-tau217 "clock" in predicting when symptoms begin

    Repeated blood %p-tau217 tests were used to estimate the age a person turned positive, which predicted the age Alzheimer's symptoms began with a median error of 3.0–3.7 years. The time from turning positive to symptoms was much shorter in older people.

    Petersen et al. 2026 · Nat MedTwo longitudinal cohorts, 258 and 345 people
  8. 8
    2 decadesbefore tau tangles, p-tau217 and p-tau181 start to rise in spinal fluid

    Different sites on the tau protein become phosphorylated at different stages of inherited Alzheimer's. p-tau217 and p-tau181 rise with early amyloid plaques, as much as two decades before tau tangles form, while p-tau205 and total tau rise later, near symptom onset.

    Barthélemy et al. 2020 · Nat MedSpinal fluid from the DIAN cohort across four decades of disease
  9. 9
    AUC 0.88for a blood amyloid-β 42/40 test against amyloid PET

    A high-precision blood test for amyloid-β 42/40 matched amyloid PET well (AUC 0.88), rising to 0.94 when combined with age and APOE4 status. People with a negative scan but a positive blood test had 15 times the risk of later turning PET-positive.

    Schindler et al. 2019 · Neurology158 mostly cognitively normal adults with plasma, spinal fluid and PET
  10. 10
    R² = 0.56link between a blood tau fragment and tau tangles on PET

    A newly described blood tau fragment, eMTBR-tau243, specifically reflects tau tangles. It rose at the mild cognitive impairment stage, rose further in dementia, and tracked tau PET and cognition better than %p-tau217 or %p-tau205.

    Horie et al. 2025 · Nat MedThree cohorts of 108, 55 and 739 people
  11. 11
    Tangle-specificspinal fluid MTBR-tau243 tracks tau tangles, not amyloid

    MTBR-tau243 in spinal fluid was most strongly tied to tau PET and cognition and least to amyloid PET, and it rose over time as tangles built up, unlike p-tau measures. The authors propose adding it to the A/T/N criteria as the marker for tangles.

    Horie et al. 2023 · Nat MedTwo cohorts, 448 and 219 people
  12. 12
    0.93accuracy of %p-tau217 versus 0.83–0.88 for immunoassays

    In a head-to-head comparison of leading p-tau217 blood tests, all detected abnormal amyloid PET well (AUC 0.91–0.96), but the mass spectrometry %p-tau217 test performed best, with 91% sensitivity and 94% specificity.

    Warmenhoven et al. 2025 · Brain998 people in the BioFINDER-2 cohort
  13. 13
    24% moreAβ42 relative to Aβ40 made by people with presenilin mutations

    Carriers of Alzheimer's-causing presenilin mutations produced 24% more Aβ42 relative to Aβ40 in the brain than non-carrier relatives, whether or not they had plaques. This supports overproduction of Aβ42 as the cause of this inherited form.

    Potter et al. 2013 · Sci Transl MedStable isotope labeling study in mutation carriers and non-carrier relatives
  14. 14
    2.5-foldlonger amyloid-β half-life over five decades of age

    Amyloid-β turnover in the brain slowed markedly with age, with a 2.5-fold longer half-life over five decades. This links aging, the biggest risk factor for Alzheimer's, to a buildup that makes protein misfolding more likely.

    Patterson et al. 2015 · Ann NeurolStable isotope labeling in 112 participants
  15. 15
    16 yearsbefore symptoms, rising blood neurofilament flags inherited Alzheimer's

    The rate of change of neurofilament light in blood, a marker of nerve damage, distinguished mutation carriers from non-carriers 16.2 years before expected symptoms, almost a decade earlier than a single reading. It predicted brain thinning and cognitive decline.

    Preische et al. 2019 · Nat MedDIAN cohort, 405 people with serum and 196 with repeat samples
  16. 16
    25–30%rise in overnight amyloid-β after a night without sleep

    One night of sleep deprivation raised overnight amyloid-β38, 40 and 42 in spinal fluid by 25–30%, through increased production, compared with normal sleep.

    Lucey et al. 2018 · Ann NeurolRandomized study with serial spinal fluid sampling and isotope labeling
  17. 17
    10–20% loweramyloid-β after a single dose of the sleep drug suvorexant

    A single 20 mg dose of suvorexant, an approved insomnia drug, lowered amyloid-β by about 10–20% and tau phosphorylation at one site (T181) by 10–15% in spinal fluid versus placebo. Long-term studies are needed to know whether it could help prevent Alzheimer's.

    Lucey et al. 2023 · Ann NeurolRandomized placebo-controlled trial, 38 cognitively unimpaired adults aged 45–65
  18. 18
    23 dayshalf-life of tau in the human brain

    Tau protein turns over slowly in the human brain, with a half-life of about 23 days. Tau production rose with the amount of amyloid plaque, a biological link between plaques and tau.

    Sato et al. 2018 · NeuronStable isotope labeling in people and stem-cell-derived neurons
  19. 19
    C index 0.91agreement of a two-marker blood test with PET-based Alzheimer's staging

    Combining blood %p-tau217 and eMTBR-tau243 staged Alzheimer's in close agreement with PET (C index 0.91), matched clinical severity, and aligned with autopsy findings (AUC 0.96), better than %p-tau217 alone.

    Salvadó et al. 2026 · JAMA NeurolLongitudinal study with validation and autopsy subsamples
  20. 20
    Up to 84%drop in brain amyloid-β production with a γ-secretase inhibitor

    Single doses of the γ-secretase inhibitor LY450139 cut amyloid-β production in the brain by 47%, 52% and 84% at 100, 140 and 280 mg, without changing clearance. This showed isotope labeling could test drug effects directly in people.

    Bateman et al. 2009 · Ann NeurolDose-ranging study in 20 healthy men
  21. 21
    5 to 6 years earlieramyloid builds up in inherited Alzheimer's than in Down syndrome, relative to symptoms

    Amyloid plaques rose about 23 years before expected symptoms in carriers of Alzheimer's genes versus about 17.5 years in Down syndrome, but overall patterns were similar. The authors suggest adults with Down syndrome aged 35 or older warrant inclusion in prevention trials.

    Boerwinkle et al. 2023 · Lancet NeurolCross-sectional study, 192 people with Down syndrome and 265 mutation carriers
  22. 22
    Tau stayed localin a gene carrier who stayed free of dementia past the expected age

    A PSEN2 mutation carrier who remained without symptoms well past the expected age had tau confined to the back of the brain without spread, a proteomic profile rich in heat shock proteins, and significant heat exposure, pointing to blocking tau spread as a treatment target.

    Llibre-Guerra et al. 2025 · Nat MedLongitudinal single-person case study
  23. 23
    Earlier onsetwith presenilin variants that weaken γ-secretase more

    PSEN1 variants that reduce γ-secretase activity more were linked with earlier symptom onset, faster amyloid buildup, faster hippocampal shrinkage and faster memory decline, partly explaining why inherited Alzheimer's varies between families.

    Schultz et al. 2024 · Lancet NeurolCross-sectional and longitudinal analysis of the DIAN observational study
  24. 24
    35–55% up in fluid, 5–15% down in bloodAlzheimer's markers after a sleepless night

    Sleep deprivation raised amyloid-β and tau in spinal fluid by about 35–55% but lowered them in blood by about 5–15%, by slowing clearance from brain to blood. Ratios such as Aβ42/40 stayed stable, so they are safer to interpret than single blood values.

    Liu et al. 2023 · Alzheimers DementCrossover study, 5 cognitively normal adults sampled every 2 hours for 36 hours
  25. 25
    1.5- to 4-foldswing in spinal fluid amyloid-β levels within a day

    Amyloid-β in spinal fluid varied 1.5- to 4-fold within individuals over 36 hours, tied to time of day or activity, so studies should control for when samples are taken.

    Bateman et al. 2007 · NeurologyHourly spinal fluid sampling in 15 non-demented adults
