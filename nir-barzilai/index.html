Ask about Nir Barzilai's research

Answers are drawn only from Nir Barzilai's published papers listed on this page, with each point linked to its paper. They describe what the papers report, not personal views or advice from Nir Barzilai. This is not medical advice.

Try

Genetics and physiology of exceptional human longevity and clinical trials targeting aging.

  • Compression of morbidity. Showed that people with exceptional longevity develop age-related diseases 18 to 24 years later than reference groups, despite lifestyle factors similar to the general population.
  • Longevity genes. Identified a lipoprotein phenotype and CETP genotype and functional IGF1R mutations enriched in Ashkenazi Jewish centenarians.
  • Rare variants. Reported that centenarians carry rare coding variants in conserved longevity pathways, including Wnt-pathway variants associated with longevity in APOE4 carriers (Nature Aging 2021).
  • Metabolism and targeting aging. Showed that removal of visceral fat prevents insulin resistance of aging in rats, and proposed metformin as a tool to target aging in the TAME trial.

  1. 1
    18-24 yearslater onset of major diseases in people who live to about 100

    People aged 95 and older developed cancer, heart disease, diabetes, high blood pressure and osteoporosis much later than younger comparison groups. The age at which 20% of them had a given disease was 18 to 24 years later, and the same pattern held in two separate centenarian studies.

    Ismail et al. 2016 · J Am Geriatr SocCase-control study of 1,937 people aged 95+ in two cohorts (Longevity Genes Project, New England Centenarian Study)
  2. 2
    42% lowerrisk of death for children of centenarians

    Across three independent cohorts in the US and UK, children of centenarians had a 42% lower risk of death, 33% lower risk of heart disease and 32% lower risk of high blood pressure than people whose parents lived shorter lives. Death came about 3 years later and high blood pressure about 5 years later.

    Reed et al. 2026 · JAMA Netw OpenMeta-analysis of three cohorts, 4,030 participants (LonGenity, New England Centenarian Study, UK Biobank)
  3. 3
    No differencein diet, exercise, smoking or drinking between centenarians and their peers

    People living independently at 95 and older had the same body weight, drinking, exercise and diet habits as people of the same birth years in the general population. Their long life did not come from a healthier lifestyle.

    Rajpathak et al. 2011 · J Am Geriatr SocComparison of 477 people aged 95-109 with 3,164 people from the same birth cohort (NHANES I)
  4. 4
    43% lowerrate of Alzheimer's disease in children of long-lived parents

    Older adults with at least one parent who lived to 85 or beyond developed Alzheimer's disease at a lower rate (hazard ratio 0.57) and had slower memory decline over up to 23 years of follow-up, even after accounting for heart disease, diabetes and stroke.

    Lipton et al. 2010 · J Am Geriatr SocCommunity cohort of 424 adults aged 75-85 followed up to 23 years (Bronx Aging Study)
  5. 5
    3xmore common CETP "VV" genotype in centenarian men

    Centenarians and their children had larger HDL and LDL cholesterol particles, linked to less high blood pressure, heart disease and metabolic syndrome. They were about three times (men) as likely to carry two copies of a CETP variant (I405V) that lowers CETP and enlarges these particles.

    Barzilai et al. 2003 · JAMACase-control study of 213 centenarians, 216 offspring and 847 controls
  6. 6
    2xas likely to have good memory at 99 with the CETP longevity genotype

    Among people about 99 years old, those with a good memory score were twice as likely to carry the CETP VV longevity genotype. In a younger cohort, the same genotype was about five times more common in people without dementia.

    Barzilai et al. 2006 · NeurologyTwo cohorts, 158 people with exceptional longevity and the Einstein Aging Study
  7. 7
    25% vs 10%carry the protective APOC3 genotype, centenarians vs controls

    A variant in the APOC3 gene that lowers blood APOC3 was carried in two copies by 25% of centenarians and 20% of their children, versus 10% of controls. Carriers had better cholesterol profiles, less high blood pressure, better insulin sensitivity and longer survival.

    Atzmon et al. 2006 · PLoS BiolGenetic association study of 66 variants in 36 genes in 687 people, plus a prospective survival cohort
  8. 8
    IGF1Rmutations that reduce growth signaling are enriched in female centenarians

    Female centenarians more often carried mutations in the IGF-1 receptor gene that reduce how strongly cells respond to IGF-1, a growth hormone signal. Their daughters were shorter and had higher IGF-1 levels, suggesting that slightly dampened growth signaling favors long life in humans as in animals.

    Suh et al. 2008 · Proc Natl Acad Sci U S AGene sequencing and cell studies in Ashkenazi centenarians, offspring and controls
  9. 9
    Lower IGF-1predicts longer survival in women in their 90s

    Among people in their 90s, women with IGF-1 levels below the median lived significantly longer than women above it. In men and women with a history of cancer, lower IGF-1 also predicted longer survival.

    Milman et al. 2014 · Aging CellSurvival study of 184 nonagenarians
  10. 10
    U-shapedlink between IGF-1 and disease, harmful when high in later life

    In 440,185 UK adults, younger people with high IGF-1 were protected from disease while older people with high IGF-1 had more disease and death. Both very high and very low levels carried risk, across dementia, heart disease, diabetes, osteoporosis and death.

    Zhang et al. 2021 · Aging CellProspective analysis of 440,185 UK Biobank participants
  11. 11
    +9%median lifespan in old female mice given an IGF-1 receptor antibody

    Blocking the IGF-1 receptor with an antibody starting at 18 months of age, roughly late middle age, improved health and raised median lifespan by 9% in female mice, with fewer tumors and less inflammation. Males did not benefit in the same way.

    Mao et al. 2018 · Nat CommunPreclinical study in aged male and female mice
  12. 12
    ~10 yearsadded lifespan linked to a growth hormone receptor variant in men

    Two copies of a common growth hormone receptor variant (exon 3 deletion) became more frequent with age among long-lived men in four cohorts. Carriers were about an inch taller with lower IGF-1, and the genotype was estimated to add about 10 years of life.

    Ben-Avraham et al. 2017 · Sci AdvFour cohorts of long-lived people, 841 participants
  13. 13
    11-22%fewer gene-breaking mutations in centenarians

    Centenarians carried 11 to 22% fewer rare loss-of-function mutations than controls, and their children showed the same trend. The analysis pointed to new longevity genes, including RGP1, PCNX2 and ANO9.

    Ying et al. 2024 · Nat CommunWhole-exome sequencing in two Ashkenazi cohorts, validated in UK Biobank
  14. 14
    Samenumber of known disease mutations in centenarians as in others

    Centenarians, their children and controls carried a similar number of known disease-causing variants. Variants linked to cancer and Alzheimer's disease were, however, less common in centenarians.

    Gutman et al. 2020 · Aging CellWhole-exome sequencing of 1,879 Ashkenazi participants
  15. 15
    Insulin/IGF-1 and AMPKpathways where centenarians' rare protective variants cluster

    Rare protein-changing variants enriched in centenarians mostly fell in the insulin/IGF-1 and AMPK signaling pathways, the same pathways that control lifespan in laboratory animals. Rare Wnt pathway variants appeared to protect people carrying the Alzheimer's risk gene APOE4.

    Lin et al. 2021 · Nat AgingWhole-exome sequencing of Ashkenazi centenarians and controls
  16. 16
    Strongergenetic protection against Alzheimer's at older ages

    Centenarians carried more genetic variants that protect against Alzheimer's disease (not counting APOE) than people without family longevity, and protection rose further in those who reached 105 and 110. Higher scores were tied to better thinking and lower mortality.

    Bae et al. 2026 · GerosciencePolygenic protective score across five studies in the USA, Europe and Asia
  17. 17
    P3Shumanin variant enriched in centenarians who carry APOE4

    A rare variant of humanin, a small protein made by mitochondria, was enriched in centenarians carrying APOE4. In APOE4 mice it reduced amyloid buildup in the brain more than normal humanin, apparently by helping clear amyloid.

    Miller et al. 2024 · Aging CellGenetic analysis in centenarians plus mouse and cell experiments
  18. 18
    Higher humaninin children of centenarians

    Humanin extended lifespan in worms and improved metabolic health in middle-aged mice. In people, children of centenarians had much higher blood humanin than matched controls, while levels were lower in Alzheimer's disease.

    Yen et al. 2020 · Aging (Albany NY)Worm, mouse, naked mole-rat and human studies
  19. 19
    Longer lifein rats after removing belly fat

    Rats whose visceral (belly) fat was surgically removed at 5 months lived longer on average and at maximum, with less severe kidney disease, than normally fed rats. Calorie-restricted rats lived longest of all.

    Muzumdar et al. 2008 · Aging CellProspective lifespan study in rats
  20. 20
    ~18% of fatremoved restored young-level insulin action in old rats

    Removing visceral fat, about 18% of total body fat, restored insulin action in old rats to young levels and delayed diabetes in diabetes-prone rats, with lower TNF-alpha and leptin in remaining fat.

    Gabriely et al. 2002 · DiabetesSurgical study in aging and diabetic (ZDF) rats
  21. 21
    TAMEproposed trial of metformin to delay diseases of aging

    Metformin has protective effects against several age-related diseases in humans. The TAME (Targeting Aging with Metformin) trial was proposed to test it as a first drug aimed at aging itself, opening a path to more effective next-generation drugs.

    Barzilai et al. 2016 · Cell MetabPerspective and trial rationale
  22. 22
    647 geneschanged in muscle after 6 weeks of metformin in older adults

    In a placebo-controlled crossover trial in 14 people about 70 years old, 6 weeks of metformin lowered blood sugar and insulin after meals and changed 647 genes in muscle and 146 in fat, including pathways beyond metabolism such as DNA repair.

    Kulkarni et al. 2018 · Aging CellRandomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial, 14 participants
  23. 23
    Bluntedmuscle gains when metformin is added to strength training

    In older adults doing 14 weeks of resistance training, metformin blunted the gain in muscle size compared with placebo, though it shifted muscle genes toward aging-related pathways such as autophagy and cellular senescence.

    Kulkarni et al. 2020 · Aging (Albany NY)Muscle gene analysis in 47 participants of a randomized trial
  24. 24
    2.01 vs 2.53children per person, centenarians vs comparison group

    Centenarians had fewer children and were older at their first and last childbirth than a comparison group, in both men and women, consistent with a trade-off between reproduction and lifespan.

    Tabatabaie et al. 2011 · Aging (Albany NY)Cohort comparison of Ashkenazi centenarians and controls
  25. 25
    94%of men aged 90-106 had a working brain-pituitary hormone axis

    In 84 men aged 90 to 106, 94% had normal hypothalamic-pituitary function, mostly an appropriate rise in LH in response to failing testes. Testosterone level was not linked to metabolism or survival.

    Aleksic et al. 2022 · Aging CellHormone study of 84 men with exceptional longevity
