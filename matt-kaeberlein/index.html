Aging

Matt Kaeberlein, PhD

Optispan; formerly University of WashingtonCo-founder, Dog Aging Project

Studies the biology that sets how fast we age, from genes in yeast to the drug rapamycin in mice, pet dogs and people.

The early work used budding yeast to find the genes that control lifespan. Kaeberlein and colleagues showed that the protein Sir2 extends yeast lifespan and works as an NAD-dependent enzyme that strips acetyl groups from histones. Later papers found that calorie restriction lengthens yeast life mostly through a separate route, that resveratrol's apparent boost of Sir2 was an artifact of the test chemistry, and that extra Sir2 did not lengthen life in worms or flies once genetic background was controlled.

A screen of 564 and later 4,698 yeast gene deletions pointed to the nutrient-sensing TOR pathway as a main link between food intake and aging, a pathway shared with worms and mammals. This led to work with rapamycin, a drug that blocks mTOR. In mice, 3 months of rapamycin in middle age raised life expectancy by up to 60%, regrew bone lost to gum disease in old mice, and greatly improved survival in a mouse model of the childhood mitochondrial disease Leigh syndrome.

Kaeberlein co-founded the Dog Aging Project, a study of tens of thousands of pet dogs that share our homes and our risks. Its papers report that rapamycin was safe in a 10-week trial in 24 middle-aged dogs and may have improved heart function, that dogs fed once a day had fewer health problems, and that large dogs and male dogs show faster epigenetic aging, in line with their shorter lives.

In people, the work collected the experience of 333 adults taking rapamycin off-label and found it can be used safely in healthy adults, while a 2026 trial in 40 older adults found weekly rapamycin did not help, and may have slightly blunted, gains from exercise. The papers argue that slowing aging itself is the best form of preventive medicine.

  1. 1
    Up to 60%longer life expectancy in middle-aged mice after only 3 months of rapamycin

    Three months of rapamycin given to middle-aged mice raised life expectancy by up to 60% and improved measures of health, with lasting effects after the drug was stopped. One dose in female mice did not extend life and shifted cancers toward aggressive blood cancers.

    Bitto et al. 2016 · ElifeMouse lifespan and healthspan study of short-term rapamycin started in middle age
  2. 2
    6 of 10lifespan-extending yeast genes were in the TOR and Sch9 nutrient pathways

    A screen of 564 yeast gene deletions found 10 that lengthen lifespan, and 6 of them are in the nutrient-sensing TOR and Sch9 pathways. Calorie restriction gave no extra benefit to these mutants, pointing to TOR as a main route by which excess nutrients shorten life.

    Kaeberlein et al. 2005 · ScienceReplicative lifespan screen of 564 single-gene deletion yeast strains
  3. 3
    Tens of thousandsof pet dogs followed for life in the Dog Aging Project

    The Dog Aging Project follows tens of thousands of companion dogs over time, collecting surveys, environment data, veterinary records, genomes and blood and stool samples. Dogs share our homes and health care but live much shorter lives, so they can show which genes, environments and habits go with healthy aging.

    Creevy et al. 2022 · NatureDesign of a long-term, open-data community science cohort of companion dogs
  4. 4
    No side effectsand better heart measures in dogs given low-dose rapamycin for 10 weeks

    In 24 healthy middle-aged dogs, a low, non-immunosuppressive dose of rapamycin for 10 weeks caused no clinical side effects compared with placebo. Heart ultrasound suggested improved pumping and filling (ejection fraction, fractional shortening, E/A ratio) in the rapamycin group.

    Urfer et al. 2017 · GeroscienceRandomized placebo-controlled trial in 24 companion dogs
  5. 5
    333 adultstaking rapamycin off-label to stay healthy were surveyed

    Survey data from 333 adults using rapamycin off-label for healthy aging, compared with 172 adults who never used it, gave initial evidence that rapamycin can be used safely in adults of normal health.

    Kaeberlein et al. 2023 · GeroscienceSurvey of 333 off-label rapamycin users and 172 non-users
  6. 6
    No added benefitfrom weekly rapamycin on exercise gains in older adults

    In 40 sedentary adults aged 65 to 85 doing home exercise for 13 weeks, weekly rapamycin (6 mg) did not improve chair-stand gains over placebo and in some analyses reduced them by 2 to 3 repetitions. People on rapamycin had more minor side effects (99 vs. 63 events) and one possibly drug-related pneumonia.

    Stanfield et al. 2026 · J Cachexia Sarcopenia MuscleRandomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial, 40 older adults (RAPA-EX-01)
  7. 7
    Survivalgreatly improved in mice with Leigh syndrome given rapamycin

    In mice lacking the mitochondrial gene Ndufs4, a model of the fatal childhood disease Leigh syndrome, rapamycin greatly improved survival, delayed neurological symptoms, reduced brain inflammation and prevented brain lesions.

    Johnson et al. 2013 · ScienceMouse model of Leigh syndrome (Ndufs4 knockout) treated with rapamycin
  8. 8
    Regrown boneand a younger mouth microbiome in old mice after brief rapamycin

    Short-term rapamycin in elderly mice regenerated bone lost to periodontal (gum) disease, reduced gum and bone inflammation, and shifted the oral microbiome toward a more youthful mix.

    An et al. 2020 · ElifeShort-term rapamycin treatment in aged mice
  9. 9
    More Sir2means longer life for yeast mother cells

    Sir2 is a limiting factor for yeast lifespan, and adding an extra copy of the SIR2 gene extends lifespan in normal cells. Without Sir2, cells fail to suppress harmful recombination in their ribosomal DNA and die young.

    Kaeberlein et al. 1999 · Genes DevYeast genetics of replicative lifespan
  10. 10
    NAD-dependentenzyme activity explains how Sir2 works

    Yeast and mouse Sir2 proteins remove acetyl groups from specific histone sites using NAD, and this activity accounts for Sir2's effects on gene silencing and lifespan. This connected metabolism, gene silencing and aging.

    Imai et al. 2000 · NatureBiochemistry and yeast genetics of Sir2 mutants
  11. 11
    Sir2-independentroute carries most of the lifespan benefit of calorie restriction in yeast

    Most of the lifespan gain from calorie restriction in yeast does not need Sir2. Combining calorie restriction with Sir2 overexpression or FOB1 deletion produced the longest-lived yeast strain reported at the time, showing the two act in parallel.

    Kaeberlein et al. 2004 · PLoS BiolYeast replicative lifespan with combined genetic and dietary interventions
  12. 12
    No effectof resveratrol on Sir2 activity in living yeast

    Resveratrol boosted Sir2 and human SIRT1 in test-tube assays only when the substrate carried an artificial fluorescent tag. In three yeast strains it had no detectable effect on Sir2 activity or on lifespan.

    Kaeberlein et al. 2005 · J Biol ChemEnzyme assays plus lifespan and silencing tests in three yeast strains
  13. 13
    No longer lifefrom extra Sir2 in worms and flies once genetics were controlled

    When genetic background and proper controls were standardized, earlier reports that extra Sir2 lengthens life in worms and flies did not hold up. Dietary restriction also extended fly life without needing Sir2.

    Burnett et al. 2011 · NatureRe-examination of sirtuin overexpression lines in C. elegans and Drosophila
  14. 14
    4,698yeast gene deletions tested for effects on lifespan

    Testing lifespan in 4,698 yeast gene-deletion strains showed that many longevity pathways are shared between yeast and worms. Deleting the tRNA exporter LOS1 robustly extended life, and this works through the same route as dietary restriction and mTOR inhibition.

    McCormick et al. 2015 · Cell MetabGenome-wide replicative lifespan screen of 4,698 deletion strains
  15. 15
    Once a dayfeeding linked to better health in pet dogs

    Among more than 24,000 dogs, those fed once a day had lower cognitive dysfunction scores and lower odds of digestive, dental, orthopedic, kidney/urinary and liver/pancreas disorders than dogs fed more often, after accounting for age, sex and breed.

    Bray et al. 2022 · GeroscienceCross-sectional Dog Aging Project data, 24,238 dogs (10,474 for cognition)
  16. 16
    3.5-4.6 yearsshorter median life for "Mountain" breed dogs

    In 20,970 dogs from three US primary care clinics, bigger dogs lived shorter lives and spayed or neutered dogs lived longer, especially females. Mountain-group breeds lived 3.5 to 4.6 years less than other purebreds even after accounting for size, and more genetically diverse breeds lived 3 to 6 months longer.

    Urfer et al. 2020 · Canine Med GenetSurvival analysis of 20,970 dogs from three US veterinary clinics
  17. 17
    Fasterepigenetic aging in large and male dogs, which live shorter lives

    An epigenetic clock built from 1,640 blood methylation profiles of 894 dogs predicted death and showed aging is fastest early in life. Larger and male dogs aged faster at the molecular level, with size effects concentrated in transposable elements.

    Mariner et al. 2026 · ScienceDNA methylation study, 1,640 methylomes from 894 dogs
  18. 18
    Amyloidin dog brains rises with age and tracks dementia scores

    In pet dog brains, amyloid beta 42 levels rose with age in three brain regions and were linked to canine cognitive dysfunction scores, supporting companion dogs as a natural model of Alzheimer's-like disease.

    Urfer et al. 2021 · GeroscienceBrain tissue and spinal fluid measurements from a companion dog biobank
  19. 19
    Taurinedeclines with age, and adding it back extended healthy life in mice

    Blood taurine falls with age in mice, monkeys and humans. Taurine supplements extended healthspan and lifespan in mice and healthspan in monkeys, and in people lower taurine went with several age-related diseases.

    Singh et al. 2023 · ScienceSupplementation studies in worms, mice and monkeys plus human cohort data
  20. 20
    Rapamycin + acarbosebeat either drug alone in male mice

    In the NIA Interventions Testing Program, male mice given rapamycin plus acarbose from 9 months lived longer than earlier groups given rapamycin alone. Captopril gave female mice a small 4 to 5% lifespan gain, while four other compounds had no benefit.

    Strong et al. 2022 · Aging CellMulti-site lifespan study in genetically diverse mice (ITP C2017 cohort)
  21. 21
    3 specieslived longer on ibuprofen

    Ibuprofen extended lifespan in yeast, worms and flies. In yeast it works by reducing tryptophan uptake through the Tat2 transporter.

    He et al. 2014 · PLoS GenetLifespan studies in yeast, C. elegans and Drosophila
  22. 22
    Starvationlengthens worm life more than partial food restriction

    Removing all bacterial food from adult worms extended lifespan more than partial food reduction, through a route independent of insulin signaling and sirtuins. It worked even when started after reproduction ended.

    Kaeberlein et al. 2006 · Aging CellC. elegans lifespan assays without bacterial food
  23. 23
    Fewer ribosomesof one type slow aging in yeast

    Reducing the large (60S) ribosomal subunit, by gene deletion or a drug, extended yeast lifespan by activating the stress-response factor Gcn4, apparently the same route used by dietary restriction.

    Steffen et al. 2008 · CellYeast replicative lifespan and genetic epistasis analysis
  24. 24
    Longer survivalin lamin-deficient mice given rapamycin

    Mice lacking A-type lamins, which model muscular dystrophy and heart muscle disease, had overactive mTORC1 in heart and muscle. Rapamycin improved heart and muscle function and extended their survival.

    Ramos et al. 2012 · Sci Transl MedLmna knockout mouse model treated with rapamycin
  25. 25
    Light-poweredboost to mitochondria extended worm lifespan

    Raising mitochondrial membrane potential in adult worms with a light-activated proton pump improved age-related traits and extended lifespan, direct evidence that its decline with age helps drive aging.

    Berry et al. 2023 · Nat AgingOptogenetic experiments in C. elegans
  26. 26
    11 of 40apparently healthy middle-aged dogs had hidden heart valve leaks

    Heart ultrasound of 40 dogs aged 6 or older with no history of heart disease found valve leakage in 11, but a stethoscope exam picked up only one. Silent valve disease may be common in middle-aged dogs, as in people.

    Urfer et al. 2017 · GeroscienceEchocardiography screening of 40 companion dogs
